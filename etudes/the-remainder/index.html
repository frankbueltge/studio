THE REMAINDER

Two movements. I: sixteen humanitarian appeals for Sudan, 2011 to 2026. II: every one of the 46 appeals the record lists for 2025, the year of the cuts. Each row is what was asked for. Every lit grain is one grant that came, in the order it was dated, as wide as its dollars. What is left of the row is what was asked for and never came. Press play: the grants sound as the line passes them, and the remainder is heard as silence, one second for every hundred million dollars.

hover or tap a grain, or the dark part of a row

What the dark is

The Financial Tracking Service records every reported grant to an appeal. It records nothing for the part of an appeal that no one paid: no line, no donor, no date. The requirement is the only trace that the need existed. Across the sixteen appeals … The silence in the playback is that remainder, at the same scale as the sound of what came.

2026 is an open appeal: on the day of the fetch its year had two months left, so part of its dark is still only "not yet".

The second movement widens the same row to every appeal of 2025: … Played at the default pace it lasts about eight minutes, and most of them are silent. Two appeals were met or more than met (the Philippines' cyclone plan at 145 %, Cuba's hurricane plan at 99 %); their rows run past their outline, and their silence is nil.

Source and method

OCHA Financial Tracking Service, public API (api.hpc.tools/v1/public), fetched 2026-10-26: the country's plan list (plan/country/SDN), and for each of the sixteen Sudan response plans every flow (fts/flow?planId=). A grant counts when FTS marks it incoming to the plan and paid or committed; pledges do not count. For every plan the counted flows add up exactly to FTS's own incoming funding total; the build script asserts it. Requirements are the plans' revised requirements as FTS states them. Nine flows of zero or negative value (corrections), skipped and counted in data.json. Grains in a paler grey are commitments not yet marked paid. Donor is the first organisation FTS names as source. Data: CC BY-IGO, OCHA FTS. Script: build_data.py; data: data.json.

Movement II: the plan list for the year (plan/year/2025), fetched 2026-10-27, and every flow of each of its 46 plans, by the same rule; for all 46 the counted flows equal FTS's own total (asserted in build_2025.py; data: data-2025.json). It includes regional refugee plans and flash appeals, sorted by what they asked. No flow is counted in two plans. A 2025 appeal can still receive funding after its year, so its dark can still shrink a little.

In movement I, regional refugee plans and the COVID plans of 2020 are not Sudan's appeals and are left out. Funding outside any plan is left out too: this is the record of what was asked, not of everything that was given.

Neighbours in the Atlas of Data Art

Of All the People in All the World: Stats with Rice (Stan's Cafe, 2004) gives a statistic its physical quantity, one grain a person. The Library of Missing Datasets (Mimi Ọnụọha, 2016–) shows as empty folders the datasets nobody collects. The REDress Project (Jaime Black, 2010–) makes an uncounted gap present as empty dresses. Daylight: here the absence is not an uncollected dataset but a sum that was counted, asked for and refused. It is measured to the dollar, and it is given a duration, a silence exactly as long as the sound of everything that came. Grains here are grants, not people.
