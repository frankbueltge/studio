# delivery/

*Opened session 51, 2026-07-31, answering the architect's world-contact seed of the same date
(`REQUESTS.md`). A new kind of artefact needed a home: not a project, not a work, not memory —
a **packet prepared for one named receiver outside this ecology**, and the record of what came
back.*

One directory per delivery, named `<year>-<month>-<work-slug>`. Each holds the files that
travel, the files that do not, and — when there is one — the returned record.

## The counting rule (adopted BEFORE the first packet was written)

The Kritiker's first condition tonight, and it binds every future month:

> **The send counts for nothing.** A named receiver, a channel and a date are an address, not an
> encounter.

What counts as a delivery, and may be entered in any review:

1. a **mounting record** returned under the work's own item 20 (or the equivalent for a future work);
2. a **correction to our record** — the receiver tells us we have something wrong, and we are wrong;
3. a **documented refusal**, with the reason the receiver gave;
4. a **measurement** we did not have and cannot take ourselves (for *NO PART*: the length of a real
   wall).

What does **not** count, whatever a monthly review would prefer to see: an appreciative reply with
no number, no correction and no refusal in it. That is recorded as a **non-delivery**. So is
silence — published as silence, never omitted.

*(Item 4 is the conductor's one widening of the Kritiker's rule, and it is named rather than
smuggled: a wall length returned by a stranger is a fact about the world this house cannot obtain
and has never had. It is not manners. Items 1–3 are the Kritiker's own, unchanged.)*

## Standing conditions on any packet

Merged tonight from the Dramaturg's fourteen staging conditions and the Kritiker's eight, with the
two conflicts ruled by the conductor (see `journal/2026-07-31-session-51.md`):

1. **One receiver, named, per send.** No list, no cc, no broadcast. A packet sent to a category is
   not a delivery.
2. **The object before the sender.** The first screen names the thing and a physical fact about it.
   The collective's identity is one line at the foot.
3. **The house's record does not travel.** No staging notes, no correction narratives, no reader
   studies, no gate, no session numbers, no journal links, no links to our own site.
4. **No image of the work travels.** A picture lets a receiver feel they have already had the
   experience, and a satisfied curiosity does not measure a wall.
5. **No claim about what a body finds in a room** — until a body has been in one.
6. **Every number on a packet is re-verified against the source the day it is written, or dropped.**
7. **Refusal is one word, and the packet says so**, with a commitment not to write again unasked.
8. **The studio writes; Frank sends.** He is the publisher and carries the press-law
   responsibility. Receiver, channel and date go in the record either way.
9. **What comes back is the receiver's.** Published as theirs, unedited, or not at all.

## The price of the second delivery, named tonight

The Kritiker permitted *NO PART* as the first delivery on the ground that its costlessness is
permitted **once** — a work that already needs a stranger's hand is not evidence that this house
can be used — and required the next month's price to be named before this month's packet was
written. It is named:

**September's candidate is *Recovery*, and it cannot be delivered as it stands.** It is a screen
apparatus on our site; sending its URL to a stranger is postage, and the only possible answer is an
opinion. To make it deliverable to the receiver it actually implies — a welfare-rights or benefits
advice service — it would have to become something that runs in their waiting room: an offline,
installable build, and a decision this studio has never had to make, because the work puts the
person operating it in the dock. Running that in a room where people are actually waiting on a
benefits decision is a materially different act from running it on a website, and the honest
possibility is that the answer is **no, not to that receiver** — in which case the price of the
second delivery is that we say so publicly and deliver something else, or nothing, and record the
month as a non-delivery. Either way the price is a build plus a gate the house has not yet run,
and it is not costless.
