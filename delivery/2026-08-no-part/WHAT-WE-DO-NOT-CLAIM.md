# What we are not claiming

**If you intend to mount the work: measure your wall first, then read this.** Item 13 of the
instruction asks you to choose where the line breaks from the room and not from the sheets. This
page tells you nothing about where to cut, but it does put numbers in your head, and the instruction
is easier to keep if they arrive afterwards.

This page is a list of refusals, not findings. It exists because we are sending this to people who
work on these cases, and the readings we most want to head off are the ones our own measurements
already contradict.

## 1. We do not claim this document shows that being poor decides the outcome

Of the **820** entries printed under `CERTIORARI DENIED` on 6 October 2025, **573 (69.9 %)** carry a
5000-series docket number — the Court's own numbering for petitions filed without prepayment of the
fee. That proportion is **69.8 %** among the 789 entries printed before the single sentence
*The petitions for writs of certiorari are denied.*, and **71.0 %** among the 31 entries printed
after it, which are the only ones the Court answered one at a time.

Those two figures are 1.2 points apart. Across the one place in this document where the register
changes, fee status barely moves.

Nine readers have been shown these sheets in our own reading tests, and **none of them connected fee
status to the outcome**, under a rule we fixed before reading their answers: name the docket-number
convention, or say the document marks who could not pay. The phrase *in forma pauperis* does appear
in several of their answers — the document prints it — and the one reader who treated it as naming a
population named the wrong one, glossing it as "unrepresented" rather than unable to pay the fee.
That convention is public but untaught, and a reader who does not already know it cannot see wealth
in this document at all.

We do not have a finding about wealth here, and no reading of this work that requires one is a
reading we support.

## 2. We do not claim to know what the length does to anyone

No one has ever stood in front of this. It has never been printed or mounted. Item 19 of the
instruction is the boundary: below sixteen sheets in the longest run, the mounting is legitimate and
our claims stop. Above it, we still have no evidence, because there has been no wall.

## 3. We make no claim about any person named in the document

The instruction adds nothing and asserts nothing about anyone. Every name that appears on the wall
appears there because the Court printed it, in the position the Court printed it in.

## 4. What is measured, and what is judgement

**Measured first-hand on the Court's file** (SHA-256 in item 1 of the instruction): thirty-nine
pages; 215.9 × 279.4 mm each; every text operation set at 10.02 pt; the counts and percentages
above, derived from the file's own text-positioning operations; 39 × 215.9 mm = 8,420.1 mm.

**Instruction and judgement, asserting nothing about the world:** everything in the twenty items —
the height, the joint, the prohibitions, the approach, what the realiser is asked to decide.

**Not present anywhere:** anything imagined, dramatised or reconstructed.
