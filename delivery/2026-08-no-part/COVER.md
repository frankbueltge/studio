**Subject:** The 6 October 2025 order list, printed at full size — 39 sheets, 8.42 metres

The order list the Supreme Court of the United States issued on Monday, 6 October 2025 is
thirty-nine pages. Printed at 100 % on ordinary paper and laid end to end in reading order, one
sheet tall, it is **8,420.1 mm** long.

Your longest uninterrupted wall, in metres, divided by 0.2159, is the number of sheets in your
first run. The rest continue round the corner.

Attached is an instruction for mounting it: twenty items, the whole of our authorship. Nothing is
added anywhere — no label, no title, no caption, no credit, no arrow, no light on one part of the
line rather than all of it. Every mark on the wall is the Court's. We do not re-host the file; the
instruction gives the Court's own URL and the checksum of the exact document it covers.

**Three things you could send back, in ascending cost. Any one of them is the whole of what we
want.**

1. **What we have wrong.** The second attachment is a page of things we are *not* claiming, and the
   arithmetic behind them. You read these documents professionally and we do not. If any of it is
   wrong, that is the sentence we most want. Five minutes.
2. **One number:** the length of your longest uninterrupted wall, at 1600 mm above the finished
   floor. Nobody has ever answered that question for this document, ourselves included.
3. **The wall itself.** Print the thirty-nine, mount them, and fill in the one-page record — the
   third attachment. An afternoon. If your longest run comes out under sixteen sheets, mount it
   anyway: the work stands, and what stops is *our* claim about it, not your mounting.

If you are going to mount it, **measure the wall before you read the second attachment.** Where the
line breaks is your decision, and item 13 asks you to take it from the room rather than from the
sheets — which only works if you have not read them first.

The record you return is yours. We publish it under your name, unedited, or not at all.

If the answer is no, one word is enough and we will not write again. You will still have 8,420.1 mm
and 215.9 mm to a sheet, and every corridor in your building is now measurable against one Monday's
docket.
