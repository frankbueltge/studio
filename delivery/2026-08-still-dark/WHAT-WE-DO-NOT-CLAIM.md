# What this work does not claim

Eight refusals. Each is here because the claim next to it is one a reader could reasonably think
we were making, and we are not. Where a refusal rests on arithmetic, the arithmetic is given —
not as a finding about the sea, but as the reason we are declining to make one.

## 1. Nothing here is an accusation

No claim of illegality is made against any vessel, operator, flag state or coastal state. The
*intentional* classification the underlying data carries is a model output — the register's own
method sheet states it is *"a probability, not proof"* and that the label comes from a
machine-learning model; Global Fishing Watch's own announcement of the global study calls these
**suspected** disabling events. That restraint is inherited and repeated here rather than
quietly dropped one link down the chain. Vessel names appear as the register printed them. No
person is named.

## 2. We do not claim this is Global Fishing Watch's latency

We measured one daily edition — `https://frankbueltge.de/ghost-fleet/`, published inside our own
research ecology, built from the GFW Events API under its own seven-day window. We did not
measure the Events API. Whether the delay we found is inherent to complete vanish-and-return
evidence or is an artefact of one page's cadence is precisely the question the cover asks, and
we do not answer it ourselves.

## 3. We do not claim to know which way the share moves if the lists were longer

Doubling a list's length does not halve our share. The numerator is the count of names in the
list dated 4 August — one of the lists being lengthened — and longer later lists add to the
denominator. Both ends of the quotient grow. We ran that check rather than asserting either
direction, and it is why no such sentence appears on the work.

## 4. The band is a band because the input is a band

The register publishes an event's end only as *within the last seven days*. Every interval
derived from it is therefore a seven-day band, never a point, and every share is a range. The
**upper** end assumes nothing: whatever became of the eleven names the day itself printed, no
case this record allows puts the share above 11 of 29. The **lower** end does assume all eleven
were in fact dark on that day; not one of them is certain, so unconditionally the floor is 0.
Both ends are printed. Neither is a best estimate.

## 5. Eight days is a chosen constant, and the choice flatters us

The four-day comparison stops each day at the same age — eight days. That is the only maturity
in this corpus at which the sequence descends cleanly. At seven days a fifth day turns it back
up (33, 12, 7, 3, **9**); at four days it is a U. We publish the constant, the alternatives, and
the fact that the tidiest number was the one we chose.

## 6. The four-day sequence is a reading of our archive first

Its numerators are exactly the counts of names our record met for the first time on each of
those days, and 4 August is the only day whose record begins on itself. That makes the first
row structurally the most favourable day in its own archive. The work states this against
itself on its own face rather than in a footnote.

## 7. The corpus is closed, and later evidence does not move the figure

Frozen at 2026-08-15T04:36:57Z: thirty-two saved copies, twelve distinct lists, 4–15 August
2026, every count derived at that instant. Lists arriving afterwards are published as dated
addenda beside the work. A figure that moves each night cannot be checked by anyone, and a
measurement that never closes is a dashboard rather than a work.

## 8. We do not claim completeness of any kind

Not of the sea, not of the register, not of our own capture record. A gap in the nightly capture
is recorded as a gap; the capture script exits with an error rather than writing a copy that
silently parsed nothing. Where no saved copy exists from on or before a day, the instrument
prints *not yet measurable* and refuses to substitute a derivation for a measurement.
