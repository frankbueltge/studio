# What this work does not claim

Eight refusals. Each is here because the claim next to it is one a reader could reasonably think
we were making, and we are not. Where a refusal rests on arithmetic, the arithmetic is given —
not as a finding about the sea, but as the reason we are declining to make one.

## 1. Nothing here is an accusation

No claim of illegality is made against any vessel, operator, flag state or coastal state. The
*intentional* classification the underlying data carries is a model output — the register's own
method sheet states it is *"a probability, not proof"* and that the label comes from a
machine-learning model; Global Fishing Watch's own announcement of the 2022 study calls these
**suspected** disabling events
(`https://globalfishingwatch.org/press-release/analysis-shows-vessels-identification-switched-off/`).
That restraint is inherited and repeated here rather than
quietly dropped one link down the chain. Vessel names appear as the register printed them. No
person is named.

## 2. We do not claim this is Global Fishing Watch's latency

We measured one daily edition — `https://frankbueltge.de/ghost-fleet/`, published inside our own
research ecology, built from the GFW Events API under its own seven-day window. We did not
measure the Events API. Whether the delay we found is inherent to complete vanish-and-return
evidence or is an artefact of one page's cadence is precisely the question the cover asks, and
we do not answer it ourselves.

## 3. We do not claim to know which way the share moves if the lists were longer

Doubling a list's length does not halve our share. The numerator is the count of names in the
list dated 4 August — one of the lists being lengthened — and longer later lists add to the
denominator. Both ends of the quotient grow. We ran that check rather than asserting either
direction, and it is why no such sentence appears on the work.

## 4. The band is a band because the input is a band

The register publishes an event's end only as *within the last seven days*. Every interval
derived from it is therefore a seven-day band, never a point, and every share is a range. The
**upper** end assumes nothing: whatever became of the eleven names the day itself printed, no
case this record allows puts the share above 11 of 29. The **lower** end does assume all eleven
were in fact dark on that day; not one of them is certain, so unconditionally the floor is 0.
Both ends are printed. Neither is a best estimate. And the whole band is a **ceiling**: further
nights can only add vessels to that day, so this share can only fall. The numerator is observed —
what the day's own list printed, counted by us; the denominator is derived. They are different
kinds of number and the work marks them apart on its face.

## 5. Eight days is a chosen constant, and the choice flatters us

The four-day comparison stops each day at the same age — eight days. That is the only maturity in
this corpus at which a **four-day** sequence descends throughout. At seven days a fifth day turns
it back up (33, 12, 7, 3, **9**); at four days it is a U (55, 21, 12, 6, 17, 23). Nine and ten days
also descend, but this corpus is only eleven days deep and they yield three rows and two. We
publish the constant, the alternatives, and the fact that the tidiest number was the one we chose.

## 6. The four-day sequence is a reading of our archive first

The work runs the same measurement on four consecutive days of the same sea, each stopped at the
same point in its own life — the instant our record first held the list dated eight days after it:

| day | knowable on the day |
|---|---|
| 4 August 2026 | 31 %–73 % (11 of 15–35) |
| 5 August 2026 | 10 %–25 % (3 of 12–31) |
| 6 August 2026 | 6 %–15 % (2 of 13–32) |
| 7 August 2026 | 3 %–12 % (1 of 8–33) |

**We first read that falling line as a property of the sea. It is not, and we say so on the work's
own face rather than in a footnote.** Those four numerators — 11, 3, 2, 1 — are exactly the counts
of names our record met for the first time on each of those days, and 4 August is the only day
here whose record begins on itself. That makes the first row structurally the most favourable day
in its own archive. Every band above carries the same conditions as §4: the lower ends assume, the
upper ends do not, and each is a ceiling that can only fall.

## 7. The corpus is closed, and later evidence does not move the figure

Frozen at 2026-08-15T04:36:57Z: thirty-two copies saved between 5 and 15 August 2026, holding
twelve distinct lists dated 4 to 15 August 2026 — the two windows are different and are not
interchangeable — with every count derived at that instant. Lists arriving afterwards are published as dated
addenda beside the work. A figure that moves each night cannot be checked by anyone, and a
measurement that never closes is a dashboard rather than a work.

## 8. We do not claim completeness of any kind

Not of the sea, not of the register, not of our own capture record. A gap in the nightly capture is
recorded as a gap. When a fetch returns a page the parser cannot read, the capture script writes
the copy anyway — captures are immutable and a record that hides its own bad nights is not a
record — and then exits with an error and a warning naming what it failed to parse, so an empty
copy can never pass silently for a night with nothing in it. No capture in this corpus parsed zero
vessels. Where no saved copy exists from on or before a day, the instrument prints *not yet
measurable* and refuses to substitute a derivation for a measurement.
