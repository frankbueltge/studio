# A day of the sea, held open — and one question we cannot answer without you

A public register names fishing vessels whose AIS transponders went dark. It is a good
register. It cannot describe the day it is published on, and we measured by how much.

**On 4 August 2026 that register printed eleven names for that day. Eleven days later the same
day held between twenty-nine and forty-nine.** So the share of that day's darkness that was
knowable on the day itself is **22 %–38 % — eleven of twenty-nine to forty-nine**. The rest
arrived afterwards, over weeks, one list at a time.

That is not a defect of anyone's diligence. A disabling event is a **complete vanish-and-return
story**: it cannot be counted until the ship comes back. A day of the sea is therefore nearly
empty while it is happening and keeps filling long after it is over — and nobody, including the
register, keeps a day-addressed record of that filling. We kept one: thirty-two saved copies of
the register, taken between 4 and 15 August 2026, holding twelve distinct daily lists, each copy
committed with the SHA-256 of the bytes it was parsed from, so anyone can re-fetch, re-hash and
check that our reading belongs to those bytes.

**The second measurement is the one we would most like taken away from us.** Run the same
question on four consecutive days of the same sea, each stopped at the same point in its own
life — the instant our record first held the list dated eight days after it:

| day | knowable on the day |
|---|---|
| 4 August 2026 | 31 %–73 % (11 of 15–35) |
| 5 August 2026 | 10 %–25 % (3 of 12–31) |
| 6 August 2026 | 6 %–15 % (2 of 13–32) |
| 7 August 2026 | 3 %–12 % (1 of 8–33) |

We first read that as a property of the sea. It is not, and the work now says so on its own
face: those four numerators are exactly the counts of names **our record was seeing for the
first time**, and 4 August is the only day here whose record begins on itself. The falling line
is a reading of our archive before it is a reading of the ocean. We publish it against
ourselves.

## Why we are writing to you and not to someone who would find this useful

The register we measured is **not yours**. It is a daily edition published inside our own
research ecology at `https://frankbueltge.de/ghost-fleet/`, built from your Events API, under
its own published rule: *"Window: disabling events that ended in the last 7 days (complete
vanish-and-return stories)"* (`https://frankbueltge.de/werke/ghost-fleet/`). Its own method
sheet passes on your restraint, and so do we: the *intentional* label is a model output, *"a
probability, not proof"*; your own announcement of the global study calls these **suspected**
disabling events; no claim of illegality is made here against any vessel, operator or state.

Which means the latency we measured has two components and **our instrument cannot separate
them**:

1. the part that is inherent — an event does not exist until the vessel returns, so it cannot
   be published before then; and
2. the part that is one editor's cadence — a seven-day window over events that have already
   ended.

If the first component dominates, we have measured a property of this kind of evidence and the
finding travels to every dark-vessel register in the world, including yours. If the second
dominates, we have measured one page's publishing schedule and dressed it as an ocean. **You are
the only people who can tell us which**, because you hold the events themselves and we hold only
a page made from them.

## Three asks. None of them is an endorsement.

| ask | what it costs you | what we count it as |
|---|---|---|
| Tell us what we have wrong | five minutes | a correction |
| One number: the typical lag, in days, between a disabling event *ending* and that event being available in the Events API | one figure | a measurement |
| One ruling: is the day-to-day variation above a property of the register, of our archive, or of the sea? | a sentence | a ruling |

A generous reply containing none of the three is recorded here as a **non-delivery**, and
silence is published as silence. We would rather be corrected than praised, and a rigorous
negative is a full-value answer.

## Where the thing itself is

The work is one self-contained page that runs by itself for about thirty-two seconds. It is
committed, with its builder, its captures and its checks, at

`https://github.com/frankbueltge/studio/tree/main/works/2026-08-15-still-dark`

Two files stand beside it and we would rather you had them than not: `ADDENDA.md`, where every
list arriving after the corpus was frozen at 2026-08-15T04:36:57Z is published as a dated
addendum instead of quietly moving the figure — and `OPEN-DEFECTS.md`, our own register of
everything still wrong with this work, published the day it was released.
