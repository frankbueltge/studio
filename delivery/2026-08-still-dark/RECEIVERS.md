# Receivers — *STILL DARK*, August 2026

**One receiver, named, per send. No list, no cc, no broadcast.** A second name is written to only
after the first has answered or the month has closed. Silence is published as silence. The
receiver is an **organisation and its public channel**, not a private individual; whoever the
institution routes the letter to is who answers. We do not compile people.

## The rule this list obeys

Inherited from the previous packet and unchanged: **send to the receiver who can falsify you, not
the one who can use you.** A dark-vessel register is useful to a great many organisations. Only a
handful can look at our measurement and say *you measured our publishing schedule, not the ocean*
— and that sentence is the one thing we most need said if it is true.

The measurement's own weakness names the receiver for us. The latency we found has two
components — the inherent one (an event does not exist until the vessel returns) and the
editorial one (a seven-day window over events that have already ended) — and our instrument
cannot separate them, because we hold a page and not the events. Whoever holds the events can.

## First send

**Global Fishing Watch**, research and data enquiry channel.
<https://globalfishingwatch.org/contact-us/> — the organisation's own contact page lists separate
channels for media, portal support, research and data enquiries, and API support. *(The addresses
themselves are obfuscated to automated readers on that page; we did not transcribe what we could
not read first-hand. Whoever sends reads the page.)*

Chosen because they hold the material under the register we measured. Their own Events API
supplies the disabling events — by the register's method sheet, *"only high-confidence,
intentional-classified disabling: ≥ 12 h, ≥ 50 nm offshore, good satellite coverage"*
(<https://frankbueltge.de/werke/ghost-fleet/>) — and their own research programme published the
global study of exactly this phenomenon: *"Hotspots of Unseen Fishing Vessels"*, Science Advances,
2 November 2022, lead author Heather Welch of the Institute of Marine Sciences, University of
California, Santa Cruz, reporting over 55,000 suspected intentional disabling events from 2017 to
2019 obscuring nearly five million hours of activity and up to 6 % of vessel activity
(<https://globalfishingwatch.org/press-release/analysis-shows-vessels-identification-switched-off/>).

**The obvious objection, and why it is an argument for them.** We are, in effect, writing to the
source of the data to report a property of somebody else's page built from it. That is exactly
the position in which a receiver can refuse us with authority. Ask 2 — the typical lag between an
event ending and its availability in the Events API — is a number only they can give, and it is
the number that decides whether this work has found anything at all.

## Reserves, in order

**2. Trygg Mat Tracking (TMT).** <https://www.tm-tracking.org/> — its own page: *"fisheries
intelligence, analysis, and capacity building, targeting a reduction of illegal fishing and
broader improvements in ocean governance"*, and it maintains the **Combined IUU Vessel List**,
described there as *"an up to date record of the current best-known identities and operations of
all vessels that currently appear on lists of recognised IUU fishing vessels maintained by
Regional Fisheries Management Organisations"*. Published channel: `info@tm-tracking.org`.

Second, and the strongest reserve we have: they are **register keepers whose register makes a
currency claim in its own description**. A house that says *up to date* about its own list is the
right second reader for a work about what *up to date* costs. They cannot answer ask 2 — they do
not hold the events — which is the only reason they are not first.

**3. SkyTruth.** <https://skytruth.org/> — its own page: *"Making environmental harm visible,
measurable, and actionable"*, *"leverages satellite imagery and environmental data to create tools
and develop insights that drive accountability, accelerate climate action, and support
biodiversity protection"*. Published channel: `hello@skytruth.org`.

Third, and listed with a stated reservation rather than without one: SkyTruth is named on Global
Fishing Watch's own pages as a founder of that organisation, but **its current front page names
ocean pollution, fossil fuel dependence, biodiversity, mining and gas flaring, and no
vessel-tracking work at all**. We do not claim they work on AIS today. Nothing goes on this list
that we have not read first-hand, and this is what we read.

*(A fourth slot is deliberately empty. We considered writing to the study's lead author directly
and did not: this list takes organisations and their public channels, not people at their desks.)*

## What is recorded when a send happens

Receiver · channel · date sent · date answered or closed · which of the three counting outcomes it
produced (correction / measurement / ruling) — or **non-delivery**, which is what an appreciative
reply with no correction, no number and no ruling in it is called here.
