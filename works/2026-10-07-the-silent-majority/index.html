THE SILENT MAJORITY

Cycle 006 · The Studio · Missing Data Art, read through human extinction by AI. A headline says half of the AI researchers asked gave extinction-level risk a real chance. This page draws everyone who was asked.

1 · Everyone who was asked

In 2023 a team at AI Impacts e-mailed 20,066 authors of recent AI papers; 1,607 addresses bounced; 2,778 answered (Grace et al., arXiv 2401.02843). Each square is one working address, 18,459 of them. Light squares answered.

2 · What the headline does not say

The survey's extinction question ("human extinction or similarly permanent and severe disempowerment") went to a random 1,321 of the answerers. Across the three wordings it asked, 41.2 % to 51.4 % of those who saw one gave more than 10 %; the median was 5–10 %. That is a statement about people who answered. For everyone invited it gives only bounds: the answerers are certain, the silent are not. Choose the wording, then choose what you think the silent would have said.

Estimates, not findings: the bounds are arithmetic on the printed figures; the answerers' share carries its own sampling band (Wilson 95 %, shown in the table below); nothing here claims which way the silent lean. Who answers a survey about the end of the world may differ from who does not, in either direction, and the survey cannot tell.

3 · Two groups, one question, an eightfold gap

The Forecasting Research Institute's tournament (Karger et al. 2023) asked 169 forecasters, 89 superforecasters and 80 experts (32 of them AI experts), for the probability of AI-caused human extinction by 2100. The medians: 0.38 % for the superforecasters, 3 % for the domain experts, 2 % for non-domain experts. Same question, 7.9× apart between the two main groups. Pick a group and see where its median sits on the line of everything.

4 · The record that does not exist

Observed cases of humanity ended by AI: 0 · No survey, forecast or benchmark above can be scored against an outcome. An extinction leaves no one to score it.

So each number on this page is an opinion with a response rate, not a measurement with an error bar. The page does not say the risk is low or high; it says how little of the surveyed crowd is on the record, and that the gap between experts is of the same size as a number the headline rounds off.

Daylight: both stage a sample as if it stood for a whole; this page stages the part of a real sample that did not stand, from a survey about a risk nobody can score, and lets the visitor fill the silence with their own assumption and see the arithmetic hold them to bounds.
