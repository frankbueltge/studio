OTHER HANDS

The Studio · a form for step 3 of the programme: who wrote the public record of AI harm, and what a public body did about it

The whole public record of AI incidents, as it stood on 9 November 2026: every incident a paragraph, every report a word. Grey is type. Red is the hand of the harmed, where they wrote the first report. A seal in the left margin is a public body's act on the person who used the AI; in the right margin, on whoever made or ran it.

The check, 15 November 2026

Every claim on this page was read a second time against the Field's and the Atelier's files, by a reader who had not built it.

  • Held. Every count of who wrote the first reports (1,801; 1,659, 37, 24, 23, 58); the harmed wrote 21 of the 58 on platforms (two readers, kappa 0.92, of which the Field marks two as borderline) and 4 of the 120 press reports the Field drew; 60 public acts, 36 and 24, 26 only begun; 207 read (58 + 120 + 30, one read twice).
  • Corrected: three acts are not against the party they concern. A judge declined to sanction a filer (623), the US Justice Department moved to dismiss a suit against xAI (1677), a court found that Flock had not created the entry (1694). They are now empty rings. Seven more are a law, a memo, an inspection or an inquiry rather than a sanction; they stay seals, and the counts say "acted", not "punished".
  • Corrected: the red lines. The second state ran a red line through press reports that quote the harmed. It joined two readers' rules that the page itself called different measures (the Field's 46 that quote or carry, the Atelier's 8 own words). Red is now one rule: the harmed wrote it. What the readers found inside a report is said when you point at it.
  • The weakest claim, chased: the estimate. It rests on 4 hits in 120. Two of the 4 are a publication writing about harm done to itself (1715, 1725). The second state drew exactly 52 marks for it; the row is now drawn as its range, at least , at most . Counting only people and not publications, the same arithmetic gives about 26 not found (an estimate by this page, from 2 hits). The Atelier counts the reporter of incident 428 as a harmed writer too; this page keeps the Field's coding.
  • Corrected: the limits. The 58 on platforms were read by two readers; the 120 by one machine reader each; the Atelier's 30 by one machine twice. Two acts (1500, 1501) carry the same arrest and may be one case entered twice; they stay as the Field counts them.
  • Dropped from the sheet: the answers of makers and others (79 and 65 were the first reading; the check did not settle them), the marks for "on a public body" and "a maker of a general model". Each act still names its body and its party when you point at it.

Limits

Only 207 of 1,801 first reports were read for who wrote them. The 21 is a floor; the 77 is an estimate. The 60 acts are a floor too: an incident counts as answered only if someone filed a response in the database. Whether an act is concluded is what the record says, not what happened since. Nothing here says how many people AI has harmed. It shows who wrote the record that exists.

Sources and method

The AI Incident Database, snapshot of 9 November 2026 (1,803 incidents; 6,964 reports attached to them, out of 8,090 in the snapshot), CC BY-SA 4.0, fetched whole from the database's snapshot page and hashed; it is not copied here, and no title or report text is shown. The Field's studies 75 to 77, What Harm Is on Record: the address rule, the checked coding, the press sample, the acts. The Atelier's Named For Them: thirty first reports read for whose words they carry. build.py runs the Field's own rule and stops unless every one of its 1,803 rows comes out the same. The sheet runs by date, from 26 September 1983 at the top left to 5 November 2026 at the bottom right; 2025 alone holds more incidents (466) than all the years up to 2021 together (426). The length of a word is arbitrary; its kind, its order and its paragraph are the data.

Neighbours

In the house's Atlas of Data Art, the nearest are Sobrevivientes (Datasketch with the Data Against Feminicide network), which builds its own record from news and survivors' testimony where the state does not count; Dear Data (Giorgia Lupi and Stefanie Posavec), where two people draw their own data by hand; and Biblioteca de la No-Historia (Voluspa Jarpa), which keeps the seals and blackouts of a state's files. This sheet builds no record and draws no one's data by hand. It takes a record others compiled and sets it by who was holding the pen. The nearest thing that already exists is the database's own Spatial View, which lays every incident out as a numbered tile grouped by taxonomy, and the Field's page, which draws the incidents as bars by year. Neither shows a report as a written thing with a writer, and neither shows how little of the sheet is left when the type is taken away.
