# UNISON — concept proposal, session 102 (2026-08-18)

*The Artist's proposal as returned, unedited. It was written before the conductor's probe ran;
`MATERIAL.md` reports what the probe found, including where it contradicts this text.*

---

**Claim** — Eighty-seven national laboratories whose one job is to be UTC exactly are shown missing it, the same subtraction repeated ~144,000 times at speed until the promised unison is visibly and audibly a smear.

**Machine advantage (REPETITION)** — The dial advances one five-day grid date every ~50 ms; on each, the machine re-performs one identical operation per lab: place a mark at UTC−UTC(k). At ~20 dates/second and ~65 labs/date the visitor watches ~40,000 identical subtractions land within thirty seconds, and hears them: each lab's click is delayed by its own offset, one millisecond per nanosecond. 1996 opens near unison — a single attack. By 2010 the attack is a roll. A counter logs the operations and never stops. Ordinary hands cannot: the values sit in 364 differently formatted monthly bulletins, 1996–2026, and the drift exists only as texture across 144,000 placements, never in one number.

**Form** — Full viewport, black. A dial: angle = laboratory (fixed seat), radius = deviation, log-scaled, 1 ns at centre to 100 microseconds at rim. Each date, every present lab flashes at once and decays over ~2 s, so three or four dates overlap — that after-image is the smear. Outer ring = calendar; a bottom band accumulates the run. Sound: one click per lab per date, offset 1 ms/ns; agreement is one attack, disagreement a flam, excursions past a microsecond drop in pitch. Loops from 1996, unattended. Pointer or arrows select a seat: that lab solos as a line, the ensemble dims. Carries (a), (b), (c). Not a chart, because the claim under test is unison, and unison is something you hear failing.

**Nearest neighbours**
- Ryoji Ikeda, *test pattern*, 2008, https://www.ryojiikeda.com/project/testpattern/ — his data is interchangeable, barcoded for sensation; here the exact values are the content.
- Aaron Koblin, *The Sheep Market*, 2006, https://www.aaronkoblin.com/work/thesheepmarket/ — ten thousand humans repeat one task and their drift is the charm; here one machine repeats without drift, so institutional drift is the only variable.
- Jem Finer, *Longplayer*, 1999–, https://longplayer.org/about/how-does-longplayer-work/ — its tirelessness composes; ours replays a measured record whose disagreements are documentary.

**The visitor** — Someone in the room ninety seconds, who never wondered where time comes from. One belief dents: that "the time" is one thing. They hear eighty-seven versions fail to coincide, and feel a machine do one small sum until the sum becomes an image. No lesson.

**Material** — BIPM Circular T, [UTC−UTC(k)]/ns, five-day grid, at https://webtai.bipm.org/ftp/pub/tai/Circular-T/cirt/ (index https://www.bipm.org/en/time-ftp/circular-t). Verified first-hand by curl: cirt.100 (1996 May 14; 24,058 bytes; 200) through cirt.463 (200); cirt.464 is 404. 364 issues, thirteen sampled averaging 32,362 bytes, ~11.8 MB raw. cirt.462 lists 87 labs; ~2,200 grid dates, ~144,000 values. Parsed offline to one typed-array binary beside index.html: ~0.9 MB, one top-level file, inside 25 MiB, no runtime network. CC BY 4.0 (https://www.bipm.org/en/copyright); credit stays on screen.

**Discriminating act?** — No. It shows one published subtraction; it never rules which lab is good or which gap matters. Nothing classified, nothing to probe. Missing entries ("−") draw as absences, never zero.

**Milestones**
1. Harvest — 364 files fetched, byte-logged, hashed.
2. Parser — both bulletin layouts read; counts published; every unparsed line listed, none dropped.
3. Silent dial — thirty years sweep, correct seats, honest absences.
4. Ensemble — click per lab, 1 ms/ns, stable over thirty unattended minutes.
5. Solo — a seat is selectable; the screen changes.
6. Panel — severed readers meet it cold; answers published verbatim beside it.

Panel survival: nothing to read. Cold viewers have words for clocks, disagreement, worsening; a twelve-word caption names the source only after they speak.

**Strongest case against** — It is a polar scatter plot with a click track. The repetition is playback, not computation: the machine subtracts nothing, it reads 144,000 numbers a committee already subtracted, so the repeated on-screen operation is "draw a dot" — what every animated chart does. That local realizations differ from UTC is metrology's most published commonplace, issued monthly by the institution itself; nothing is exposed. Audibility rests on a millionfold scale factor the artist chose, so the drama is manufactured by the mapping, not found in the material. And a motion designer could fake it in a week with invented laboratories, and nobody standing there ninety seconds could tell.
