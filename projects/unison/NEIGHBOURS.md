# UNISON — adversarial neighbour search

*Commissioned hostile: find the work that already exists and makes this one unnecessary. Returned
unedited except for length, which was capped at 550 words in the brief. The three URLs that carry
the verdict were re-fetched first-hand by the conductor before the gate: all three HTTP 200.*

---

## Nearest neighbour

**Brian House, *Synchronizing Uncertainty* (2025), ArtYard, Frenchtown NJ** —
https://brianhouse.net/works/synchronizing_uncertainty/
Hundreds of Mirollo–Strogatz oscillators on a mesh network, each flashing an LED and firing a piezo
click, negotiating their timing until factions emerge; agreement collapses to one attack,
disagreement spreads into polyrhythm. This is UNISON's exact sensory thesis — *clocks that are
supposed to agree, failing, rendered as simultaneous flash plus per-element click* — already built,
and built better than a screen can, because it is physical and live. UNISON survives only on the
strength of its *source*: House's clocks disagree by simulation; UNISON's disagree because 87
governments actually missed, on the record, for thirty years. That is a real difference, but it is
the only one. The form is taken.

## Further neighbours

- **Brian House, *Metric Displacement* (2021)** — https://brianhouse.net/works/metric_displacement/
  — three turntables streamed from Portland/Bern/Shenzhen, network latency turning a unison groove
  into a per-listener flam. ***Overlap***: the delay-proportional-to-error click mechanic is
  precisely this.
- **Agustina Woodgate, *National Times* (2016/2019)**, Whitney Biennial —
  https://whitney.org/media/42350 — 40 "slave" clocks synced to NIST, sandpaper hands erasing their
  own faces. Daylight: about labour and the *enforcement* of unison, not the residual error.
- **Sara Morawetz, *61/60* (2015–)** — https://artistprofile.com.au/sara-morawetz — cymbal struck in
  Times Square at the instant of each leap second. Daylight: one-second astronomical correction, not
  the nanosecond lab spread.
- **Jonathon Keats, *New England River Time* (2025)**, MIT Museum *Split | Second* —
  https://mitmuseum.mit.edu — river flow as a rival clock. Daylight: proposes an alternative time,
  doesn't audit the official one.
- **Katie Paterson, *Timepieces (Solar System)* (2014)** —
  https://katiepaterson.org/artwork/timepieces — nine clocks legitimately disagreeing by planetary
  day-length. Daylight: cosmological, no institution, no error.
- **Ruth Ewan, *We Could Have Been Anything…* (2011)** —
  https://www.ruthewan.com/we-could-have-been-anything-that-we-wanted-to-be-and-its-not-too-late-to-change
  — decimal Republican clocks in public. Daylight: political refusal of a standard, not measurement
  of compliance.

## The existing-tool answer: **yes**

**https://webtai.bipm.org/database/canvas.html** — the BIPM's own "Graphs → UTC-UTC(k)" page. Select
a laboratory, toggle UTC−UTC(k) or UTCr−UTC(k), set a custom MJD range; it plots the series
interactively. It is Cartesian, one lab at a time, silent, unanimated — no ensemble, no smear. **The
data view exists; the ensemble view does not.** Also checked: `/database/canvas_gnss.html`,
`/d_plot.html`, `/timescales.html`, the Time Dept API (`webtai.bipm.org/api`), the Circular T HTML
edition and FTP archive, `bipm.org/en/time-metrology`, KCDB CCTF-K001.UTC. Negative checks (nothing
found): NIST/PTB/NPL UTC(k) history plots, GitHub repository search for Circular T
parsers/dashboards (**zero results**), time-nut/amateur-metrology and Grafana communities, and the
Data Sonification Archive — **no one has used Circular T as art material at all.**

**House catalogue** (505 entries, fetched): clean. No clock, time-standard or synchronisation work.
Nearest are Lozano-Hemmer's *Pulse Room* (per-element flash-and-decay from a body signal) and
Sakamoto/Manabe's *Sensing Streams* — both formal cousins only.

## Verdict

**Half-occupied** — the subject is genuinely unclaimed and the data is untouched by artists, but the
flash-plus-click-ensemble-of-disagreeing-clocks form is already Brian House's, twice.
