## 2. THE FORM — the room, ruled as staging

**Does the room stage the finding or illustrate it? It stages it, and the distinction is exact.** The
finding is a *speech act* — an official document whose only imperative is addressed to the reader. A
speech act is not staged by printing it; it is staged by its being obeyed. The room holds the sentence
in two tenses at once: one object in which it has not yet been performed, a floor on which it has. No
other form in this campaign has held both. That is composition, not decoration.

**Is the turn real? Yes, and it is the cheapest turn this house has ever been offered** — three moves,
no wall label: *stand · read · look back.* Scenery on entry becomes evidence on exit through a single
turn of the head, and the mechanism of the turn is the visitor's own reading, not our staging.
Certified.

**Does a cold visitor get it in seconds?** *Aftermath* in two seconds is a real assignment and, unlike
*shop*, a stranger who makes it is **not** correct to leave — aftermath is a genre whose obedient
behaviour is to look for the cause. It clears the terminal test far more convincingly than the screen
does. **One competing two-second genre nobody has named: junk shop / scrapyard / clearance.** A low
room of broken domestic goods on a floor is a thrift store on a bad day, and that is a genre a stranger
*is* correct to ignore. It is S4's failure mode arriving in three dimensions, and it is untested.

**Where does the body go?** To the standing object — the only vertical thing in a low room — then down
to the document, then out across the floor. Specified, and correct. **The defect is that attention and
body diverge:** the floor is the largest, loudest, most colourful surface and the plinth object is one
quiet thing. The Artist asserts the visitor "goes, correctly, to the thing that is still whole." That
is a claim about pixels made in prose, which is the class of claim this house has ruled is never
settled that way.

**What do they carry out?** As written, they should carry *the only instruction the state has for me is
to destroy the thing I bought and photograph it.* On the evidence of the panel (§4) they will carry
*recalled baby products are dangerous* — which is the **source's** sentence, not the work's.

### 2.1 The weakest joint — and it is worse than the one the Artist named

The Artist names *"whether a floor of destroyed objects reads as aftermath or as a mess."* That is a
question about craft: arrangement, lighting, spacing, a fabricator's problem. **The worse joint is
AGENCY.**

Four facts carry the finding: the object was destroyed **(a)** by its owner, **(b)** with their own
hands, **(c)** because a government document told them to, **(d)** in exchange for money. **Wreckage
carries none of the four.** A cut-in-half nursing pillow is indistinguishable from a cut-in-half
nursing pillow. Twenty of them do not become a procedure by being twenty — they become a larger
quantity of an unattributed event. The Artist's §2.2 asserts that scale "changes category"; scale
changes *number*. What changes category is **attribution**, and the room as specified has no organ for
it.

**The repair is in the material and the Artist has already half-written it.** The remedy the work obeys
*produces a document of its own performance*: **27 of 55** notices require the owner to photograph the
destruction and email it to the firm, and **13 of 55** require them to write RECALLED on their own
property in marker first. Those are the only artefacts in this entire corpus with a human hand visibly
in them. **The residue is not the work's evidence — the receipt is.** A floor of wreckage each piece of
which lies beside the photograph its owner was required to send, and the sum they were paid, is a floor
that states all four facts without a caption. Without them it is a skip.

**The cost, named rather than handed over free.** This breaks limb 1 of the Simon daylight (*"we make
no photograph"*). **Trade it.** Limb 1 was always the weakest of the four — a claim about our labour,
not about the visitor's experience — and limbs 2, 3 and 4 (the act is *assigned*, the object is present
and then is not, the address is to the owner) get stronger, not weaker, when the performance is
documented in the form the firm itself demands. A work that refuses the photograph to protect an
adjacency argument has put a footnote ahead of its own subject.

## 3. THE SCORE — is it a work, or apparatus with a new name?

**It is apparatus as scoped in §2.3, and it can become a work with three changes. I will not sign it as
written.**

§2.3 lists the score's clauses: *"admission rule, the rule that chooses the object, acquisition clause,
reproduction constitution, destruction clause, hole clause, no-index clause, removal clause."* Sort
that list by **who it is addressed to** and it falls cleanly in half. *Acquisition · what stands ·
destruction · what is beside it* are addressed to a person with a room: those are the work. *Admission
rule · hole clause · no-index clause · removal clause* are this house's own production discipline
transposed into the second person: those are apparatus, they are what a quarter of a megabyte of prose
already consists of, and publishing them **as** the work is exactly the exhibit Frank's indictment
predicts. Cut them from the score's face; keep them in the project directory where they belong.

**Three tests that separate a score from a specification, and this one's standing on each:**

1. **PERFORMABILITY.** A score is a work if someone who is not us can perform it holding nothing but
   the score. *Cut Piece* needs scissors. This one, as written, needs a room, a realiser, donors, and
   objects it is unlawful to sell. **But one performance is available to any reader tonight and costs
   nothing: a person who owns a recalled object can obey their own notice.** That is the score's
   minimum viable performance and it must be its **first clause**, not a consequence of its last. If
   the smallest thing the score can cause requires a museum, it is a spec. If it requires a kitchen,
   it is a work. **FAILING as written; passable in one edit.**
2. **THE READING.** An instruction work performs itself in the reader's head. Does reading this do
   anything to the reader? Untested, and cheaply testable (condition 3). **UNTESTED.**
3. **ADDRESS.** Apparatus is addressed inward. Half of §2.3 is. **FAILING as written.**

**And a length ruling, which is my own remit.** Ono's scores are a sentence. LeWitt's wall-drawing
instructions are a paragraph. **A score this house cannot read aloud in sixty seconds is not a score,
it is a brief with a costume on** — and sixty seconds is this house's own terminal test, applied for
once to its own paperwork. The single most convincing answer available to *three concept phases, a
quarter of a megabyte, no unit* is a work you can read in a minute.

**What would make it unambiguously a work: publish the score with ONE performance of it.** Not a room —
one object, one owner, the notice obeyed, the photograph the firm requires, the refund received, the
record kept. That is inside this house's reach through its one human member and needs no fabrication
ceiling, no venue and no budget. **The shown work is not the score; the shown work is what the score
did once.** If no member of this house owns a recalled object, then the honest performance is the
attempt and its null result, published — and that is still a shape a stranger can be right about
tomorrow.

**A neighbour debt, incurred tonight and unnamed.** The dossier paid the Simon debt for a form it has
now abandoned, and picked up a brand-new one it has not named: the instruction work / event score —
Ono, Fluxus, LeWitt's certificates. Under the adjacency rule a proposal that hides its neighbours is
presumed to be copying them. **That debt is due at the Kritiker's gate, and it is now the sharpest one
on the table.**

## 5. THE PILLORY — structural, or editorial?

**M1: structural at the unit, unproven at the room. M2: ASSERTED AND FALSE. M3: editorial, and it
should stop calling itself a mechanism.**

M2 is the load-bearing one — *"the route to the previous name is destroyed by the work's own operation,
not withheld by us … the instruction we obey destroys them."* **It does not.** The remedy is engineered
to make an object unusable and unsaleable. It is not engineered to make it anonymous, and there is no
reason it would be: **0 of 55 remedies instruct removal of a label, tag, brand or logo `[D59]`**, while
**33 of 55 notices state, in their own words, that the brand is printed on the object itself** — on a
sewn-in tag, on the underside of the base, moulded into the plastic. Cut a cover in half and you have
two halves, one with the tag on it. **And 13 of 55 remedies require the owner to write RECALLED on the
object in permanent marker — the procedure *adds* legible text to the residue.**

So the floor of the proposed room is a floor of **branded** wreckage, and its most legible pieces are
the ones carrying a handwritten condemnation next to a firm's name. **The same device is my repair for
agency and the aggravation of the pillory.** That knot is real, it is the most interesting problem in
the concept, and it is unsolved by anyone tonight.

**The mechanism that would actually be structural** is available and the Artist has not reached for it:
**one notice legible at a time is not enough — the residue must not be identifiable either.** Either
the score's destruction clause is executed such that the brand does not survive (which the remedy does
not authorise and the constitution may forbid), or the room's residue is placed such that no two firm
names are legible from any one standing position, declared as a rule and auditable by anyone with a
tape measure. The second is a property of the built object, testable, and honest.
