## 2. CONFIRMED WORKS (URL retrieved by me tonight)

### Museum of Failure — Dr. Samuel West, founded 2017, touring
`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Failure` (confirmed via search; official site
`themuseumoffailure.net` located but not fetched directly — Wikipedia and CNN/Goethe-Institut
coverage confirm it independently). Opened Helsingborg, Sweden, 7 June 2017; now touring.
**What it is:** ~80–100 curated failed commercial products and services (Google Glass, Sony
Betamax, Coca-Cola Blak, Apple Pippin, etc.), assembled by a psychologist to make a point about
organizational risk-tolerance and innovation.
**Closeness:** This *is* the real referent behind the proposal's unsourced §13 line "the recall
genre's own popular form — the 'failed products' collection." It confirms the genre exists and is
real, and it confirms the proposal's own distinction holds: Museum of Failure is curated (a
psychologist chose ~80 objects for their humor/lesson value), closed to a fixed touring set, and
organized around **commercial failure**, not bodily hazard. It does not use CPSC or any recall
notice as its unit; the "recall" material is not its material. **Verdict: real, adjacent by genre
label only, not a material or argument neighbour.** The proposal should cite this URL and drop the
unsourced §13 flag.

### Taryn Simon — *Contraband* (2010)
`https://tarynsimon.com/works/contraband/` (fetched; confirmed also via Hyperallergic, Steidl,
Gagosian). 1,075 photographs of items detained or seized by U.S. Customs and Border Protection and
USPS at JFK, shot over five continuous days/nights in Nov 2009, presented against a white
backdrop, arranged alphabetically by object.
**Closeness — the single nearest material neighbour found:** Simon's unit is *a photograph of an
ordinary (and extraordinary) object, made possible by a U.S. federal agency's own act of
seizure/interdiction*, exhibited as a systematic index. That is structurally very close to this
proposal's "a photograph of an object the state has flagged." **Daylight that exists:** Contraband
is *illegality*, not *hazard to the owner* — CBP seizes because import is unlawful (counterfeits,
endangered-species parts, undeclared goods), not because the object will hurt the person who
already owns and uses it; the objects are not the buyer's own, they never reach a home. It is also
**closed and finite** (one five-day sweep, a fixed edition of prints/book), not a standing,
growing, clock-driven body. It has no consumer "sold at / stop using" address to the owner — the
owner is absent from the frame; Simon interviews/depicts confiscation, not domestic risk.

### Taryn Simon — *An American Index of the Hidden and Unfamiliar* (2007)
`https://tarynsimon.com/works/aihu/` (title and description confirmed via Whitney Museum
exhibition page and multiple secondary sources; not independently fetched from tarynsimon.com in
this session — flagged as secondary-sourced, not primary-fetched).
**Closeness:** A photographic index of ~60 large-format images of things hidden from or unfamiliar
to the American public (nuclear waste storage, contraband, a cryonics facility, a research herd of
diseased research animals), each captioned with a factual text — methodologically the closest
precedent in the art world for "system I did not build hands me the subject; I photograph it
straight and caption it with fact, not affect." **Daylight:** closed (2007, ~60 works, a book), not
accumulating; author-selected sites via negotiated access, not admitted wholesale by rule from a
public feed; no consumer-hazard throughline.

### Chris Jordan — *Running the Numbers* (2006–2018 series, e.g. *Venus* 2011)
`https://www.artsy.net/artwork/chris-jordan-running-the-numbers` (confirmed; corroborated by
Oberlin/Allen Memorial Art Museum and David Brower Center exhibition pages).
**What it is:** Large photographic composites, each built from thousands of individual consumer
objects (plastic bags, cans, cell phones) arranged to visualize a specific consumption statistic.
**Closeness:** Same genre-level move — mass-produced ordinary objects marshalled as evidence of a
systemic problem, at accumulating/mass scale, with a moral address to the viewer's own
consumption. **Daylight:** Jordan's objects are anonymous and generic (a photograph *standing in*
for a statistic he computed), not individuated recall notices with hazard, incident count, sold-at
line and a named seller; the works are closed, single, non-clock-driven compositions the artist
authored and finished; there is no institutional publication cadence and no per-object dossier.

### Adam Chodzko — *Product Recall* (1994), V&A collection
`https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O117542/product-recall-print-chodzko-adam` (fetched).
**What it is:** A 1994 flyer/print inviting owners of a specific Vivienne Westwood jacket to a
gathering to share memories of buying and wearing it; documented on video.
**Closeness: false friend.** Despite the title, this is not about consumer-safety recall at all —
"recall" here means *memory recall*, and the "product" is a fashion item with no hazard content.
Worth naming only so the studio does not later discover the title collision and worry over
nothing; there is no daylight argument needed because there is no shared material.

### MoMA — *SAFE: Design Takes On Risk* (2005, Paola Antonelli)
`https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/106` (confirmed).
**What it is:** ~300 contemporary products/prototypes *designed to protect* the body and mind from
danger (helmets, safety equipment, emergency design), not products found to be dangerous.
**Closeness:** Inverse of the proposal's material — objects that succeed at safety, not objects a
government agency found to fail at it. Named because a critic doing genre-adjacency work would
likely surface it; the daylight is total (opposite valence of the same word "safety").

### Regretsy (April Winchell, blog, active roughly 2009–2013)
Confirmed via multiple secondary sources (BUST, Goodreads, book edition); not independently
fetched (site is defunct).
**What it is:** A snarky blog curating badly-made Etsy craft listings for comic effect.
**Closeness:** This is the actual referent for the "regretsy-style failure collections" vocabulary
the task asked me to check. **Daylight: total.** It is commercial-craft mockery, curated for
comedy, with no safety-hazard content, no government source, and long dead as a going concern. Not
a real neighbour — noted only to close off the vocabulary check cleanly.

## 4. TOP THREE NEAREST NEIGHBOURS

**1. Taryn Simon — *Contraband* (2010).** Nearest on **form**: a systematic photographic index of
ordinary-and-extraordinary objects, individuated one-per-frame, made legible only because a U.S.
federal agency flagged each one. To have daylight, the proposal must argue (a) *hazard to the
owner* vs. Simon's *illegality of import* — the accusation in this proposal's material points at
the person who already owns and uses the object, where Simon's objects never reach a home; and (b)
*standing/accumulating/clock-driven* vs. Simon's *closed, five-day, fixed edition* — the proposal's
whole claim in §10 (REPEAT, the differential between extent 1 and extent 20) has no equivalent in
Contraband, which is whole at first sight and stays whole. This is the comparison a serious critic
would reach for first, and the proposal does not currently address it anywhere in §13.

**2. Museum of Failure (Samuel West, 2017–).** Nearest on **genre label**: the "failed/withdrawn
consumer object collection" the proposal itself invoked from memory. Daylight required: curated
(one man's ~80 picks for punchline value) vs. this proposal's zero-discretion admission rule;
commercial-failure-as-comedy vs. bodily-hazard-stated-by-the-state; closed touring set vs. a
standing room that never closes. The proposal should replace its unsourced §13 mention with this
URL and this argument — it is a one-line fix, but it is currently missing, and a Kritiker would
find the gap in about the same time I did.

**3. Josh Begley — *Metadata+*/*Drones+* (2014–2017)** — already identified and argued by the
studio itself in §13. I re-checked the daylight argument there and it holds up under adversarial
reading: Begley's unit is a push notification (a date, a place, a body count) that schedules the
recipient, which this proposal explicitly forbids; his corpus has no "extent" a visitor can ever
stand inside, only a stream of the newest. I did not find a closer photographic-recall-unit
precedent than Simon (rank 1) or a closer accumulating-clock precedent than nothing I could find —
so Begley remains correctly placed as the nearest previously-known neighbour, but it is not the
single nearest neighbour overall once Contraband is admitted to the set.

*(Chris Jordan's Running the Numbers and Christien Meindertsma's PIG 05049 — the latter already in
§13 — sit a clear rung below these three: same broad genre of "ordinary objects marshalled as
systemic evidence," but neither shares the recall-notice unit, the government-clock cadence, or
the owner-directed imperative that make Contraband and Museum of Failure the sharper comparisons.)*

## 5. VERDICT

**Is there an existing work that would make this proposal derivative? No single work found tonight
duplicates the proposal's specific combination** — (a) unit = a government agency's own recall
notice photograph, (b) admission rule = zero discretion, wholesale, from a live public feed, (c)
clock = the agency's own irregular publication cadence, not the artist's, (d) accumulation that
never closes, (e) the accusation delivered by the state's own imperative sentence pointed at the
visitor's own house. Nothing in this search combines all five. But **the proposal's daylight
argument in §13 is incomplete as written, exactly as its own §15 feared** — it argues against
Begley and Meindertsma but is silent on Taryn Simon's *Contraband*, which is the nearest neighbour
on raw form (government-flagged object, photographed straight, indexed systematically) that
exists. That gap must be closed before the Kritiker sits, or the Kritiker will find it in the same
five searches I ran.

**The work the studio should be most worried about: Taryn Simon's *Contraband* (2010),
`https://tarynsimon.com/works/contraband/`.** It is real, well-known, museum- and Gagosian-
exhibited, and shares the proposal's central photographic move — an object made visible to a
stranger only because a federal agency acted on it — closely enough that an unprepared defense of
this proposal would visibly flinch on first mention of it. The daylight is real and arguable (hazard-to-owner vs. illegality-of-import; standing/growing vs. closed/finite) but it is currently
**unwritten**, and that is the single highest-priority fix this adjacency search surfaces.
