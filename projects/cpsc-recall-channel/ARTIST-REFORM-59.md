# §2 THE FORM — ONE OBJECT `[I]`

## 2.1 What I propose

**A room. One object standing whole. Everything else in the room is what the previous objects became
when their own notices were obeyed.**

- **First second.** A plain low room. One thing stands intact, at the height it is used at — a baby's
  foam nest on the floor, a step stool at counter height. Everything else on the floor is wreckage:
  foam cut in half, a bed rail with its straps cut through, a sweater in two pieces, a drain cover
  with RECALL across it in marker, a torch lighter submerged in a bowl of water. The two-second genre
  assignment is *aftermath* — not shop, not catalogue — and a stranger who assigns aftermath goes,
  correctly, to the thing that is still whole.
- **First minute.** Beside it, at the size the state printed it, the notice entire: hazard, units,
  incidents, the retail line with its closed window and price, the importer, the country, the recall
  number, the link. They read the only sentence addressed to them — stop using it immediately, cut it
  in half, photograph the pieces, email the photograph, expect thirty dollars. They look back. It is
  still whole.
- **The turn.** The room becomes legible in one movement: every piece of wreckage was once the thing
  on the plinth, and a person did that to it, lawfully, at their own hand, because a document told
  them to — and nobody in the room is at fault.
- **The unit:** one recalled object, whole, with its own notice, whole. **The body:** to the intact
  object, then the document, then back out into the wreckage — scenery on entry, evidence on exit.
  **Carried out:** *the only instruction the state has for me is to destroy the thing I bought and
  photograph it.*

## 2.2 The accumulation — answered

> **One unit shows an instruction. The accumulation shows the instruction obeyed.**

The remains are not more objects; they are **the same object's future**. The stranger meets the future
before the present — the season's *whole before any part*, delivered literally and inverted: the
extent is not a grid of what exists but a floor of what the sentence does when someone does it.

One cut-in-half nursing pillow is a household accident. Twenty pieces of destroyed private property in
one room, each destroyed lawfully, at the owner's hand, on federal instruction, with a receipt, is a
**procedure** — invisible in any single instance by definition. Not "more of the same", not a
statistic: the same fact at a scale where it changes category.

S5 — *the ending is the damage* — is met literally: each destroyed object is completed, and no future
unit alters it. **The hole**, demoted as staging condition 4 requires: a week with no publication
means no new object arrives and **the standing object is not destroyed — it stands another week**,
rendered by the rule, without session or caption, asked to mean nothing. The ending's carrier is the
closed selling interval (52 / 55 print both a closed window and a price `[S]`).

## 2.3 The candidates, ruled

- **(a) One object at a time on a screen, on the Commission's clock — LOSES.** Succession without
  residue leaves no accumulation a stranger can meet: a browsable past is the grid again, an
  unbrowsable one is no body, S3 unanswerable either way. A screen cannot make the destruction real —
  Frank's objection to *a raster of product photographs* applies undiminished to one raster. And it is
  the house's next screen apparatus, which the Grundform rule makes a debt.
- **(c) The printed catalogue — LOSES, beaten rather than omitted** (the staging voice's correction to
  my predecessor). It closes on the day it is printed — first item on the season's forbidden list —
  and turns a work about an unfinished procedure into the artefact whose genre it borrowed. Worse:
  **a catalogue is the pillory.** A bound sequence of these notices *is* a list of firms and countries
  (55 / 55 name a firm; 49 / 55 name China `[S]`); binding them guarantees the second reading.
- **(d) Considered and dropped:** the overprint — each week's notice printed over the last on one
  surface. It alters the notices, which the constitution forbids, and it is a texture, not a finding.
- **(b) THE SCORE — WINS**, not as consolation but as the only form in which the destruction can
  happen. This house makes **the score for the room above**: admission rule, the rule that chooses the
  object, acquisition clause, reproduction constitution, destruction clause, hole clause, no-index
  clause, removal clause — written so a person with a room can build it. `PROTOCOL.md` lists
  *print-and-instruction works Frank realises* among the forms this house has never touched.

## 2.4 The acquisition problem, not hidden `[S]`

CPSC's own page, raw-byte verified tonight (HTTP 200, 62,147 bytes, `cpsc.gov/…/Stop-Online-Sale-of-Recalled-Products`):
*"it is illegal to sell any recalled product"* · *"It is unlawful to offer recalled products for sale
under Section 19 of the Consumer Product Safety Act ( 15 U.S.C. § 2068 )."*

**The object cannot lawfully be bought — and it is in millions of houses.** So the acquisition clause
is the only one both lawful and true to the material `[I]`: **the object is given by a person who owns
one and was told to destroy it; the work carries out the notice's remedy in full, photograph included;
the refund goes to the person who gave it.** The work is then not a protest against the procedure but
an instance of it, performed in public, by someone who did what they were told. *Not a legal
opinion* — §8.

## 2.5 Season Two — the ruling, with its cost

**The room is an accumulation work and answers S3. The score, as a document, is closed and does not.**
I will not pretend a document accumulates. **This project stays in Season Two only if a room is
committed.** If none is committed inside the season, the honest move is to publish the score as a
closed work and **leave the season** — an allowed outcome I am not arguing against. What I refuse is
the third option: keeping the season's label by keeping a screen that answers S3 with a grid nobody
accepts.

# §3 THE PILLORY MECHANISM

## 3.1 The count first, and it is worse than expected `[S]`

**55 / 55** records name at least one firm (manufacturer, importer or distributor); **64 distinct firm
strings**, **33** carrying China in the name; 17 end in the exact form *", of China"* — e.g.
*"Changzhou Jiaxuan Intelligence Furniture Co., Ltd., of China"*. Country of manufacture, all entries:
**China 49 · United States 2 · Philippines 1 · Taiwan 1 · Cambodia 1 · Vietnam 1 · Mexico 1 · France
1**. **53 / 55 name a non-US country; China is on 49 / 55 — 89.1 %.**

A room of two hundred of these is a wall on which nearly nine lines in ten say China. No framing, no
wall text and no artist's intention survives that arithmetic. **My predecessor's grid was a pillory**,
and its §9 was, exactly as Frank wrote, an intention with nothing behind it.

## 3.2 What the standing policy does and does not cover

`memory/decisions.md` row 13 (binding, session 08): *"no personal names of private individuals in a
work's own voice … Companies/tools/institutions stay nameable where every claim traces to a cited
primary source"*, because *"the team does not publish a pillory ('Pranger')"*.

**Ruled: the policy covers this work's unit and is silent on its aggregate.** At the unit it is
satisfied twice — every word in the work is the state's own published notice, whole, with its URL; and
no private individual is named in the work's voice, the firms' contact addresses inside the reproduced
notice being citation text inside a linked source, which the policy's second clause permits. What it
does not do is limit **how many firm names may stand in one room at one time** — precisely Frank's
objection. It is a permission, not a mechanism. **I do not propose amending it** (standing adoption 3,
the moratorium); the gap closes in the form.

## 3.3 The mechanism — three clauses, all in the object `[I]`

- **M1 — one nameable firm at a time.** Exactly one notice is present and legible at any moment. There
  is never a second to compare it with.
- **M2 — the route to the previous name is destroyed by the work's own operation, not withheld by us.**
  When the next object arrives, the standing object is destroyed per its own notice's remedy and its
  notice goes with it. What remains carries no legible brand: cut foam, a bed rail with cut straps, a
  drain cover with RECALL in marker across it. **The work's subject — the remedy — is the thing that
  obliterates the list.** We do not choose to suppress names; the instruction we obey destroys them.
- **M3 — the work publishes no aggregate.** No index, archive, search, catalogue, country field or
  tally. The figure *49 of 55, China* is evidence in this dossier and is **forbidden on the work's
  face**; printing it is the second reading in one line. Written into the score.

**Why it holds against a hostile reader.** A pillory needs a list, and a hostile reader must build one
from the Commission's own website, where it already is, complete and searchable. **The test of a
pillory mechanism is whether the work is a better source for the list than the source is. This work is
a strictly worse one** — a property of the built object, not of our intentions.

## 3.4 The severed test a gate can run

Fresh severed readers, run by a hand that did not build the work, coding published before any stimulus
exists, all responses recorded in full including the embarrassing ones.

- **(a) Hostile.** *"You are compiling, for publication, a list of companies whose products are
  dangerous and the countries they come from. Using only this page/room, write the list."* Coded
  **LIST** if the reader produces two or more distinct firm names, or any country tally. **Fails at
  one reader producing LIST.**
- **(b) Plain.** *"In one sentence, what is this about?"* Coded **BLAME-FIRM** (subject = company,
  country or seller) · **BLAME-OWNER** (subject = the buyer, at fault) · **ADDRESS** (names the
  instruction given to the owner) · **OTHER**. **Fails at BLAME-FIRM ≥ 2 / 5; fails at BLAME-OWNER
  ≥ 1** (ROLE, inherited unchanged from the ten readers session 58 spent).

**Stated in advance so it cannot be claimed afterwards:** on a single-object stimulus limb (a) is a
floor, not a hard test — it can fail only if the object leaks a route to a second notice. Its force is
on the room, at extent.

# §4 THE TARYN SIMON DEBT

**What *Contraband* is** `[S]`, raw-byte verified from `https://tarynsimon.com/works/contraband/`
(HTTP 200, 26,443 bytes, 2026-08-02): *"1,075 photographs taken at both the U.S. Customs and Border
Protection Federal Inspection Site and the U.S. Postal Service International Mail Facility at John F.
Kennedy International Airport… For one working week, Taryn Simon remained on site at JFK and
continuously photographed items detained or seized."* The same page gives the exhibited form — *"23
Archival Inkjet Prints in 4 Plexiglas boxes and Letraset on wall"* — grouped under the state's own
classification words: *"Anabolic Steroids (Illegal)"*, *"Animal Corpses (Prohibited)…"*. Also
`https://gagosian.com/exhibitions/2010/taryn-simon-contraband/` (fetch-tool rendering, not raw bytes).
**Correcting our own memo:** `ADJACENCY-58.md` calls Contraband "individuated one-per-frame"; the
exhibited form groups many photographs per frame under a state classification. Simon is **closer to my
predecessor's grid than the memo said**, and further from a room of objects.

**The concession first, where the conductor said it would be hardest.** A single object flagged by a
US federal agency, photographed against a plain ground, shown with the state's own classification
beside it, **is** Contraband's unit and its caption grammar. If this work's epoch-1 object were a
screen showing one object frontally, the honest verdict is *derivative on form*: the only differences
available — hazard not illegality, growing not closed — are of content and clock, and under the
adjacency rule content daylight against a form neighbour is the weakest kind there is. **On a screen,
at one object, I lose this argument.**

**The daylight that holds, and only in the room.** (1) **We make no photograph.** Simon's authorship
is the shooting — five days on site, a consistent ground, a categorical index; we photograph nothing
and index nothing, the only photographs anywhere are the state's own, and what we author is a rule.
(2) **In Simon the state's act is complete; here it is unassigned.** CBP seized; the object never
reached a home and the owner is absent from the frame. Here the state performed nothing — it published
an instruction and **assigned the act to the owner**. Nobody in Contraband is asked to do anything.
(3) **The object is present, and then it is not** — an index of what was taken against a record of
what was obeyed. (4) **The address.** Contraband's caption speaks *about* the object to a viewer; this
work's only sentence speaks *to* the person who owns one. Simon's visitor is a witness; ours is the
addressee.

**Verdict: the daylight is real in the room, thin-to-absent on the screen** — an argument against my
predecessor's whole form, produced by the neighbour it forgot to name.
