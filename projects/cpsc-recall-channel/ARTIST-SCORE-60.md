## (a) THE INSTRUCTION-WORK NEIGHBOURS

*All four were retrieved first-hand by the conductor, 2026-08-02; quotations verbatim from the pages
named. I name no neighbour we hold no source for, except where marked.*

**Yoko Ono, Event Scores / *Grapefruit* (1964).** `[S]` Walker Art Center,
<https://www.walkerart.org/collections/artist/yoko-ono/>: *"Event Scores: brief, poetic instructions for
performances that anyone could enact. Lighting Piece (1955) reads: "Light a match and watch till it goes
out.""* · *"Cut Piece (1964) instructs the performer to sit motionless onstage as audience members take
turns cutting off a piece of his or her clothing."*

**George Brecht, *Water Yam* (1963).** `[S]` The Getty, *The Scores Project*,
<https://www.getty.edu/publications/scores/06/commentary/> — *Drip Music (Drip Event)* entire: *"For
single or multiple performance. A source of dripping water and an empty vessel are arranged so that the
water falls into the vessel. Second version, Dripping. G. Brecht, 1959 to 1962"*. The same page calls an
event score a *"text-based performance instruction"* in *"generic, open-ended language that facilitates
vast possibilities for performance and experience"*, and quotes Brecht that *"Any and every"* realization
is feasible.

**Sol LeWitt, wall drawings and certificates.** `[S]` Tate,
<https://www.tate.org.uk/visit/tate-modern/display/materials-and-objects/sol-lewitt>: *"The instructions
for LeWitt's work function like a composer's score… 'It's as though I were writing a piece of music and
somebody else is going to play it.'"* · *"Often the artworks were made, not by LeWitt himself, but by
assistants following his instructions."* · *"I try to make the plan specific enough that it comes out more
or less as I want it, but general enough that they have the freedom to interpret."*

**Michael Landy, *Break Down* (2001), Artangel — the destruction neighbour, not the instruction one.**

<https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/02/10/like-witnessing-my-own-funeral-michael-landys-possession-pulverising-performance-piece-break-down-turns-20>:
February 2001, the former C&A on Oxford Street, London, two weeks; **7,227** items destroyed, a Saab 900
and a single tea bag among them; **5.75 tonnes** of remains buried in a landfill site in Essex. Landy:
*"It was about me being one of many millions of consumers and how we create our biographies from all the
stuff we own or possess."* (Artangel's own page returned **403** to our fetchers tonight; nothing is

adjacent to my clause 2; we have no page for it and I claim nothing about it.*

### The daylight, structurally — four axes

**1. Whose authorship is the imperative.** Ono, Brecht and LeWitt each **compose an action nobody was
going to perform**: nobody was going to light a match and watch till it goes out; the vessel was not
already under the tap. **My score composes no action. It transcribes an imperative the state has already
issued — 50 of 55 notices carry *stop using … immediately* — to millions of people who mostly do not
perform it.** The imperative is federal. ENSEMBLE authors clause 1, clause 10 and the order of the rest:
the pointing, the address, the requirement that a witness exist — **21 words of 131**, and I will not
inflate it.

**2. What the performer does that a performer of *Drip Music* does not.** *Drip Music* arranges two
objects: nothing is owned, nothing permanently changes state, nothing follows. My performer must **own**
the object, **destroy their own property**, and act as the **addressee of an instruction already binding
on them before they read my sheet** — the score creates no obligation, it makes a person notice they
stand inside one. *Cut Piece* is nearest, clothing destroyed on the body; but there the authority is the
artist's invitation and the audience does the cutting.

**3. Where the money enters — the clause none of them has.** No instruction of Ono's, Brecht's or
LeWitt's pays the performer, let alone by the party the work implicates. Clause 7 does: proof of
destruction goes **to the firm whose product it was**, and the performer takes a refund from it. **39 of
55** remedies promise money back; **55 of 55** retailer lines print a price, so the sum is already in the
state's document and we author no figure; **15–24 of 55** require destruction *and* photograph *and* pay
for it. Landy inverts it exactly: 7,227 things **he owned and had paid for**, 5.75 tonnes buried at his
own cost — **money flows out of Landy and into my performer.** And Landy authored his inventory; my
performer's is authored by a federal commission on a Thursday.

**4. What the performance leaves.** LeWitt's score produces a wall drawing: a thing on an institution's
wall, made by assistants, governed by a certificate. Mine produces **an absence and a receipt** — the
object is gone, and what exists afterwards is a photograph in a company's inbox and a credit on a bank
statement, **held by the implicated party**, not by the artist and not by a museum. None of the four has
that property; it is the sharpest thing I have.

### Where I am weaker than each of them — the part that counts

- **Weaker than Brecht and Ono, fatally if it holds.** Their language is *"generic, open-ended"*, *"Any
  and every"* realization feasible. **Mine is closed**: clause 5 says *exactly what the remedy says*, and
  the remedy is someone else's fixed text. If the contribution of instruction art is the space an
  instruction leaves, this is its inverse — **a compliance form with a title.** True at the level of form,
  and my answer (the closure is the subject) is what an artist says when the form has beaten them.
- **Weaker than LeWitt**, who authored *both* poles — *specific enough… general enough that they have the
  freedom to interpret.* I author neither: the specificity is the state's, the freedom is zero. His
  certificates make the work ownable, transferable, re-executable; mine is none of those.
- **Weaker than Landy.** *Break Down* was public, bodied, two weeks, 7,227 items, a crowd on Oxford
  Street. Mine is one person alone in a kitchen with scissors and nothing to watch. Landy destroyed things
  that were **not** dangerous — total, voluntary loss; my performer destroys a thing they've been told is
  hazardous and **is paid back**, which is indistinguishable from ordinary good citizenship. *"Your
  performance is just doing what the notice said"* is the takedown; my refutation — that the
  indistinguishability **is** the finding — is also how this work fails if it fails.
- **Weaker than all four, unarguably.** *Cut Piece* was performed, wall drawings are on walls, 5.75 tonnes
  are in Essex. **Mine has been performed zero times, including by us.**

### The send-back question, decided

**The face names one send-back only — the one the notice already names — and nothing comes to us.**
**(i) Performability:** a score is a work if someone who is not us can perform it holding nothing but the
score; a clause routing anything to ENSEMBLE makes completion depend on reaching us, which is apparatus in
a score's clothes. **(ii) We cannot keep the promise:** this house cannot receive physical objects, Frank
has been asked and has not answered, and a send-back clause is a second room we cannot build. **(iii) An
inbox of performances is a list of firms** — the aggregate the work is forbidden to print, rebuilt in our
archive. **(iv) The finding is that the instruction has exactly one addressee**; adding ours puts a second
in the work. **Cost, accepted:** we hold no evidence it was ever performed, which for a house whose law is
honesty by labeling is severe; clause 10 (*show what you kept to one person who was not there*) is the
minimum answer, social without being ours. If Frank answers, a **voluntary** channel can be noted below
the rule — never as a clause.

## (b) K6, RE-ARGUED AGAINST THE SCORE

*Ruled `FAILED — stale`: the old answer was argued for a grid that no longer exists.*

**Spielraum / Headroom** (`https://frankbueltge.de/headroom`; live per this house's decision of
2026-07-31, 301 → `/headroom` → 200) — an **instrument**: a dial per hyperscaler, reported PUE against a
computed breakeven. **Daylight.** *Locus:* its work happens on a server we control and every number on it
is **ours**, arithmetic on somebody's annual report; the score computes nothing, prints no count, and its
work happens **in other people's homes, where we cannot see it and keep no list.** *State:* an instrument
must be readable at once, and Headroom's whole state is available in one moment by design; a score's state
is the set of its performances — unbounded, held by nobody. *Clock:* Headroom ticks once a year with an
annual report; the score has no tick, only a person noticing what they own. **Weaker:** Headroom is
finished, live and read. An instrument nobody reads still exists; a score nobody performs arguably does
not.

**Diminishing Returns** (`projects/diminishing-returns/`) — closed corpus of four companies' disclosures,
"you cannot win" computed live from `data.json`, an ending the visitor reaches. **Daylight.** *Who bears
the cost:* there the loss is dramatized — a dial that will not close, a game authored unwinnable, the
honesty panel naming the dramatization as a choice. Here **the loss is real and the performer's**: an
object stops existing at their hand, and the sum they are paid is printed by the state (55/55), not by us.
*Ending:* DR's is written by us; the score's is a destruction at which we are not present. *Corpus:* DR's
is closed; the score's cannot be enumerated. **Weaker:** DR's mechanic is inspectable — a stranger can
falsify "you cannot win" by reading `data.json`. The score's central claim, that a person will perform it,
**cannot be falsified by inspection at all.** DR could be judged the night it was built; this cannot.

**One Tap** (`works/2026-07-23-one-tap/`, premiered 2026-07-23, **killed 2026-07-25** after three stagings
this house's gate had passed were rejected by the human eye: badly staged, and not art (Frank,
2026-07-21, wording private)).
**Daylight.** One Tap was a **single-moment argument built from figures on one screen**, its apparatus —
panel, self-test, needle — visible as the staging; it died because the instrument *was* the work. The
score has no panel, no self-test, no dial, no figure of ours, nothing to operate. And it takes One Tap's
harder lesson literally — *passing this house's gate is not evidence a thing is art* — by being the first
proposal here whose only possible evidence is **an act by someone who is not us**; a gate cannot pass it
into existence. **Weaker, and sharpest of the three:** One Tap at least existed as a thing a stranger
could open and be unmoved by. A page of instructions is *easier* to call not-art, and this house's own
indictment — three concept phases, a quarter of a megabyte, no unit — is an indictment of **documents**.
**Unperformed, the score is One Tap with fewer pixels and a better excuse.**

## (c) THE ROOM: THE AGENCY ORGAN, NAMED

**Binding condition 3. I keep the room and name the organ — a pair, both already in the material, nothing
composed by us.**

> **What carries *the owner did this, on instruction, and was paid* is the pair: the destruction
> photograph the notice itself required that owner to make and send, lying beside the piece it is a
> photograph of; and printed beside it the notice's own retail line — the sum that owner paid and the sum
> that owner got back — which the state prints on 55 of 55 notices and which we therefore never author. A
> photograph alone shows damage; a sum alone shows a price; together they are a **transaction with a
> private person on one side of it**, and a transaction has an actor. The organ is the receipt, and its
> second half being the state's own price line rather than a figure of ours is my one improvement on the
> staging voice's repair: every word beside every piece is then either the state's text or the owner's own
> hand.**

**Two things against my own organ, both new tonight.** *(i)* It is **untested**, and the staging voice was
right to call it the thing it is least sure of. Its pre-registration binds me: severed readers, (A)
residue alone against (B) residue with receipts, coded OWNER / OTHER-ACTOR / NO-ACTOR, claim **(B) ≥ 4 of
5 and (A) ≤ 1 of 5**. **If (B) does not beat (A) by three readers, the receipt is not the organ, and I drop
the room and keep only the score.** I adopt that consequence as mine. *(ii)* **The organ does not cover the
corpus.** Only **15–24 of 55** remedies produce all three of destruction, photograph and payment. A floor
built strictly by `THE-RULE.md` is a floor where **most pieces have no receipt beside them** — present on
a minority, absent on the rest: a scatter, not a mechanism. `THE-RULE.md` §8 names the only mechanical fix

instruction instead of the instruction. **The room is doubly conditional: on the test, and on an amendment

## (d) WHAT I CUT, AND WHAT I DO NOT KNOW

**Cut, with reasons.**

1. **"This work is a room, and the screen is a rehearsal of it"** (`ARTIST-REFORM-59.md` §5). The work is
   the score; the room is one performance of it at institutional scale, conditional on §(c). The sentence
   made the work depend on a realiser this house does not have.
2. **M2 entirely** — *the instruction we obey destroys the brand.* Not repaired, deleted: **C6 = 0/55**.
3. **M3 as a "mechanism."** An editorial promise becomes a construction fact: the face carries no count,
   list, country, firm or index, so there is nothing on it to un-promise.
4. **Four clauses off the face**, as ruled. The admission rule moves to `THE-RULE.md`, where it is
   auditable; the hole clause survives only as `THE-RULE.md` §5 (a held window issues nothing, the standing
   object stands); the no-index clause is gone because the face has no index to forbid. **The removal
   clause I cut outright:** D7 was ruled FAILED and it is right — a destroyed object cannot be un-destroyed
   and a takedown clause addressed to a performer is unperformable. What can be withdrawn is *our
   publication of a record*, never anyone's performance.
5. **The acquisition clause off the face.** The performer uses what they already own, which removes the
   "cannot lawfully be bought" problem from the score. It remains undiminished for the room.
6. **§2.5's bargain** (*stays in Season Two only if a room is committed*) — the score leaves Season Two on
   the staging ruling; a departure, not a term I negotiated.
7. **The bare figures 27/55, 13/55, 17, "two firm strings quoted."** Replaced by intervals from my own
   script per the Verifier's binding corrections; where our codings differ, both print.
8. **Any remaining comfort in the Simon daylight's limb 1.** The score does not merely permit a
   photograph, it **requires** one — made by the performer, of their own destroyed property, sent to the
   firm. We still make none, but that daylight is thinner tonight than last night.

**What I do not know, and nobody can settle tonight.**

- **Whether anyone will ever perform it.** Zero performances as of filing, including by us. On the staging
  voice's own gate question — *is a published instruction nobody performs an instance or an illustration?*
  — this is the work's whole exposure, and I have an argument, not evidence.
- **Whether the receipt is the agency organ.** Tonight's test decides it; I have pre-committed to dropping
  the room if it fails.
- **Whether a private person may lawfully give a recalled object to a work, and whether the work may
  execute the remedy and pass the refund back.** CPSC's words on **selling** are verified `[S]`; the page
  says nothing about donation or non-commercial transfer. Unretrieved, no legal opinion, and it must reach
  Frank as publisher before any object moves.
- **Whether the score's closure disqualifies it as an instruction work** (§(a)). A judgment, not a fact.
- **Whether clause 10 is a performance or a decoration.** The only witness the work has, and untested.
- **Whether my codings or the Verifier's are right.** Both published and re-runnable; I report unions
  rather than pick. Only C1 (50/55) and C6 (0/55) are coding-insensitive — and both claims that survive
  tonight rest on those two.
- **Whether the corpus generalises.** One month, 55 records, five publication dates; the channel's
