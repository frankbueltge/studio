## Findings nobody asked about

- Wrong office code is a silent-empty trap on **every** `/products/types/ZFP/locations/{code}` call, confirmed for two different wrong codes (`PHX`, `SEA`), both HTTP 200. Any office-keyed loop needs an explicit empty-`@graph` check, not a truthy-response check.
- The `/products/types/ZFP/locations` registry (126) is not a clean list of issuing offices — it is a list of *forecast-area subdivisions*, several of which share one physical issuing office. Any count sourced from it should say "location codes," not "offices."
- Reissue traffic is bursty enough (1–59 per 10 min) that a single before/after snapshot claim about refresh cadence is fragile; only a multi-hour binned series supports it.
- No rate-limit or 4xx/5xx encountered on `api.weather.gov` across ~90 requests this session; `aviationweather.gov` also clean.

## Bottom line
Claims 1, 2, and 4 do not survive as stated — 1 conflates a 126-entry location registry with the 123 offices actually in the live index; 2 is built on a small, biased office sample that a 47×-spread, partly-zero distribution cannot support linearly; 4's "about an hour" is 2–3× my measured national median. Claims 3 and 5 hold in substance though not in exact point figures — 3 because the underlying process is genuinely bursty, 5 because the pattern (zero numbers) reproduces on a fresh bulletin. Claim 6 is confirmed and sharper than assumed: the rounding lives in `aviationweather.gov`'s `reportTime`, not in the primary `api.weather.gov` feed. Nothing found here should stop the build by itself, but the office count (123 vs 126) and the lag figure ("about an hour" vs ~20 minutes measured) are wrong enough, and cheap enough to re-check, that they should not ship unchanged.
