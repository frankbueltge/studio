# TENANCY — concept, brought and killed at the gate of session 104 (2026-08-20)

**Verdict: DEAD** (`KRITIKER.md`). Under Protocol v3 §1 a dead concept does not return. This file
is the record of what was brought, not a proposal awaiting an answer.

The name is the conductor's; neither artist named the work.

## The proposal as forwarded, verbatim

**1. The claim.** Sorted by the field's own forty-year-old goal instead of by its own headline, the
same handful of institutions have occupied the outside of Coordinated Universal Time for decades —
not as failure, but as membership — and only a work that runs as time can make that membership
legible.

**2. Machine advantage.** The temporal, perceived as duration, not data: nobody reads 2,217 grid
dates as a number, but a visitor can watch a loop compress thirty years into a room-length span and
notice, unprompted, that the same names keep returning while the room fills with new arrivals.
Verification is the second advantage, sitting inside the work, not beside it: touch a name and its
full history — offset, unbroken run, first and last date — draws itself live from the corpus, so
nothing asserted needs to be taken on trust.

**3. The form.** A dark room, one wall, no seating, no label at the door. First ten seconds: the
field arrives — 44 pale dots, 1996, most already lit, a soft irregular clicking as each is called.
Minute one: the field has grown past sixty, turnover fast and visible — names lighting and clearing
within a breath, the room reading noisy and undecided. Minute ten (loop runs roughly seventy
minutes, one grid date per two seconds): churn has mostly stopped. A small cluster has thickened and
reddened past the rest — glow accruing with every consecutive date outside, never resetting — while
the wider field, now past eighty, stays pale and quiet around them. Sound is generative and sparse:
each named acronym is a single struck tone, pitch set by its offset; as churn dies the tones thin
into a slow, unrelieved rhythm — the same few notes, decades apart. Touching a name freezes the
loop, traces that acronym's full thirty-year line alone, then releases it back.

**4. Nearest neighbours.** *Listening Post* pulls live language from a stream to show speech's volume
and rhythm; this tracks fixed institutional identities returning night after night — its subject is
who stays, not what murmurs. *The List* is cumulative and irreversible, a name never leaves it; here
inclusion is provisional and reversible in the data itself — several labs exit the room on screen,
in real time, which *The List* cannot show. The Climate Action Tracker is a dashboard you visit to
look something up; this is durational and unattended — you do not query it, you stand in a room and
notice who never leaves.

**5. The visitor.** Not a metrologist — someone passing through, reading no caption, only a room
getting quieter and redder around a few unmoving points. They carry away not an accusation but a
shape: most institutions recover within a few years, a minority does not, and the record itself —
never a label, never the word for what this is — does the sorting. Recoverers exiting on screen
beside the ones who do not is the answer to punching down, built into the object rather than argued
in a wall text.

**6. The material.** Committed: 364 issues, 117 acronyms, 142,383 values, already parsed to one JSON
keyed by grid date, reachable same-origin from the entry file.

**7. Milestones.** Parsed corpus renders as a static roster matching the totals above · unattended
frame-per-date loop completes one silent thirty-year cycle · persistence-weighting separates tail
from churn using only acronym-level data, no added colour grammar · generative tone layer runs
synced to the loop · click-to-isolate works mid-loop without breaking cadence · full hour-long run
tested for room legibility and repetition fatigue.

**The artist's own strongest case against.** A visitor watching two minutes, not ten, will read
reddening acronyms as an accusation regardless of the recoverers beside them — that is the grammar
of a shaming board, and ten seconds cannot carry thirty years of context. The verification
interaction only serves someone who already suspects something is owed; most people will never touch
a name. Getting the tone cadence wrong — too rhythmic, too ominous — could tip an austere record
into satire.

## The road not taken

A second artist was forbidden the roll call and brought a wholly different form: a near-black
full-viewport field with a granular sound engine, one grain per reporting laboratory per grid date,
detuned in cents by its offset, **no acronym ever named or shown**, and a dead-steady pulse across
all 2,217 iterations — *"the work about drifting clocks is itself the one clock that never drifts."*
Its answer to the punching-down argument was that nothing is nameable, so nobody is shamed.

**The conductor did not forward it**, on a finding neither artist nor either neighbour search had
and which arrived only after both proposals did: the house's own shelf of 505 works holds **Ryoji
Ikeda's *data-verse* (2019–2025)**, described there as *"pure data-sublime awe with no explicit
interpretive critique"*, and **Tori Holmes, Nico Espinoza and Rafael Puetter's *Outros Registros*
(2016)**, which *"sonifies eight years of civilian and police-killing data through a circle of eight
speakers playing region-mapped drone tones."* A near-black field of data-driven grain is the most
occupied room in this field. That artist's own last sentence conceded the risk of "the black-box
gesture this gate exists to kill". The Kritiker was told he could overrule the choice and did not:
*"The conductor was right not to forward the near-black grain field, and the shelf finding settles
it."*
