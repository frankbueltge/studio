## 1. The experience, in one page

You arrive. One sentence, one number, nothing else:

> **On 21 November 2024, the United States published this figure. Of the people it was still
> holding — its own heading reads *Single Adults with a Positive Fear Determination Still in
> Custody* — the average time in custody, at the end of August 2024, was 274.55 days.**
>
> *Remember it.*

*Corrected on the Kritiker's condition 6 — this passage first read "people who had passed a
credible-fear screening", which is wider than the register's row, and "had by then been held", which
reads a stock average over people **still** in custody as a life someone finished serving. Nobody
spent 274.55 days; it is an average over those not yet released, and the face must say so in its own
first minute. The gamified imperative* **Remember it** *is flagged by the same memo as one register
away from a memory game, and survives this session unresolved — Étude 02's problem.*

You press on. The same question is asked again — the same population, the same finished month,
August 2024 — and the register answers:

> **80.37 days.**

Then the two curves are drawn on one axis: what the register said in November 2024, rising from
70 days to 274; what it has said since 3 January 2025, flat between 74 and 83. Twenty-two months of
the first curve were set to exactly zero for three editions in December 2024, and came back as the
second. Every point carries the date it was published and the sha256 of the file it came from.

Then the register's own sentence, which is its entire public statement on the matter — carried on
the `Header` sheet of the workbook, byte-identical in all 26 editions:

> *"ICE confirms the integrity of the data as published on this site and cannot attest to subsequent
> transmissions.  Data fluctuate until “locked” at the conclusion of the fiscal year."*

And then the apparatus opens: **pick any month the register has ever described, and see every answer
it has ever given about that month**, across 26 editions. Not the current state — the disagreement
between states. The current state is what the register already shows you; this work shows the part
it discards.

**The second movement, and the reason this form and not another.** The work remembers what *you*
saw. It watches the register on nights you are not there. When you come back — days or weeks later —
it does not greet you with the newest state. It says: **when you were last here, this work told you
X; it now tells you Y** — and puts you, personally, in the position of everyone who read the
register on 21 November 2024 and never learned that their number had been replaced. The subject is
"what you saw is not what is there now." The form is a visitor finding that out about their own
visit. **The form is the subject, not an illustration of it.**

## 2. The line, and the machine's advantage

**Digital.** A browser work; committed data; offline runtime under the site CSP; no external
request. Within remit.

**Which limb of the machine's advantage, and it is three of the four.** *Scale* — 26 editions of a
ten-sheet workbook is more state than a person holds in their head, and the phenomenon is invisible
in any one of them. *Repetition* — the watch does not tire and does not skip the boring edition,
which is exactly the one that changes. *The temporal* — a work whose material only exists because
something was kept on nights nobody was looking, and whose second movement is the visitor's own
return across days. *Verification* is the limb this work does **not** get to claim: nothing here
checks the register against the world, only against its earlier self.

> **STRUCK AND REPLACED, session 98, on the Kritiker's condition 5 — the dossier stated a premise it
> had not checked.** This paragraph read *"the register publishes roughly every two weeks and will
> keep publishing."* **It does not.** ICE has published no Detention Management report since
> **9 April 2026** — four months — and at least three lawsuits have been filed to compel it, one of
> them by the **Deportation Data Project**, this dossier's own named reserve receiver, now a
> plaintiff. ICE attributes the gap to government shutdowns; reporting notes the government had
> reopened while the gap continued. Sources:
> [The Marshall Project, 15 July 2026](https://www.themarshallproject.org/2026/07/15/ice-data-immigration-detention-transparency) ·
> [WLRN, 21 July 2026](https://www.wlrn.org/immigration/2026-07-21/amid-increased-scrutiny-ice-detention-and-deportation-data-goes-dark).
> **The corpus ends 25 September 2025 and this dossier had not said so either.**
>
> **This is not a footnote against the concept; it is the concept's hardest question, and it is now
> on the table.** A work whose second movement is *"we watched on the nights you were away"* has, at
> this moment, nothing to watch. Either the silence is itself the material — the last answer the
> register ever gave about fiscal 2024 stands frozen at the restated value, and there is no longer
> any way to ask it again — or the form is dead. **Étude 02 decides it; condition 8 kills the
> concept if the answer is inert.**

**The test both constraints serve.** A stranger who knows nothing about this house sees a number
they were asked to remember replaced under them. No single pair of hands kept 26 editions of a
spreadsheet for ten months on the chance that one row would move.

## 3. The subject, and why this keeper

The Kritiker's closing instruction at the premiere of STILL DARK: *point this same machinery at a
register whose keeper would rather it were not measured.* This keeper detains people, publishes the
count because Congress requires it, and states in the file itself that the data fluctuate — while
keeping no record of the fluctuation. **The material bar is not argued here; it is the number of
days a human being spent locked up.**

## 4. The finding, and its limits

Étude 01 (`etudes/restated/ETUDE-01.md`) establishes, cell by cell and reproducible from hashes,
that a published figure of **274.55 days became 80.37 days** for the same finished month, through
three editions of exact zeros, with no notice and no changelog.

**Three things are refused, and the refusals are on the work's face, not in a method sheet:**

1. **We do not claim the old numbers were right.** A monotonic climb to 274 days has the shape of a
   computation that was itself wrong. The restatement may well be a correction.
2. **We allege no concealment.** No intent is knowable from these files and none is asserted.
3. **We claim no rate.** The étude's two attempts at one (38.1 %, then 40.5 %) are both recorded as
   unpublishable, and why.

**What survives all three is the whole work:** a correction and a quiet rewrite are *indistinguishable
from outside*, because the register keeps no record of either. That indistinguishability is the
finding, and it is not a finding about one agency — it is a property of every register that
publishes a current state and no history.

## 5. Nearest neighbours and daylight

Full ledger with URLs: `NEIGHBOURS.md`. The short version, and it is unfavourable before it is
favourable:

**Occupied ground, named:** diffing an official source and publishing the delta is a solved utility
with a decade of prior art — NewsDiffs, diffengine, CongressEdits, EDGI's federal web tracker,
Sunlight's Gov404, Politwoops, TOSBack, changedetection.io. **"We notice when an official source
changes" is not a finding, not a risk and not a form.** Art-side, *History Flow* (Viégas &
Wattenberg, 2003) already made a canonical record's whole edit history the visual form, and *The
Deleted City* (Vijgen, 2011) already rebuilt a dead public record as a navigable ruin.

**The daylight, stated as two claims that can be checked:**

1. **Not page-diffing — statement-diffing.** Every tool above answers *did this page change?* This
   work answers *did the answer to a fixed question about a finished month change?* A page-diff of
   these workbooks reports a change at every edition, because every edition adds a month; it cannot
   distinguish the register growing from the register rewriting. That distinction is the work.
2. **The visitor's own return as the form.** Across the house's atlas of 505 neighbouring works,
   queried tonight, **nothing makes a visitor's return its form**, and nothing measures a register's
   revisions to itself. A scout's web sweep found no verified precedent for a browser work that
   stores what *this* visitor saw and shows them personally what changed. **That scout's own
   qualification travels unsoftened: this is a "we did not find it", not a "it does not exist."**
   Further search is owed — Rhizome's Net Art Anthology, ELMCIP, the Ars Electronica archive —
   before any gate relies on it.

**The Grundform rule.** This house's works have converged on the screen apparatus with a visible
self-test, and increment 1 would reach for it again. The argument for THIS subject needing THAT form
is in §1 and rests entirely on the second movement: strip the return visit out and what remains is a
spreadsheet viewer with a good anecdote. **If the concept gate finds the return visit unbuildable or
inert, the honest consequence is that this concept has no form yet** — not that it ships the
apparatus alone.

## 6. The takedown, and how the built work refutes it

**The sentence a serious critic would publish:**

> *A studio downloaded twenty-six spreadsheets, discovered that a government had corrected an
> error, and dressed the correction up as a disappearance.*

**The refutation, by construction.** The work concedes the premise on its own face — it says, in the
first minute and not in a footnote, that the old numbers may have been wrong and that no concealment
is alleged. Having conceded it, the work is still standing, because it never asked whether the
correction was right. It asks whether it is **findable**. The built refutation is the thing the
keeper does not have and this work does: a record of what the register said before. If a critic can
retrieve, from the keeper, the fact that 274.55 was ever published — the work is refuted and dies. It
is built precisely on the wager that they cannot.

**Which of the takedown law's three does it offer?** **(c)** a form only this machinery can produce
is the floor and is argued in §2. **(a)** a finding of its own — the restatement, which no source
found in tonight's sweep has published. **(b)** real risk — the subject is a federal agency's
account of how long it holds people.
