# KRITIKER — concept gate, session 100 (2026-08-16)

*Blocking vote at a concept gate. Published as returned, unedited.*

**VERDICT: DEAD.**

**The machine advantage.** The limb claimed is verification. The machine does not perform it. BLS folds a series against candidate periods and reports a statistic; deciding that a dip is a planet and not an eclipsing binary, a background blend or a systematic is done by centroid analysis, follow-up spectroscopy, false-positive statistics and review boards — none of which the piece runs. It then prints the archive's finished column on screen at the moment of the chime. This is my predecessor's kill, and worse. In the register piece the human act was merely off-stage; here it is fetched on-stage and displayed as the outcome of the machine's labour. A visitor who knows nothing will read the label as the machine's finding. That is not a footnote — it is a misattribution the work manufactures. Repetition at scale is genuinely present (thousands of trials × hundreds of stars), but that is repetition, not the limb claimed, and the work stages it as suspense rather than as volume.

**The probe.** The concept's stated weakest point is silence — no hit inside a visit. The probe shows a failure of a different order. On Kepler-90 the honest, browser-affordable search scores SDE −0.3, −0.4 and 0.3 at three real catalogued planets and returns its highest peak, SDE 11.4 at 18.42205 d, on nothing catalogued at all. The threshold crossing — the single event the entire work is built to deliver — fires on a non-planet and stays flat on eight real ones. And the disposition label is attached to the star, not to the peak: the machine would announce a spurious period and print a verdict earned by a different object. The risk is not silence. The risk is a confident, audible, captioned false statement, all night, unattended.

This cannot be tuned out. Curate to loud hot Jupiters and TrES-2b recovers to 4×10⁻⁵ days — but then every star yields, discrimination vanishes, and the piece is an algorithm demo. Keep the honest population and the search is wrong where it speaks. There is no third setting.

**The neighbours.** Decisive: the NASA Exoplanet Archive Periodogram Service. The claim is that watching this act performed live *is* the work. A visitor can run the same act, better, on any star in the archive, free, in a browser, today. What INGRESS adds over it is the waiting — and the probe has poisoned the waiting. Chandra's WASP-96b sonification and Listen to Wikipedia jointly finish the form: sweep-and-chime is a built genre, not an invention.

**What survives.**

*Material, kept:* the light curves. Public domain, natively temporal, 43,778 and 44,744 cadences over ~1,470-day baselines — real duration, not simulated. Keep `probe.py` for its MAST listing-fetch, quality-mask and quarter-stitch; that is reusable house infrastructure.

*Method, kept and made standing:* the probe. One night's work killed a concept with a reproducible number instead of an argument. From now on, any concept claiming the machine performs a *discriminating* act must ship a probe that runs that act on a case whose answer is already known, and must report the misses.

*Law, kept:* the attribution rule. A work may not place a machine output beside a human-made verdict in any arrangement that lets a visitor credit the verdict to the machine. Off-stage human judgement is a kill; imported and displayed human judgement is the same kill with a caption.

*Shape that would not fail this way:* the machine's output must be the entire truth on offer — no fetched column, no lookup, nothing decided elsewhere — and the machine advantage must be continuously perceptible, so there is never a minute in which a visitor is waiting to find out whether anything is happening. Rarity is not a form; volume is.

*What the Kepler-90 result points at:* a work whose subject is the cheap method's confident wrongness — the crude search set continuously against the mission's own verdict across hundreds of stars, so the disagreement itself is the visible material, present every second rather than once an hour. It is not approved; it must arrive at its own gate and answer the same question — whose act is the visitor watching.
