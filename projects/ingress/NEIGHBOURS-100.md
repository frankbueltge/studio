# NEIGHBOUR SEARCH — INGRESS

*Ephemeral voice, briefed adversarially against the proposal: its office was to make the work
unnecessary by showing the world already has it. Published as returned, unedited.*

**Verdict:** Partly made — every component INGRESS assembles already exists, publicly, several of
them free and more rigorous than what's proposed; the assembly itself is the only unverified thing.

## The neighbours (ranked by danger)

**1. NASA Exoplanet Archive Periodogram Service** — Caltech/IPAC, ongoing,
https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/transit.html and the live tool at
https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/cgi-bin/Pgram/nph-pgram. This is a public, free,
browser-based tool that runs Box-Least-Squares, Lomb-Scargle, or Plavchan on real archived light
curves — or on data you upload — and returns the periodogram and the phase-folded curve. It is the
exact operation INGRESS performs, done at the source, on the full archive, with more algorithms,
for anyone, right now. It makes the piece's central claim — "watch the search performed, don't just
read the result" — already ordinary. **Fatal-adjacent.**

**2. Planet Hunters / Zooniverse** — Oxford/Yale, since 2010,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_Hunters. The public has been examining real Kepler light curve
segments for dips since 2010, at scale, and found real planets (PH1 b) doing it. Human-in-the-loop
verification of transits by a lay visitor is a solved, decade-old public genre.

**3. SYSTEM Sounds / Matt Russo** — ongoing, https://www.system-sounds.com/work/,
https://www.astromattrusso.com/sonification. Sci-art studio sonifying real orbital and transit data
(TRAPPIST-1 and others), performed live in planetarium shows including the blind/low-vision-accessible
"Our Musical Universe" at the Dunlap Institute, 2018–2019. Durational, audience-facing, real data,
professionally produced.

**4. NASA/Chandra "A Universe of Sound," WASP-96 b** — https://chandra.si.edu/sound/wasp96b.html. A
vertical line sweeps across a real transmission-spectrum graph while data points sound as pitched
tones — the identical "trace moves, event sounds" grammar INGRESS proposes, already built, polished,
and distributed by a NASA-adjacent institution.

**5. Sonifigrapher** — ICAD 2019 paper, Riber et al.,
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334856051 — an instrument built specifically to sonify
light curves pulled from NASA's exoplanet archive, real data, peer-published.

**6. PH1 light-curve sonification** — amateur, 2012,
http://keplerlightcurves.blogspot.com/2012/10/the-sound-of-ph1s-light-curve.html. An actual Kepler
light curve turned to sound by a citizen scientist: background variability as "cricket-like"
texture, the real transit as distinct "tweets." The "mostly noise-texture, occasionally a
signal-sound" structure INGRESS wants to stage was already produced, with real Kepler data, fourteen
years ago.

**7. Listen to Wikipedia** — LaPorte & Hashemi, 2013, https://listen.hatnote.com/. Confirmed live:
unattended, continuous, scrolling/bubble visual, chime gated strictly to real events (never to a raw
stream), running still. This is the genre INGRESS sits inside, not beside.

**8. Exoplanet Watch** — NASA, https://science.nasa.gov/citizen-science/exoplanet-watch/. Public,
real-time transit follow-up with real telescopes and real light curves, ongoing.

## The disposition problem

Yes, this is close to fatal for the "verification" claim. CONFIRMED/CANDIDATE/FALSE POSITIVE is the
NASA Exoplanet Archive's own vetted disposition column — reached through centroid analysis,
follow-up spectroscopy, false-positive-probability statistics, and human review boards, none of
which the piece performs. INGRESS runs a client-side BLS against an arbitrary threshold, then prints
an answer that was adjudicated entirely offstage by a different, heavier process. The visitor
doesn't watch verification; they watch a toy statistic followed by a lookup into someone else's
finished verdict. The piece's own claim overstates what its algorithm does relative to what its
label represents.

## Screensaver test

Real precedent for the exact visual language exists: FlowingData's "data visualization wallpaper"
(2020, https://flowingdata.com/2020/06/15/data-visualization-wallpaper/) and the open-source
`datavisscrsvr` project (https://github.com/vis4/datavisscrsvr) are literally screensavers built
from scrolling/filling data displays with occasional highlights. INGRESS's language is not
distinguishable from this category by anything intrinsic to it — only by wall placement and
curation.

## Strongest argument against building it

Every load-bearing part already exists in public, working form: the algorithm (NASA's own
Periodogram tool, on the full archive, live), the sound grammar (Listen to Wikipedia, Chandra's
swept-line sonification), the data-as-texture aesthetic (2012 amateur PH1 sonification), and the
disposition labels (pulled wholesale from an archive column, not earned on-screen). INGRESS is a
re-skin, not a new instrument.

## Narrowest daylight

Nothing found combines all of: hundreds of *shipped, static* Kepler files (not a live API call), BLS
run client-side through an entire unattended gallery night, date-seeded star order, and a running
tally — as a single durational museum piece whose subject is the sheer, overwhelming volume of
negative results. That specific durational-negative-result framing is real and unclaimed in what
this search turned up — but it rests on primitives that are all, individually, already public and
free.
