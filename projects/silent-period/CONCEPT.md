# SILENT PERIOD — concept

*Brought to the gate collective session 105, 2026-08-21. Protocol v3 §1: one session, verdict
BUILD or DEAD, in writing, the same evening.*

Material dossier: `memory/dossiers/forecast-vocabulary.md`. Instruments: `tools/zfp_harvest.py`,
`tools/zfp_settle.py`.

## 1. The stake, before any form

A stranger sees a sentence about tomorrow's sky — a number, a word, or **nothing at all** — and
then the sky's own record answers it in the same frame. Most of the time the answer agrees. When
the sentence said nothing, the answer is sometimes **RAINED ANYWAY**, and nothing in the frame can
argue back.

Nobody needs to be told what is at stake. Everyone has been told "a slight chance" and everyone has
been rained on. What a visitor cannot do, and the machine can, is *check* — because a probability is
unfalsifiable on any one day, and only repetition settles it.

No institution is named anywhere in the work. There is nobody to misread as guilty: the subject is a
vocabulary, and the vocabulary belongs to everyone who has ever read a forecast.

## 2. The claim, in one sentence

The public vocabulary of weather risk has no word for zero and almost none for certainty, so its
only way of saying "no rain" is to say nothing — and its silence is wrong one time in sixteen.

*Correction made by the conductor before the gate sat, and it changes what the work must show:* the
one-in-sixteen is a pooled average and cannot travel alone. Silence is wrong 1.0 % of the time in
Phoenix and 10.1 % in New York; and it is wrong **2.4 %** of the time for the same day against
**13.9 %** seven days out. Near silence is fairly reliable, far silence is not, and the record marks
the two identically. A work that shows only the pooled figure states something a hostile reader
dismantles in one query.

## 3. The machine advantage

**Verification**, with **the temporal** carrying it. A person reading a forecast never learns whether
its promise held; they are gone before the sky answers, and one day's answer proves nothing anyway.

How a visitor perceives it: the work is not a chart of a finished tally but a settlement happening
now, one claim at a time, at a rate no person could sustain and with no claim skipped. Three running
ledgers — numbers, words, silence — are built in front of the visitor out of nothing but claims that
have already been checked. At minute one they are noise. At minute twenty they have separated. That
separation is the machine's work made visible: it cannot be seen in one frame and it cannot be
photographed.

## 4. The form, and why this form

A single dark full-viewport frame, unattended, no interaction required.

**Centre:** one forecast unit, drawn from the settled corpus and never invented — a number
("40 percent"), a word ("likely"), or a true blank. A beat. Then the sky's answer resolves beside
it: RAINED / DID NOT RAIN. The claim recolours: what held stays steady, what broke fractures.

**Below:** three thin horizontal ledgers, one per class of claim, each extending in real time with
its own live rate. Not decoration — the running proof of what that class of language is worth.

*First ten seconds:* a piece of ordinary weather-speak is put on trial and answered.
*Minute two:* the three ledgers are visibly different objects, drifting apart.
*Minute twenty:* two decades have cycled through and the shape is unmistakable — numbers hugging
their promise, words running hot, silence failing at a steady clip.

Why not another form: the finding is about a *language*, and a language cannot be shown as a still
image without becoming a table. The claim exists only as a trajectory, which is what a time-based
work is for.

**The alternative not forwarded.** A second Artist proposed a typographic field, words and blanks
sized at their true frequency, opened by touch into verified marks, with an idle self-probing state.
It answered the stake well and its idle behaviour was strong. The conductor did not forward it for
one reason, disclosed here so the gate can overrule: **a field of words sized by frequency is a word
cloud**, the most exhausted form in this territory, and an unattended room means most visitors never
touch anything. The proposal is in `ARTIST-B.md` and the gate may take it instead.

## 5. Neighbours, and the daylight

Searched adversarially before either Artist was briefed; full report in `NEIGHBOURS.md`.

- **Stan's Cafe, *Of All the People in All the World*** (2003–) — the closest structural neighbour:
  accumulation as argument. Daylight: the rice show accumulates uniform marks toward a *known total*.
  Here there is no total and no uniform mark; the unit is a category of language on trial, and what
  accumulates is a comparative rate that the next claim can move.
- **James Bridle, *Cloud Index*** (2016–17) — generates speculative forecasts. Daylight: this work
  generates nothing. Every sentence it shows was published, and every verdict was observed.
- **Ryoji Ikeda, *data-verse*** — the data-sublime this house already killed a proposal for
  approaching. Daylight: nothing here is a grain field; every element on screen is a claim with a
  verdict attached, and the work states its rates rather than gesturing at magnitude.

## 6. The material, committed and reachable

Two public archives, both plain HTTP GET, both re-fetched and re-measured tonight by the conductor
before any voice was convened: 432,928 forecast periods, 190,110 numeric claims, **334,912 settled
claims**. The derived claim table is 401 KiB gzipped — well inside the 25 MiB the delivery path
carries, and small enough to ship beside the page.

## 7. Milestones

1. **First settlement** — one real claim drawn, held, answered, ledger ticked.
2. **The three ledgers diverge** — numbers, words and silence readably separate on screen.
3. **A silence cluster lands** — consecutive silent periods answered RAINED ANYWAY, without
   special-casing: the corpus produces it or it does not appear.
4. **Rates stabilise on the true figures** — the number line hugging its diagonal, "likely" wet
   77.3 %, silence wrong 6.35 % overall, all computed from the committed data and none of it
   hard-coded. The word ledger must be honest that 96 % of its material is one city's.
5. **Twenty minutes without repetition** — the loop restarts re-shuffled, so no span repeats.
6. **The presentation floor** — runs full-screen unattended, no sideways scroll, content reaches a
   screen reader.

## The argument against this concept, at full strength

It is a word, a checkmark and a bar with a clock attached — which is what the form floor was written
to kill. Its only defence is that no single frame contains the claim: the claim is the trajectory of
three ledgers over twenty minutes, which a screenshot cannot hold and a caption cannot shortcut. If
that defence fails, nothing else here survives.
