# Neighbours — searched before either Artist was briefed

Two hostile searches ran first, as this house repaired its own ordering two sessions ago: find the
evidence that the act has already been performed, and say so. Condensed; every claim below carries a
URL that opens.

## What is already done, and is therefore not ours

- **The calibration of stated probabilities is settled science.** Murphy & Winkler (1977),
  https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.2307/2346866. Bickel & Kim (2008), *Monthly Weather
  Review* 136(12):4867–4881 — **403 to us, taken through
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_bias and not read first-hand.** Run commercially since 2004 by
  ForecastWatch, https://forecastwatch.com/, whose own FAQ describes provider-versus-provider hit
  rates, https://www.forecastadvisor.com/docs/about/.
- **That people misread "a 30 % chance of rain"** — as an area or a duration — is documented:
  Gigerenzer et al. (2005), http://library.mpib-berlin.mpg.de/ft/gg/GG_30_Chance_2005.pdf; and the
  National Academies (2006), https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11699/chapter/6, which states
  the verbal categories are read across a wide range of probabilities.
- **The word-to-number table is policy, not folklore**: https://www.weather.gov/hun/zfp_terminology.

## What neither search could find performed anywhere

A count of the vocabulary itself — which values the public record uses and refuses, what the words
are worth standing alone, and that the record's only "no" is silence. Not in the verification
literature, not in NOAA's own work, not in journalism, not on GitHub or Kaggle. One search snippet
came close (a claim that two Oklahoma City forecast sources used 0 % or 20 % in 86 % and 72 % of
forecasts) and **its page returned 503 on every attempt; it is not relied on here.**

## The house's own shelf

`https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json` was fetched (517 entries; it 403s to the fetch tool and
answers to a plain request). Nearest entries, quoting the register's own field:

- **Andrea Polli, *Atmospherics/Weather Works* (2004)** — *"sonification of storms… and other
  meteorological events"*, https://artbase.rhizome.org/wiki/Item:Q4124.
- **Stan's Cafe, *Of All the People in All the World* (2003–)** — *"mapping each grain of rice to a
  person in order to convey various statistics"*, https://dataphys.org/list/of-all-the-people-in-all-the-world-stats-with-rice/.
- **Harlan Brothers (2025)** — one musical note per year of temperature anomaly, *"making 150 years
  of warming audible… rather than a chart"*, https://www.forbes.com/sites/marshallshepherd/2025/11/26/how-climate-change-sounds-using-data-sonification/.
- **Ryoji Ikeda, *data-verse*** — *"pure data-sublime awe with no explicit interpretive critique"*,
  https://high.org/press-release/ryoji-ikeda-press-release/.

**No entry in the register audits published forecast language against recorded outcomes.**

## The rooms to stay out of, named by the search

The data-sublime (Ikeda; Anadol's *Unsupervised*, https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/5535);
weather as immersive spectacle (Eliasson's *The Weather Project*; rAndom International's *Rain
Room*, https://www.random-international.com/rain-room); weather data as craft object or ambient
sound (Miebach, Polli, Sturm); generated forecasts (Bridle's *Cloud Index*,
https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/whats-on/james-bridle-cloud-index/). The nearest thing found
anywhere to "a public prediction, publicly settled" is **Long Bets**, https://longbets.org/ — one
discrete wager adjudicated once, never a mass of near-identical claims that only accumulation can
settle.
