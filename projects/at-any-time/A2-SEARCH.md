## 8. THE VERDICT ON A2

**Does any channel I opened tonight beat *AT ANY TIME* on the season's terms? — No. Not one of the six
clears every bar. But that is not an acquittal, and I will not write it as one.**

Here is what the search actually returned, stated so the gate can rule on it rather than on my
preferences:

**(a) The Court's material survives on three axes, and they are the season's own.**
`SOURCED.` **The clock.** A miscellaneous order is published the day it is issued; there is no lag between
the act and the date. Of the six channels I opened, only **FEMA** matches this. **PFD reports lag by two
months to twenty-one months. MAIB lags by one to three years.** Both of those were channels I preferred on
material and both failed on the season's constitutive ruling, which is not a small thing to discover.
**The geometry.** A one-page sheet with six lines on it is the only form in this file that survives
contact with a page-height-per-day column; every other candidate is 8 pages, 60 pages, or an unbounded HTML
narrative — and item **A4** is already this concept's second wall. **The licence.** The incumbent's status
is clean and the house has already relied on it once. Of the six, two are OGL (attribution, third-party
carve-outs), one is **NC-SA** (disqualifying), and **two I could not verify at all tonight.**

**(b) The incumbent is beaten on the material bar, and I say so plainly.**
`SOURCED.` **Prevention of Future Deaths reports beat *AT ANY TIME* on the material bar and it is not
close.** Every one of ~6,380 documents names a dead person and the thing that killed them and says, in a
coroner's own voice, that it will happen again unless someone acts. The incumbent's material clears that
bar on **38 % of unit-days** and on the other 62 % a visitor meets a motion to seal. The docket's own
`README` concedes the mix. A stranger given a PFD column and a miscellaneous-order column would know what
the first one was about in one line and would need help with the second.

**(c) The incumbent is beaten on its worst standing failure, and that is the finding of the night.**
`SOURCED.` **S5 stands FAILED** against *AT ANY TIME* for a stated reason: a blank day is a fact about a
filing label, not about an institution's conduct. **FEMA's blank days are not like that.** Fifty-one
consecutive days on which no disaster was declared anywhere in the United States is a fact about the world
that a stranger can carry out of the room, which is the entire content of the ruling *the ending is the
damage*. This is the only channel I opened whose holes would **repair** the item the Dramaturg killed. It
failed tonight on one criterion — the retrievable per-declaration document — and it failed it **to a CDN,
not to the source**, on both routes.

**So the answer to A2 is no longer "there was no time."** It is this: **we looked at six channels across
six kinds of institution, here is the table, here are the dates, and the Court's material survives — on the
clock, on the geometry and on the licence — while being beaten on the material bar by a coroner's channel
that fails the clock, and beaten on the hole's meaning by an emergency-declaration channel whose document
form I could not verify before the session ended.**

**What that changes about the charge.** A2 is a charge about a *habit*, and a habit is not answered by
proving the fourth helping was nourishing. What this search can honestly do is **price** the choice, and
the price is now on the record: reaching a fourth time for `supremecourt.gov` costs this house the
strongest first-minute of the six channels it looked at, and costs it the only holes it found that mean
something. If the gate rules that the price is too high, the ruling will be **correct**, and unlike last
session the Artist will now have handed it the evidence to rule with. If the gate rules the other way, it
must do so **naming FEMA/emergency declarations as the thing it declined**, not as a thing nobody had
looked for.

**Was the search thin?** `SOURCED.` No, but it is **unfinished in one specific place**, and I will not
dress that up. The FEMA document question was one successful fetch away from either killing this proposal
or ending the night with nothing to report, and the fetch did not land. Everything else in this file is

### 10.1 The FEMA per-declaration document — the `IMAGINED` hypothesis is HALF right, and the half that fails is the fatal half

The Artist marked as `IMAGINED`: *"the presidential declarations are also published as notices in the
Federal Register, which is a public-domain, per-document, per-date channel."* Opened tonight:

**True, and it does not save the candidate.** The Federal Register's API returns **5,418** documents for
the exact phrase *"Presidential Declaration of a Major Disaster"*
(<https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json>, retrieved 2026-07-31), each with its own
document number and its own retrievable PDF on govinfo.gov. So a dated, individually addressable,
public-domain document does exist. **Three findings kill it anyway:**

**(a) THE LAG IS BACK, AND IT IS THE DEFECT THAT KILLED TWO OTHER CANDIDATES.** Each notice prints its
own act-date. Measured on the five most recent, by fetching the PDFs and reading the `DATES:` line:

| FR document | issued (the act) | published | **lag** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-15086 | 2026-07-20 | 2026-07-27 | **7 days** |
| 2026-14433 (Kansas) | 2026-06-30 | 2026-07-17 | **17 days** |
| 2026-14430 | 2026-06-30 | 2026-07-17 | **17 days** |
| 2026-14429 | 2026-06-30 | 2026-07-17 | **17 days** |
| 2026-14427 (Nebraska) | 2026-06-30 | 2026-07-17 | **17 days** |

The Artist's whole case for this channel was that *"its declaration date **is** the act, with no backlog
