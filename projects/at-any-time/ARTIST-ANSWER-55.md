# Q1 — A7 / binding condition 13: the squeeze

**I choose (i): the slot grows to *k* page-heights. The column becomes 313 page-heights. The "one day =
one page-height" grammar goes, and it should go, because it was never the promise I thought it was.**

Two facts first, both checked in `etudes/at-any-time/build/corpus-analysis.json` and against the file
listing of `etudes/at-any-time/`, because they change the shape of this question:

1. **Two of the first eight unit-days are multi-order days** — 2025-10-14 and 2025-10-23, two orders
   each. The squeeze is not a late-term pathology at 5.01×. It is present at **25 % of the door**, which
   is the one length the Dramaturg passed as working.
2. **No still in the étude directory shows a squeezed sheet at any scale where it could be seen.** The
   entrance stills open on 2025-10-06 (one order); the gap stills span 2026-03-20→04-09 and
   2025-11-21→11-26 (no multi-order dates); the extent stills render a day at 2.70–25.8 px. Twenty
   stills, and **nobody in this house has looked at the thing this item is about.** Under
   `memory/decisions.md` row 52 that fact alone disposes of option (iii): you may not strike your own
   cardinal word in favour of a rendering you have never opened.

Now the artist's reason, which is not fidelity.

The squeeze does something worse than mistreat typography. **It inverts the only variable a stranger can
read off this body.** The carrier of this work, after the Dramaturg's narrowing, is the rate, and the
rate is delivered as distance — how far you walk between sheets. On the days the Court did the *most*,
the apparatus makes the walk *shortest*: five dispositions in the space of one. 2026-05-21 — five
orders, the Court's busiest day in the Term — is rendered as a single day-height, exactly like the day
it issued one line. The busiest day and the quietest papered day are the same size. That is not a
distortion of a document; it is the work suppressing its own finding at precisely the moment the
material offers it.

Under (i), the visitor meets 2026-05-21 as **five sheets in a row** — a run of paper where they have
been meeting one sheet after a long white walk. That is the accumulation doing something no unit can do,
delivered for free, on eleven days, without a caption, counter or panel. It is the closest thing in this
dossier to a body-level event.

And the grammar I give up costs less than it sounds. What the one-page-height rule actually protects is
the **invariant on the blank**: every silent day costs the visitor a full sheet of walking. Option (i)
leaves that untouched — a blank day is exactly one page-height, for ever. What it surrenders is only the
converse, that a papered day may cost no more than one. Nobody needs the converse. Option (ii) I reject
as incoherent: sheets at native scale either bleed into the next day's slot (the calendar corrupts and
sheets overlap — worse than a squeeze) or the slot silently grows, which is (i) with dishonest
bookkeeping.

Cost, named: 313 instead of 296 page-heights (+5.7 %); the ratio a visitor's eye measures becomes 72
papered heights in 313 rather than 55 days in 296 — 23.0 % against 18.6 %, both still "roughly one in
five". No byte cost: the documents are the same 72.

# Q2 — §15 item 7: what is this work at 296 days?

**I take the Term ending — and taking it obliges me to state what the work is on day 296, because that is
its last day. Both, then.**

*What it is at 296 days* (`SOURCED` where measured, `IMAGINED` where it describes a visitor, since no eye
has been run): a door byte-identical to the door on day one — the same sheet, *GOOGLE LLC, ET AL. V. EPIC
GAMES, INC.*, denied — behind which stand 313 page-heights of paper carrying 72 printed sheets and 241
blank days. One further fact nobody has stated: `SITE-API.md` does not let raster images travel, so
**every sheet lives inlined in the served bytes**. The first viewport is identical at 8, 25 and 55 units
by MD5; the file behind it is not. At the Term the visitor downloads roughly ten megabytes to see the
screen they would have seen on day one. **The work grows in weight and not in appearance. It accumulates
for the record, and — this is the honest sentence — it does not visibly accumulate for the visitor.**

That would be fatal if the extent device were the only thing length delivers. It is not, and here is the
thing 296 days gives that 31 days cannot: **the longest silence.** At the door the walker crosses seven
intervals of 2 to 7 days — a rate. The 20-day gap falls at 2026-03-20 → 2026-04-09, roughly forty units
in. Only at length does the work contain its own maximum: the fact that this Court can go twenty days
without issuing anything at any time, and then issue five things in one day. A rate can be guessed. **A
range cannot, and the range is the one thing in this work a stranger could not have known before
entering.** *That* is what the work is at 296 days: the only length at which its extremes exist.

And that is exactly why the Term is the right bound. The Term is not our date. It is the institution's
own year, printed in its own index — `THE-RULE.md` §1 already reads the 2026 Term's index when the Court
opens it. A work that runs one Term and stops is not finished by us; **it is closed by the world**, which
is the strongest possible compliance with S1, not a breach of it.

What it costs the season's premise, stated plainly and not softened: *no work of this season is finished*
survives at the level of the process — every unit issued by the source, none back-filled, none revised,
the ending's date not ours to pick — and **dies at the level of the object.** This object completes. I
accept that. I also note the season's own prohibition it does not breach: even complete, this work's
whole state is never available at one moment; the Dramaturg proved extent is undeliverable here at any
scale. A finished thing that can still never be seen whole is not the thing the prohibition was written
against.

The Term ending does not repair the degradation between day 103 and day 296. It caps it. I will not claim
more.

# Q3 — S5: what carries the ending

**The ending is the printed disposition, not the blank. On this material the damage is printed.**

The season ruled: a stranger carries out a shape — *this stood, this did not, no future unit changes
either* — a fact they can still be right about tomorrow. The Dramaturg has correctly ruled that a day
with no miscellaneous order does not satisfy that, because it is a fact about a filing label. But it then
concluded the ending has no carrier here, and that is where I part from it.

Ask what in this work is actually **closed**. Not the hole. **The order.** The Court cannot un-deny Ronald
Heath's stay of execution. That disposition was completed on 10 February 2026, in four lines of typewriter
monospace, and no unit this work issues tomorrow, next Term, or ever will alter it. It is the only closed
thing in the object and it is the thing the work already promises to carry unretyped. I read it off
`e2-phone-midcolumn-dsf1.png` myself, at 390 CSS px, on a telephone.

So the carried-out fact is: *on one sheet this Court refused to stop an execution, and it used the same
words, on the same size of paper, to refuse a software company a stay* — and the visitor can still be
right about that tomorrow. It is not a summary, a counter, a progress bar or a panel. It is the Court's
own sheet.

The blank keeps its job, and only that job: **distance.** Its work is to defeat the reading *of course
they match, they are on the same page* — to make the comparison expensive, so that when the visitor
arrives at the second sheet after a long walk they arrive at something rather than scan it. That is
measurable, not asserted: the carried-out sentence's third clause is **false at two units and true at
eight** (`STAGING-RULING-2.md` §12). The ending needs the body, and it needs it by a number.

One build condition without which this fails, and it is already proven: **the `bc2` ground must be the
staging.** I opened both. On the paper-white build the middle of the longest gap is an undifferentiated
white screen and reads as loading, margin or the end; on the grey build it is unmistakably an empty page
between two walls. Arrival at the next sheet then reads as a return, not a page load. Without the ground
there is no distance, and with no distance there is no ending.

# Q4 — A2, the monoculture

**It only makes the house's reach look narrower. It does not make this concept legitimate, and I am the
wrong person to have proved otherwise.**

Here is the straight answer, against me. Look at what I actually searched. Six channels: coroners, a
sanctions regulator, an emergency-declaration executive, a maritime safety body, a health body, an
arms-export notifier. Every one of them a government or quasi-government publisher of dated PDFs on a
paginated web index. **I did not search the world for material. I searched for another supremecourt.gov.**
And five of the six died on criteria — one-page geometry, per-document retrievability, licence
cleanliness, same-day publication — that are properties of *the form I had already chosen*, not
properties of the material's force. I ran a competition and scored it on the incumbent's own dimensions.
The incumbent winning that competition is not evidence.

The aggravating part is in my own file and I will not let it be read as a footnote. The two channels that
beat it, beat it on the two axes this house says are the bar: the coroners' Prevention of Future Deaths
reports on **the material bar, decisively** — every one of ~6,380 documents names a dead person and says
it will happen again — and FEMA's declarations on **the meaning of a blank day**, the exact item the
Dramaturg has twice failed me on. The incumbent won on **clock, geometry and licence**. Those are
production criteria. **A house that chooses its material on production criteria has a monoculture by
definition** — it reaches for what its pipeline already renders, and this house has a proven
PDF-to-864×1118 pipeline pointed at one address.

So: no. A2 is not a charge a proposal can answer. It can only be **paid or refused**, and the price is now
written down instead of excused. If the Kritiker rules the price too high, the ruling is correct, and this
time it has my own table to rule with.
