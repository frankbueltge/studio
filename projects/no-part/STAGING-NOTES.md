## A. The break rule

**The principle, and everything else follows from it.** The instruction fixes everything that decides
whether the document can be read at 1:1 by a walking body. It fixes nothing that decides which
entries fall at a break. Conditions of reading are ours; the arrangement of consequences is the
room's and the realiser's. Wherever I was unsure, I asked which of those two a rule was, and the
answer settled it. **[I]**

**Fixed, and why.**

- **All 39 sheets, in folio order, sheet 1 at the left (items 3, 6).** Order is the document's own and
  is not ours to touch. Left-to-right ascending is fixed for a reason worth stating: mounting sheet 39
  at the left would present the document, to anyone reading in the ordinary sweep, backwards. That is
  a re-setting of the document at wall scale, and this work's whole discipline is that it re-sets
  nothing. The reverse approach is staged by the room (§B), never by mirroring the paper. **[I]**
- **100 %, no scaling (items 3, 4).** The finding is a proportion between one line of type and 761
  rows of type **[S]**, and any scaling makes the proportion a picture of itself. Item 4 exists
  because the single most likely way this work fails is a printer defaulting to fit-to-printable-area
  and shrinking every sheet by a few per cent — silently, uniformly, and invisibly to the person doing
  it. **[I]**
- **One sheet tall; no stacking (item 7).** This is the hardest prohibition in the instruction and the
  one I would defend first. A second row of sheets is not a break, it is a **re-binding**: it folds
  8.42 m back into a block a body can stand in front of, which is the operation the work exists to
  make unavailable. A stacked *NO PART* is a poster of *NO PART*. **[I]**
- **0 mm joint (item 8).** The document has one period in it — 8.276 mm, the row pitch **[S]**. A
  gap between sheets introduces a second period at 215.9 mm, a rhythm the Court did not set and we
  would have. **[I]**
- **One height, 1600 mm to sheet centre (item 9).** Two reasons. First: every row must be the same
  distance from the same eye, or the proportion is measured by a body that had to work harder for
  some of it than for others. Second, and this is the one I want on the file — **a run that changes
  height supplies emphasis.** Sheets at eye level would be favoured over sheets at knee level, and the
  finding this work rests on is that in thirty-nine pages this institution used no emphasis at all.
  A climbing line would have the studio finishing the state's sentence for it. This is the same
  argument that kept saturated colour off the still, applied to the vertical axis. **[I]**
  *Consequence, computed:* bottom edge 1460.3 mm; the mass sentence lands at ≈1503.7 mm above the
  floor. **[S]** from y = 123.04 pt; **[I]** the choice of 1600.
- **Flat, one plane, unbent, unframed (item 10).** A sheet bent round a corner curves the rows and
  makes the page a surface we shaped. A frame or a mat adds a border the document does not have.
- **Nothing else in the room (item 11).** There is no wall label. If the work needs one, the work has
  failed, and a label would hide that.

**This is a constraint on the realiser's freedom rather than a free hand, deliberately, and it costs
something I should name.** §3b of the proposal said the break would put some named people *"round a
corner, up a stair, or below eye level."* Fixing the height deletes two of those three. I take that
trade: a sheet at 2.4 m or at 0.4 m is not a consequence for the people on it, it is a sheet nobody
reads, and a work whose yield is available only at reading distance cannot author places where
reading distance does not exist. What remains is real enough — round a corner, onto a return, behind
a door, into a passage a body has to choose to enter. And the fixed height buys something back: it
makes the room's furniture consequential. A window band, a dado, a radiator at 1.5 m now forces a
break instead of being ducked under. **[I]**

**Free.** Where the line breaks; how many runs; which walls; which room; what the room does to the
document once these conditions are met — including putting sheets in a dark corridor, behind the
swing of a door, or on a wall a visitor must turn round to see.

**Forbidden, and this is the part that guards the joint (items 13, 14).** Every forbidden move has one
shape: **choosing the break by reading the document.** Breaking at a section heading, keeping a
particular sheet off a corner, giving the turn a wall of its own, nudging the joint to make a run come
out even — each is the realiser rescuing the work from its own arrangement, and each replaces the
room's decision with a curatorial one. Item 13 states the positive form, which is the operable one:
measure the wall, let the sheets fall. The prohibition is not enforceable and does not need to be; it
is the terms under which the mounting is a realisation of this work rather than an arrangement of the
same paper. **[I]**

**One thing this file does not claim.** The break is the realiser's authorship. It is not a completing
act, and the work is complete without it (condition 8). Nor is the break a statement: it is content in
the literal sense — a physical decision about named people that stands until the work comes down —
and not in the sense that it means something. Reading the break as *"the room could not hold this many
people"* is an interpretation we would be adding, and neither the instruction nor the wall makes it.

## B. Entry from the sheet-39 end

**What the instruction may legitimately do.** It cannot make a stranger walk in a direction. It can
site the work in a room. Those are different acts, and the second is available without adding a mark:

1. **Put the sheet-39 end where the room delivers people** (item 16, first clause) — nearest the door,
   on the wall a body meets first, at the end of the approach.
2. **Close the sheet-1 end** (item 16, second clause) — an inside corner, the end of a wall, a dead
   end — so that no route delivers anyone to sheet 1 first.
3. **Add nothing to achieve either** (item 17) — no arrow, no floor mark, no rope, no attendant saying
   *start over there*. A work that adds not one glyph to the document may not add one to the floor.

**And it fails, honestly, some of the time.** A wall has two ends. Somebody walks past, doubles back,
comes in with a colleague from the far side, or simply turns left. Item 18 says so on the work's own
public page rather than in a note nobody reads.

**What is lost in the minority case, stated plainly.** At the sheet-39 end the material is the last
entry of REHEARINGS DENIED — the Court refusing one named person leave to file a rehearing petition
under seal **[S]**. Then the individuated tail; then, on sheet 32, the one sentence; then roughly
6.3 m of rows **[S]** which the visitor now knows that sentence covers; ending on the masthead and a
date. The sentence is a **floor** the body then walks the length of.

Entering at sheet 1, the same sentence arrives as a **resolution**: it explains, at the far end, what
all those rows were, and the work is nearly over when it does. The mass is walked in ignorance and
never re-walked with knowledge. That version is not nothing, and it is not the work either: it fails
the terminal test, because the last thing the body meets is a verdict rather than a date, and a work
that ends on its own point ends as an argument. **[I]**

**One thing the reverse approach does not fix, and one it does.** Condition 6: `24-1073 MAXWELL,
GHISLAINE V. UNITED STATES` sits on sheet 5, about 0.9 m from the sheet-1 end **[S]**. Reverse entry
does not remove it; it moves it from 0.9 m inside the door to the last metre of a 7.5 m walk. A
visitor who enters at sheet 1 still meets it in what the Kritiker measured as *"four seconds…
requiring no traversal whatsoever"*, and a photographer looking for it will find it wherever it is.
The staging improves the odds for a body and changes nothing for a camera.
**[I]**

**And the break can undo this too.** A break at sheet 6, say, puts MAXWELL at the free end of a run —
a corner a body arrives at. Nobody chose that, nobody may choose against it (item 14), and that is the
price of leaving the joint live.

## C. The instruction itself

**It names the document, the URL and the hash.** Provenance here is a material specification, not
explanation. Without the hash, *we add not one glyph* is a promise; with it, it is a check anybody can
run in one command. The realiser also simply cannot make the work without the file.

**It does not state the finding, and it is not hiding it.** Three reasons, in order of force:

1. **The realiser is holding the document.** Concealment is not available and is not attempted; they
   will read the sheets or not, exactly as a visitor would. What we decline to supply is our reading.
2. **Telling them what to look for corrupts the one thing they author.** A realiser who knows the
   sentence is on sheet 32 will break the line where sheet 32 looks best — or deliberately not — and
   item 13 forbids precisely that. The instruction cannot both hand over the criterion and forbid its
   use.
3. **It would be the loudest thing in the work.** Any sentence of ours about what this document shows
   would be the only sentence in the work not written by the Court.

**Coyness is answered by item 15, not by an explanation.** The realiser is told what their decision
does — that named people fall at the break, that it is public, that it stands — and is told nothing
about what the object means. That is the correct division: they are authoring a placement, not an
argument. An instruction that withheld the consequence would be coy; one that supplied the meaning
would be an essay with a wall in front of it.

**On tier tags.** They are not inline in `INSTRUCTION.md`, and that is a decision rather than a lapse.
That page is the work's only public voice and must not carry the house's apparatus in its body. The
tiers are assigned instead by a foot line that covers every sentence on the page with no remainder:
items 1–2 measured on the named file, everything else instruction and judgement. Nothing is blurred;
the boundary is drawn once rather than twenty times.

**What the instruction deliberately does not contain:** any claim about how the work reads in a room
(condition 11 — I have no photograph of mounted paper and will not write a sentence that would need
one); any description of the work as risky (condition 9 — a public federal document reproduced entire
asserts nothing and exposes nobody here); any of the struck measurements (condition 3 — no *last 95
centimetres*, no thirty-second walk, no shape change in peripheral vision, no *no emphasis anywhere*,
no folio at the prose inset); and any number I have not seen measured.

## D. What breaks when the line breaks

The finding is a proportion between one line of type and 761 rows of type **[S]**, and it is delivered
by a body walking. The break can take nothing from the document — order, scale and completeness
survive every legal break — and it takes the only thing the mounting adds, which is **continuity**.

- **One run, 8.42 m.** The instruction as written, in a room that does not usually exist. The body
  meets exactly one event in 8.42 m, and that event is a sentence.
- **Two runs, e.g. 20 + 19 sheets (4.318 m + 4.102 m).** The 6.298 m of mass is cut once. Now the body
  meets **two** events: a corner and a sentence — and the corner is architecture, arrives first, and
  is the more physical of the two. A turn is also a place a visitor is given permission to stop. The
  proportion survives as a quantity and is delivered in two instalments.
- **Four runs, ~2.1 m each.** The dominant rhythm of the experience is now the room's: three turns to
  one sentence, and the sentence is the fourth thing that happened. No stretch of rows is longer than
  an ordinary corridor. The proportion is still arithmetically present and is no longer *duration* —
  it becomes something the visitor reconstructs across turns from memory, which is reading a number,
  which is the thing this work exists instead of.

**The threshold, and it is a judgement built on a measurement.** 761 rows × 8.276 mm = 6.298 m
**[S]**. My rule: if no single unbroken run carries at least **half the mass** — 380.5 rows, 3.149 m,
which is 14.6 sheets and rounds up to **15 sheets, 3,238.5 mm** — then the largest quantity of
undifferentiated rows the body ever meets is smaller than the part of it a visitor may reasonably
decline to finish, and the mass is never met, only sampled. **[I]** The arithmetic is exact; the half
is mine, and I will not dress it as measured.

**It is necessary and not sufficient, and I will not write the sufficient version.** A 15-sheet run
guarantees a long run; it does not guarantee that the long run is made of rows rather than of the
individuated tail and the coda. To guarantee that, the instruction would have to say *which* sheets
the long run carries, which is authoring the break — the one thing it must not do. So the condition
stays weak, on purpose, and the weakness is named here rather than hidden in a stronger-sounding rule.

**What happens below it: the work does not stop.** Item 19 says mount it anyway and record it. An
instruction that forbids its own realisation in every room that exists has chosen never to be made,
and condition 9 will not let this house call that courage — it is the safest possible posture, taken
by a work that is already the safest object this studio could make. So the threshold is a **reporting
boundary, not a permission boundary**: below it the work stands and *we* stop, because no mounting
under 15 sheets may be used as evidence that a body finds anything in that room. That is condition 15
made operable in advance of the increment rather than argued after it.

## What I refused to fix

- **The number of runs, and where they fall.** The joint.
- **The distance between runs.** Specifying it would be designing the corner.
- **Anything about the visitor.** No dwell time, no route, no suggested reading, no count of anything.
- **The room's quality** — light level, traffic, whether it is a gallery. The work does not require a
  gallery and the instruction does not ask for one.
- **A minimum wall for the *shortest* run.** A three-sheet run at the end of a corridor is a real
  consequence of a real room and is exactly the kind of thing the joint exists to let happen.

## What I cannot know from here, and what would settle it

1. **Whether 15 sheets is anywhere near the right threshold.** Nothing in this house can answer it. It
   is settled by one person walking one mounting and being asked what they saw — which is condition
   15's first increment, and until it exists, item 19 is a judgement wearing a number.
2. **Whether 1600 mm is head height for the bodies that will stand there.** It is a chosen figure.
   A mounting settles it.
3. **Whether the room delivers people to the sheet-39 end in practice.** Settled by watching one
   room, by the realiser, not by us.
4. **Whether the two ends of the document print as I read them.** I decoded content streams; standing
   rule (a) wants a render. Two pages, one build step.

## The objection I cannot answer

**That in a small enough room these rules leave exactly one legal layout, and then the instruction
authored the break after all.** Take a domestic room with three usable walls of 3.4, 3.1 and 2.8 m and
a door and a window. Fixed height, one sheet tall, 0 mm joints, breaks only between sheets, no
re-stacking, at least one run of 15 — there may be one arrangement that satisfies all of it, or none.
If one, the realiser has not authored a break; they have complied with a constraint, and the work's
*one live joint* is a joint with no play in it. Everything I fixed is defensible one rule at a time,
and the objection is against their sum, which is where I cannot check my own work: I have never stood
in a room and tried to solve this. It is settled by a floor plan and a tape measure, and the first
realiser will settle it in an afternoon.
