## The one result that changes the work

All three readers, asked which single sentence they would repeat to somebody else, returned **the same
sentence, verbatim, unprompted, independently**:

> *"As the petitioner has repeatedly abused this Court's process, the Clerk is directed not to accept
> any further petitions in noncriminal matters from petitioner unless the docketing fee required by
> Rule 38(a) is paid and the petition is submitted in compliance with Rule 33.1."*

The proposal predicted a different residue. Its §2 states the carry-out is *"a phrase they can repeat:
took no part in the consideration or decision of this petition"*, and §11 keeps the *consideration*
finding inside the work on the strength of that. **Nobody carried that out.** Three of three carried
out the filing bar — the Court's harshest individuated sentence, the one the proposal itself calls
"the longest thing the Court says to any single person in the section".

Read honestly, this cuts both ways and the record states both. It **confirms** the structural claim
that the individuated voice is what a stranger takes away, and that its content is sanction. It
**refutes** the house's stated guess about which sentence does it, and therefore the *tone* of the
ending we have been describing: the walk does not end on an absence a visitor can name, it ends on a
punishment they can quote. Nothing about the object changes; the sentences we write about it do.

Limits, stated before anyone leans on this: three readers, one cell, no control, model instances and
not people, pages read one at a time and not a body walking eight metres. It cannot show that entry
direction matters and it says nothing about the traverse.
