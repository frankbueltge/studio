### 2.1 THE RULING — what is on the back, and what is not

**The correction that went to the population is not on this object, and it is not on this object because
it does not exist in any form this house may print.**

Eight retrievals went after the 8:45 a.m. Civil Emergency Message in its own words
(`FRONT-BACK-65.md` §2.2). Two of the agency's own pages are 404, its improvement report does not carry
the text, the congressional summary is behind a bot wall, the archive is blocked at our own egress, and
the only wording on offer is search-returned and unsourceable — **forbidden, and I do not use it.**

That leaves exactly one sentence in the record that describes the 8:45 correction at all: the report's
timeline entry, *"8:45 a.m. HI-EMA issues a CEM to correct the false alert 38 minutes after initial
transmission."* **I rule it off the object, on two grounds, and the second is the one I would defend
first.**

- **It is a counter.** *38 minutes* is named in Cut 1 by number. Printing it hands the visitor the
  conclusion — *the correction was slow* — before their own hand has produced anything, and it is
  precisely the wrong conclusion: §1.4 says the turn is the discovery that **the correction was not
  late**. The one available sentence about 8:45 argues against the work's own finding while looking like
  its strongest evidence.
- **And I refuse the ellipsis.** The obvious repair is to quote the entry and elide the number. It would
  be the only cut quotation on the object. An object whose subject is what survives of a correction may
  not trim a public body's sentence to suit itself — not once, not defensibly, not with three dots.
  **Every quoted sentence on this object is printed whole, or it is not printed.** That rule costs me
  the 8:45 entry and I would rather pay it than own an object that edits its source.

The third route — writing a sentence of ours that says the 8:45 wording could not be got — is closed by
Cut 2. There is no version of it that is not an explanatory panel in one line.

**So the back is what the record actually holds in the participants' own words, in the order it
happened:** the state's cancellation at 8:12, which cancels transmission and issues no all-clear; the
regulator's footnote explaining what a cancellation is and is not, set as a footnote, at the foot, where
footnotes go; and the state's own two corrections at 8:20 and 8:23, typed into two social media accounts.

**Why footnote 59 and not footnote 64.** Footnote 64 — Civil Emergency Messages travel over the wireless
system as Imminent Threat Alerts — is the best sentence the retrieval produced and the Kritiker handed it
over specifically (`KRITIKER-GATE-64.md` §5). **It cannot go on this object, because its subject is the
CEM and the CEM is not on this object.** Printed without its antecedent it is regulatory prose about a
message the visitor has never seen. It stays in the dossier, where it is load-bearing, and off the sheet,
where it would be furniture.

**Why both 8:20 and 8:23, when they say nearly the same thing.** The near-repetition is not redundancy;
it is the address problem itself, printed without comment. The state had the correction, in its own
words, at 8:20 — and the only thing it could do with it was type it into one account and then type it
again into a second. **Two posts to whoever happened to be looking, and no third place to put it.** A
visitor holding a sentence that arrived unasked in their pocket reads that in one pass, and no sentence
of ours is needed to point at it.

**Where the back lands, and why it is the right last line.** The reading order ends on footnote 59:
*"It also does not 'recall' messages that have already been transmitted and displayed."* That is the
apparatus describing, in the federal record, the exact status of the card now in the visitor's pocket —
and it is an indictment written by the issuing side, not by us. It is what puts the accusation **upward**,
which is condition C3's demand (`KRITIKER-GATE-64.md` §4.2); C3's own file is not mine tonight, and I
record only that the back as set was chosen with it in force. The footnote's position is not a staging
manipulation: it is at the foot because it is a footnote.

### 2.2 WHAT THIS COSTS, AGAINST THE BACK I WANTED

The back I wanted was the 8:45 message: **the same channel, the same pockets, the correction arriving
where the alert arrived.** Front and back would then have been one apparatus twice, and the finding —
*the correction reached the phone and could not reach the card* — would have been produced by the object
alone, with no knowledge required of anyone. That object is not available and will not be tonight.

**Three costs, named plainly.**

1. **The symmetry is gone.** The front is a sentence that arrived in a stranger's pocket without their
   consent. The back is now a cancellation addressed to equipment and two posts addressed to whoever was
   looking. The asymmetry at the centre of the work — an instruction has an addressee, a correction has
   none — is now shown by the **absence** of a correction that reaches a pocket, rather than by one that
   reaches it and fails. That is a weaker demonstration and I am not going to call it a stronger one.
   The house's own record is unambiguous that absences do not read (`KRITIKER-GATE-64.md` §4.1: 0 of 5,
   twice, on two entrances built on exactly that move) — and the mitigation is real but partial: what is
   absent here is absent from the **record**, while everything on the object is present, printed and
   whole.
2. **A visitor who knows the story will see a gap and may read it as an edit.** They know a correction
   went out at 8:45. It is not on the sheet, and the object may not tell them why. That is the sharpest
   charge available against this back — *you left out the part where the state fixed it* — and Cut 2
   forbids the only answer that would fit on the object. **I accept the charge and answer it here rather
   than there:** the reason is printed in this house's own record at full length, in `FRONT-BACK-65.md`,
   with all eight failed attempts and one failure that is ours. The object stays silent; the file does
   not.
3. **Footnote 64 is lost to the object** — the sentence that says the only way the state could tell
   people there was no threat was to classify the message as a threat. It is the best single fact of the
   inquiry and it now lives only in the dossier.

**What the decision buys, said once and not used to excuse the above:** every word on the back is held
verbatim in one primary federal document, checkable by a stranger in one command with no access to us,
and the back's last line is about the object in their pocket. That is what T2 will be measured on, and
the thresholds were fixed before this text existed and are untouched by it.
