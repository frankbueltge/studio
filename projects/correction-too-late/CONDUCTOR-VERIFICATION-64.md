## 2. AND THE PRIMARY TEXT PRODUCED SOMETHING STRONGER THAN THE COUNT

**Source:** FCC, *Report and Recommendations: Hawaii Emergency Management Agency January 13, 2018 False
Alert* (April 2018), <https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-350119A1.txt>, the full text fetched
and searched first-hand on 2026-08-04.

**Footnote 59, verbatim — the Commission's own description of what a cancellation is:**

> *"The cancellation is an instruction to downstream EAS and WEA equipment to cease retransmission. It
> does not generate a new alert transmission (e.g., an 'all clear' message). It also does not 'recall'
> messages that have already been transmitted and displayed."*

This is the finding stated by the regulator itself, and it answers in advance the objection the Artist
predicted against its own proposal — *cancellation exists elsewhere in the stack, at protocol level*.
It does exist. **It is addressed to equipment, not to people**, and the authority that wrote the rule
says so in a footnote.

**Footnote 58, verbatim:** *"According to the HI-EMA Report, a State Warning Point employee sent a
cancellation message at 8:12 a.m., and the State Warning Point issued a cancellation of the CDW message
at 8:13 a.m."* — **the cancellation was sent at 8:13 and the public correction went out at 8:45.** The
machine was told in six minutes. The population was told in thirty-eight.

**The timeline entry for 8:27 a.m., verbatim:** *"HI-EMA staff meets to discuss options for a second,
corrective EAS and WEA message. HI-EMA determines that a Civil Emergency Message (CEM) is the alert
event code that best meets the criteria."* At **8:30** they call the federal programme office *"for
guidance that CEM is the appropriate event code, but the call is not answered"*; they reach a second
official on a mobile and, *"after 45 seconds, they agree that the correction meets the criteria for a
CEM."* **They had to hold a meeting to decide what to call the truth, and then get a second opinion on
it.** From 8:31 to 8:44 an official *"logs into HI-EMA's alert origination software and creates false
alert correction messages"* — by hand, because there was no template. At **8:45** the CEM goes out.

**And the Commission's own recommendation to every state**, in the report's list: *"Develop, in a manner
consistent with the Commission's rules, protocols governing tests, uses, and corrections to alerts that
are sent to the public"* — and that corrections *"must be issued over the same systems used to issue the
false alert."* A regulator does not recommend building a thing that already exists.
