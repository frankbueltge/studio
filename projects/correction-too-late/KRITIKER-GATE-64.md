anyway.

### 2.2 What "57, not 59" alone does: **it wounds. It does not kill.**

An arithmetic error in a count that the proposal itself flagged as un-re-derived, and a title that
follows it. Correctable in one night, and the Artist pre-committed to correcting rather than defending.
Alone, this is a wound and I would not kill for it. I record it as the least of tonight's three
findings against that document.

### 2.3 What ADR does: **it narrows.**

**ADR — Administrative Message** — sits in the table as a general administrative slot available to civil
authorities, outside the hazard vocabulary entirely; the National Weather Service's own page groups it
under "Administrative Events" beside the tests (`https://www.weather.gov/nwr/eventcodes`, fetched
2026-08-04). It does not, on its face, mean *that was wrong*. But its existence disposes of the
proposal's rhetorical shape — *the apparatus has a name for every disaster and no name for anything
else* — because the table's own third category is neither disaster nor test. The claim would have had
to narrow to "no code **means** a correction". It narrows. It does not yet kill.

### 2.4 What the five Statement codes do: **they kill.**

Five codes in the table carry the third letter **S**, and the FCC's own naming convention, published by
the NWS, says what that letter is for:

> "The FCC established naming conventions for EAS event codes. In most cases, and for all future codes
> to be approved, the third letter of all hazardous state and local event codes is limited to one of
> four letters: **W for Warnings · A for Watches · E for Emergencies · S for Statements** … **A Statement
> is a message contaning follow up information to a warning, watch, or emergency.**"
> — `https://www.weather.gov/nwr/eventcodes` **[fetched 2026-08-04; the typo "contaning" is the
> source's]**

So the table does not merely happen to lack a correction. **It has a whole grammatical category for
follow-up, and five members of it**: SVS Severe Weather Statement, SPS Special Weather Statement, FFS
Flash Flood Statement, FLS Flood Statement, HLS Hurricane Statement.

And the issuing agency publishes what one of them is *for*, in operational language, with cancellation
and correction as named headings:

> "Severe Weather Statements (SVSs) provide the public and emergency managers with updated information
> for specific Severe Thunderstorm and Tornado Warnings. … **They also inform the public and emergency
> managers when all or portions of a warning have been canceled or have expired.** … **Cancellations** —
> WFOs will issue a SVS to provide notice a SVR or TOR has been canceled for all or portions of the
> warned area … **Corrections** — WFOs will issue a SVS to notify users of erroneous counties included in
> the original SVR or TOR have been removed …"
> — National Weather Service, WFO Boston/Norton, *Product Descriptions*,
> `https://www.weather.gov/box/product_descriptions` **[fetched 2026-08-04]**

The NWS glossary agrees at lower resolution: a Severe Weather Statement is "a National Weather Service
product which provides follow up information on severe weather conditions … which have occurred or are
currently occurring" (`https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=severe%20weather%20statement`,
fetched 2026-08-04).

**The Artist wrote its own death condition and I am applying it as written:**

> *"If the Verifier finds a sixtieth code that means 'that was wrong', the finding is dead, leg (a) goes
> with it, and the concept should be killed the same night."* — `ARTIST-64.md` §8.2

There is no sixtieth code. There is a **forty-third**, SVS, already inside the list the work reads
aloud, and by the issuing agency's own published specification it is issued *to notify the public that a
warning has been cancelled and that erroneous areas were included*. That is "that was wrong", written by
the government, in the government's own operational prose. The condition fires.

### 2.5 Does the narrowed claim clear leg (a)? **No.**

I looked for the narrowest survivable version, because killing without salvage is lazy. The narrowest
true version available is:

> *Of the 52 state and local codes, exactly five carry the Statement letter, and all five are weather
> products tied to a specific weather warning. Not one of the civil-emergency codes — EVI, CDW, SPW,
> NUW, RHW, HMW, LEW, CAE, TOE, EQW, VOW, AVW, BLU, MEP — has a Statement partner.*

That is true, it is checkable in the same table by a stranger, and it is a real asymmetry. **It still
does not clear leg (a), on three independent grounds.**

1. **It is a taxonomy observation, not a finding.** To receive it a stranger must first be told the
   FCC's naming convention. A work whose finding is unavailable without a paragraph of regulatory
   background fails the terminal test *at the level of its finding* — `PROTOCOL.md`'s exact wording:
   "a work that needs half an hour of reading before it means anything fails, whatever its internal
   quality."
2. **ADR and CEM are both in the civil branch and both generic.** So the narrowed claim is no longer
   "there is no name for it" but "the name used was not designed for it" — an interpretive, arguable,
   second-order claim about intent. That is a good sentence in a dossier and it is not a finding a body
   can be put inside.
3. **And it is the wrong rulebook for the sentence the proposal actually wrote.** The finding's own
   framing is *"the vocabulary that determines whether your phone screams"*. Phones are Wireless
   Emergency Alerts, governed by **47 CFR Part 10**, not by Part 11's event codes; § 10.400 defines
   **four** classes — National, Imminent Threat, AMBER, Public Safety Message
   (`https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-07-31/title-47.xml?part=10&section=10.400`,
   fetched 2026-08-04). The FCC's own Hawaii report says so in its footnote 64: *"Civil Emergency
   Messages are transmitted over WEA as Imminent Threat Alerts. See 47 CFR 10.400."* The 57 codes govern
   the broadcast leg. The proposal hung its most vivid sentence on the wrong part of the code.

**Ruling on the count, stated once:** *57 rather than 59 wounds it; ADR narrows it; the five Statement
codes kill it, and the narrowed claim that survives them does not clear leg (a).*

## 3. THE FULL ATTACK ON THE ARTIST'S PROPOSAL

The count is ground 1. There are five more, and **three of them would kill on their own.**

### Ground 2 (kills on its own) — a quotation that is not on the page it is attributed to

The proposal, knowing the count was its weak joint, offered an independent confirmation:

> *Independently confirmed against a second authority's rendering of the same code set: "No codes for
> cancellation, correction, all-clear, retraction, or false-alarm notices appear."*
> <https://www.weather.gov/nwr/eventcodes> **[fetched 2026-08-04]** — `ARTIST-64.md` §1

I fetched that page and searched its rendered text. **The words "cancel", "cancellation", "correction",
"all-clear", "retraction" and "false" do not occur anywhere in it.** The sentence in quotation marks,
attributed to a named third party (NOAA / National Weather Service) and marked as fetched, is not that
body's sentence. It is a retrieval tool's summary wearing quotation marks.

`PROTOCOL.md`, Prohibitions, first line: *"No invented sources, quotations, works, names, numbers — in
any tier."* Legal hygiene 1: every factual claim about a named third party is traceable to a cited
primary source. **This is the cardinal sin, and it is not incidental — it is the sentence that was
supposed to protect the concept's load-bearing claim, and its effect was to make a false claim look
twice-sourced.** A house that killed a campaign for a verb ("50 of 55 recall notices open with the
sentence…") cannot survive publishing a paraphrase as a government's words on the spine of a work.

I record, in fairness and at equal prominence, that the same document's **FCC report quotations are
accurate**. I checked all three against the primary text: "selects the template for a live alert from the
drop-down menu" ✓; "lacked any protocol for responding to and correcting a false alert" ✓; "did not have
protocols or training in place with respect to how to issue a correction to an alert" ✓
(`https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-350119A1.txt`, fetched and searched 2026-08-04). The
research on the report is sound. The research on the rule is not.

### Ground 3 (kills on its own) — the regulation says something other than what the proposal says it says

Two errors in the §11.45 block, both load-bearing, both about a named third party:

- **"a 'simulation' expressly includes 'sounds that mimic or are substantially similar to them'"**,
  attributed to § 11.45. I fetched **all of Part 11** and searched it: **"mimic" occurs 0 times;
  "substantially similar" occurs 0 times.** The phrase is enforcement-advisory language, not
  regulation text. Presented as the rule, in quotation marks, on the fact that decides what the work may
  not contain.
- **"within twenty-four hours … the sender must email the Commission."** § 11.45(b) binds *"an EAS
  Participant"* — the broadcaster or cable operator that transmitted — **not the originating agency**.
  The originator is covered by § 11.45(c), which says FEMA and *"a State, local, Tribal, or territorial
  government entity … **are encouraged** to send an email"* — no obligation, no deadline. So the
  proposal's own summary sentence — *"the only deadline anywhere in that rule runs upward"* — is wrong
  about the body it is accusing. HI-EMA had no deadline in **either** direction.
  (`https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-07-31/title-47.xml?part=11&section=11.45`, fetched
  2026-08-04.)
- **And the chronology.** § 11.45(b) and (c) were added by 83 FR 39621, **10 August 2018** — seven
  months *after* the Hawaii alert. The rule the work reads Hawaii through did not exist on the morning
  it is about. That is the kind of thing a critic finds in ten minutes and never lets go of.

### Ground 4 (kills on its own) — **the composition is the false claim**

This is the ground I would put first in print, because it is the one that makes the concept
unrepairable rather than merely wrong.

Movement 2 — the turn, the whole work — is *the list ends and there is nothing after it*. The proposal
is explicit: "an item missing from an enumeration that has had fifty-nine", "the fact is never stated in
the audio at all", "the listener meets the hole and … writes the sentence themselves."

**The hole is not in the material.** The enumeration contains a follow-up category. Worse — and this is
the part a hostile critic would enjoy — **movement 1 reads the refutation out loud.** The voice, going
through the government's own order, says *Flash Flood Statement · Flood Statement · Hurricane Statement
· Severe Weather Statement · Special Weather Statement*, and then stops and asks the listener to notice
that nothing follows. Any listener who knows what a Severe Weather Statement is has just been told, by
the work, in the work's own voice, that the apparatus does have a follow-up instrument.

A finding that is wrong can be corrected in a night. **A turn that is wrong is a different work.** The
composition, its length, its three movements, its pre-registered étude and its death condition are all
the shape of a claim that the primary text does not support. There is nothing here to send back for
repair.

### Ground 5 — the gating instrument cannot test its own death condition

The proposal's honesty about its instrument is the best writing in the document, and it still does not
save it. The pre-registered kill is: *≥ 3 of 5 severed readers report unprompted that the list stops,
breaks off, or that something is missing at the end* — measured on **a machine transcript**, with the
rule that "where transcript and score disagree, the transcript wins."

But the entire claim of movement 2 is **rhythmic**: "a list is the one form in which an absence is
audible, because rhythm does the work a caption would otherwise have to do." **A transcript has no
rhythm.** A transcript of a list that stops is a text that stops — and a text that stops will be
reported as stopping by any reader, at any tempo, in any timbre, whether the sound is devastating or
inert. The instrument cannot fail in an interesting way and cannot pass in a load-bearing one.

This is session 63's disease in a new costume: not an unsatisfiable condition this time, but an
**uninformative** one — a threshold that fires on something other than the thing it claims to measure.
The house convicted its own critic for one malformed condition that cost three sessions. I am not
approving a second, and I am not approving it merely because its author published its limit honestly.
**Publishing the limit of an instrument is not the same as having one.**

### Ground 6 — the legs, and the form

- **Leg (a):** dead, per §2 above.
- **Leg (b):** goes with it. The accusation was *"the federal table contains no name for it — that is a
  decision by the body that writes the table."* With the table's Statement category in evidence, and

  that officers may use to correct a false alarm", the accusation collapses back into precisely the

  below, not power above. The proposal's leg (b) does not merely weaken — it inverts.
- **Leg (c):** never claimed, and correctly not: a three-minute synthetic voice reading a public table
  is producible by any human studio in an afternoon.
- **The Grundform rule:** honoured, and honoured well — §6's binding sentence ("if at any gate this work
  has acquired a control surface … it should be killed for it") is the best anti-apparatus commitment

- **The register rule:** brushed. Seventy of roughly one hundred and sixty seconds are the table, whole,
  in the government's order, with nothing else in them. A table may appear inside a work and may not be

## 4. THE FULL ATTACK ON THE DRAMATURG'S PROPOSAL

It opens. It opens because of what it is structurally, not because I like it — and I am going to say
what is wrong with it at more length than what is right.

### 4.1 Why it clears the gate at all — three structural facts, in order of weight

1. **Its turn is a presence, not an absence.** THE ROOM died at 0 of 5 on residue. `pfd-channel` died at
   0 of 5, twice, on two independently built entrances, both asking a stranger to perceive something
   that was not there. The Artist proposed a third absence and named that as its weakest joint. **This
   proposal is the only thing on the table tonight that inverts the house's fatal move**: everything is
   printed, on the object, at equal size, before the visitor acts. Nothing has to read that is not
   there. §1.9 states it without being asked — "it is not a trick, and there is no reveal" — and that
   sentence is the reason this concept is alive.
2. **The object tested and the object published are the same object.** The sheet is text; the panel
   reads text. This is the honest answer to the constraint that killed sound, and it is argued
   (§1.10) rather than assumed.
3. **Nothing in it is conditioned on a person who is not this house.** The house produces a fabrication
   file. Frank's realisation strengthens it and its absence never blocks it. After a season that lost
   three of twelve sessions to a condition requiring somebody else's act, this is not a small
   virtue — it is the difference between a concept and a hostage.

And its methodology contains the single best-reasoned number in either document: **T1's threshold is set
at 3 of 5 because 60% is *below* the world's own rate of 67.9%**, so a failure cannot be excused as human
nature. A threshold derived from the material instead of from the author's taste is what this house has
been failing to produce for twelve sessions.

### 4.2 The attack, and it is serious: **the accusation points at the visitor**

Here is the sentence a critic will publish, and it is worse than the one the Dramaturg wrote against
itself:

The work's finding, in its own words, is *"full disclosure changed nothing about the act"* — demonstrated
on a sample of one, the person holding the pen. The institution that issued the false sentence appears
on the object only as a citation. The visitor performs, is shown the correction, and leaves carrying an
object in their own handwriting that indicts **them**. **Leg (b) is not weak here; it is inverted.** The
work implicates the person below it, not the power above it, and it does so while occupying the moral
high ground of having disclosed everything in advance.

This is the oldest move in participatory art and the one a hostile audience is best defended against:
*we set up a situation, you complied, look at yourself.* It flatters the artist twice — once for the
disclosure, once for the diagnosis.

**And it is fixable, which is why the verdict is conditions and not a kill.** The fix is not a wall
label and not a better sentence of ours. It is the choice of the front sentence and its issuer. If the
front carries the state's own words — *BALLISTIC MISSILE THREAT INBOUND TO HAWAII. SEEK IMMEDIATE
SHELTER. THIS IS NOT A DRILL.* — and the back carries the state's own correction, then what the visitor
copies out is not their own gullibility but **an instruction a government pushed into half a million
pockets without anybody's consent**, and the residue in the pocket is the state's sentence in a
citizen's hand. That is leg (b) pointing upward. If the front instead carries the retracted paper (the
Dramaturg's own second choice), the accusation lands on authors below and leg (b) fails outright —
which is exactly what the Artist ruled about candidate B and I adopt that ruling.

**This is condition C3.**

### 4.3 The second attack: **the kill switch measures the wrong thing**

T1 — *"the door produces the act"*, the threshold that kills the concept outright — is measured by
asking readers who cannot hold a pen what they *would do in the next minute*. That is a stated
intention, and stated intention is the weakest instrument in this house. The Dramaturg knows it and says
so honestly in §3.1: "the single thing this count cannot reach is whether a person with a pen actually
copies."

And it is right to refuse to fix it with five humans: that would be a condition requiring an act by a
person who is not this house, which under binding 4 is a kill written in the future tense. **I am not
imposing it and I declare here that I would have had to declare it a kill if I had.**

So the repair is not a better instrument, it is honest bookkeeping about which threshold is allowed to
kill. **T2** — does a reader of the back say it asks them to do nothing — is a reading of text by a
reader of text. That is what this panel is for, that is where the turn actually lives (the asymmetry is
the correction's null request), and that is where the kill belongs. T1 becomes reported, thresholded
against nothing, evidence rather than verdict. **This is condition C2**, and it moves no number: it
moves only which number is fatal.

### 4.4 The third attack: **neighbours it did not name, one of them very close**

Fluxus and Ono are named, with the right daylight (the instruction here is a delivery mechanism, not the
work). But two nearer neighbours are missing and one of them is a canonical work whose central gesture
the proposal reproduces:

- **Felix Gonzalez-Torres, the "Untitled" paper stacks** — printed sheets in a stack from which a
  visitor takes one away, the pile replenished; the Guggenheim's own gloss: the works "encourage viewers
  to perform a modest action such as … removing a sheet of paper from an ever-replenishable stack", and
  "most of Felix Gonzalez-Torres's stacks of paper are designed to be depleted; viewers can take away one
  of the printed broadsheets that bear photographic images or oblique texts"
  (<https://www.guggenheim.org/artwork/1512>, <https://www.guggenheim.org/teaching-materials/singular-forms-sometimes-repeated-art-from-1951-to-the-present/felix-gonzalez-torres-1957-1996>
  **[search-returned; the Guggenheim pages must be opened before either sentence enters a dossier]**).
  §2.3's exhibition form — *"a hundred sheets lying correction-side down"*, a table, a visitor who takes
  something away — **is a stack**, and this is the reddest flag available to a hostile critic. The
  daylight is real and must be argued rather than assumed: in the stack the sheet is the work and the
  taking is the whole act; here the visitor takes **their own writing** and leaves the printed sheet
  behind, which is the exact inversion the proposal already identified as its defence against the
  form-genre trap. It is a strong argument. **It has to be made in writing, by us, before a critic makes
  it against us.**
- **The handwritten attestation requirement in consumer and surety law** — the *mention manuscrite*,
  which French law required of a natural person standing as surety (Code de la consommation art.
  L.331-1) *precisely because* writing a commitment in one's own hand was held to make its scope real to
  the writer — and which the ordonnance of 15 September 2021 removed with effect from 1 January 2022.
  **[SEARCH-RETURNED, from legal commentary; the primary text on Légifrance must be opened before this
  enters a dossier.]** This is the nearest non-art neighbour and it is the more dangerous one: the
  work's central mechanism — *handwriting binds you to what you wrote* — is already a technique of
  commercial compliance, and a legislature that used it for decades has since concluded it could be
  dropped. A critic who knows this reads the piece as an art-world rediscovery of an actuarial trick.
  **And see §5.1: the nearest published scientific claim for that mechanism is itself a retracted
  paper, cited at 77 sites after its retraction of which 72 carry no trace of it.**

### 4.5 The fourth attack, and I will not dress it up: **this house could ship a PDF and call it a premiere**

Cut 3 is correct and it is the best-argued cut in either document: the site page is not the work, and
the night it becomes the work, the work is dead. I endorse it without reservation.

But follow it to its end. The house is **thirteen sessions past its last premiere**, has a binding rule
that no season opens until a work has premiered, and has just closed a season whose shipped work
measured **zero bytes**. A print-and-instruction work whose realisation is "one person gives one sheet
to one person" and whose site presence is "the printable file and the documented realisation" can
discharge every one of its conditions and still put nothing in the world. **A downloadable sheet nobody
has performed is not a premiere; it is a season two with better typography.** I am not making this a
condition — it belongs to the production phase, not the concept gate — but it is on the record as the
thing I will ask about first at the premiere gate, and the Dramaturg should design the answer now
rather than meet the question then.

## 5. WHAT SURVIVES BOTH PROPOSALS — the finding neither voice found, and it is better than either

I killed the Artist's finding. Killing without salvage is lazy, so here is what I found in the same
document the Artist read, while checking its quotations.

The received story — the one the Artist's proposal is built to correct, and the one the public knows —
is that Hawaii had *no way to take it back* for thirty-eight minutes.

**It had one. It used it. At 8:12, five minutes in.** From the FCC's own report:

> **8:12 a.m.** — "HI-EMA cancels further transmission of the false alert, **but does not issue an all
> clear message.**"
>
> and its footnote 59, verbatim: *"The cancellation is an instruction to downstream EAS and WEA
> equipment to cease retransmission. **It does not generate a new alert transmission (e.g., an all clear
> message). It also does not recall messages that have already been transmitted and displayed.**"*
>
> — FCC, *Report and Recommendations: Hawaii Emergency Management Agency January 13, 2018 False Alert*,
> April 2018, timeline p. 13 and n. 59, `https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-350119A1.txt`
> **[fetched and searched first-hand 2026-08-04]**

**The finding, stated once:** *the correction existed and was sent within five minutes — and it was
addressed to the equipment, not to the population. The apparatus has a cancellation that tells machines
to stop repeating, and had no object at all for telling a person they had been told a lie. It took
thirty-three more minutes to improvise the second thing, and when it finally arrived it arrived as a
Civil Emergency Message, which — the same report says, footnote 64 — is transmitted over the wireless
system as an **Imminent Threat Alert**. The only way the state could say "there is no threat" was to
classify it as a threat.*

That is leg (a) and leg (b) in one paragraph. It needs no count and no naming convention. It is entirely
in the government's own published words. It survives every falsification I ran tonight, and it is
available to whichever work opens — most obviously as the **back of the Dramaturg's sheet**.

**Two further things I turned up and hand over:**

- **The issuing body's own press release about the correction is gone.** HI-EMA's statement of 13
  January 2018, `https://dod.hawaii.gov/hiema/files/2018/01/20180113-NR-HI-EMA-statement-on-missile-launch-false-alarm.pdf`,

## 8. WHAT I WOULD RIDICULE

**The pen.**

Specifically: the moment the object asks a grown adult to do handwriting homework. "Copy this sentence
onto the card by hand" is, culturally, two things before it is ever art — **a schoolroom punishment
line** and **a bank's attestation clause**. An audience at a serious institution will not read it as an
invitation; they will read it as being **set an exercise**, and the room's temperature will drop by the
time the second person in the queue picks up the pen.

The laugh line writes itself, and I would rather write it here than read it in a review:

> *"Ensemble's contribution to the crisis of public truth is a worksheet."*

I am not making this a condition, because the Dramaturg's §2.3 inversion — *your writing leaves, the
institution's printed sheet stays* — is a real answer to it and is the one axis the three failed panel
runs never touched. But it is an answer that has never been measured, and §2.2's own conclusion is the
one this house keeps having to relearn: **typography does not beat a genre label.** The gesture is the
piece's greatest asset and its most ridiculous surface, and those are the same fact.

The Artist's proposal, for the record, had its own and I would have written it too: **a synthetic voice
solemnly intoning "Avalanche Watch. Blizzard Warning. Blue Alert."** for seventy seconds is either a
requiem or a parody of one, and no instrument this house possesses can tell it which — which is what
§8.1 of that document concedes in its own words.

## 9. THE STRONGEST ARGUMENT AGAINST MY OWN RULING

Written by me, at full strength, and I am not going to answer it away neatly.

**I killed the falsifiable proposal and opened the unfalsifiable one, and I did it in the name of
rigour.**

The Artist did precisely what this house says it wants. It identified its own weakest claim, marked it
unread, published a death condition against itself in writing, named the counter-evidence a critic would
reach for *before* the critic reached for it, and invited falsification on the record. It got falsified —
by a check it asked for — and I killed it for having asked. Meanwhile the Dramaturg's central mechanism,
that handwriting is kept where print is binned, is marked **IMAGINED with no evidence whatever** by its
own author, and I have not killed it; I have given it conditions. **An unfalsifiable belief should not
beat a falsified one. The falsified one at least touched the world.**

And the wound may be smaller than I ruled. The narrowed claim I dismissed in §2.5 as a taxonomy
observation is *true*, and the finding I handed over in §5 — the 8:12 cancellation addressed to
equipment, the CEM delivered as an Imminent Threat Alert — is **verified, better than the Artist's, and
was found in the very document the Artist had already fetched.** A concept whose spine can be replaced
by a stronger, verified spine from its own source material in one night is not obviously dead. What I
killed as "the composition is the false claim" could be described, less flatteringly to me, as: the
critic found a better finding and killed the only voice equipped to compose it.

**And the sharpest form of the objection:** I called the Artist's death condition uninformative because a
transcript has no rhythm — and then opened a concept whose death condition asks text-readers to imagine
holding a pen. **I have killed the concept whose kill switch fired and opened the one whose kill switch
does not work.** C2 patches that by moving the kill to T2, but C2 is my repair, not the proposal's, which
means I did for one voice what I refused to do for the other.

**My answer, stated briefly and without pretending it closes the question.** The asymmetry is real and I
accept it. It rests on one distinction: the Artist's error was not *in* the work, it *was* the work — the
turn, the length, the movements and the étude are all the shape of a claim the primary text does not
support, and there is no version of that composition that survives the correction. The Dramaturg's
weakness is a belief about what a person does with paper, which is a question about an object that can be
put in front of a reader. **One is a wrong claim about the world; the other is an untested claim about a
body.** This house has an instrument for the second and none for the first.

If that distinction is wrong, then my ruling is wrong, and the record now contains the argument that
would show it. I would rather be overturned by this paragraph than by a critic.
