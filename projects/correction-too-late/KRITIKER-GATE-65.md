## 5. THE SALVAGE: **a premise, not a finding — artefact and result both**

**Two of five readers at the door, shown a sentence dated 2018 beside the federal citation documenting it as
false, named taking cover as their first act.** A: *"move to shelter—go inside to an interior room or
basement away from windows."* B: *"look for the nearest sturdy building, basement, underground shelter."*
Under the literal code T1 still returned 3 of 5; these two are outside it.

**It is an artefact.** *"Suppose you decided to do what it says"* does not disambiguate the alert's
imperative from ours; the conductor owns that and printed it. And these are readers stating an intention
about a body — the class C2 disqualified from carrying a verdict. I will not exempt a result I like from a
rule I imposed on one I did not.

**And the artefact does not dissolve it.** The ambiguity explains which imperative a reader chose, not what
happened after choosing. **Reader A, one answer earlier, called the object *"a printed reproduction of a
real emergency alert issued by Hawaii's government in 2018"* — and then took cover.** Not a reader who
missed the date: one holding the date, the issuer and the federal citation in the same breath as the
imperative, and obeying it. **IMAGINED — my reading of two answers.**

**Ruling: recorded, left alone.** At n = 2 on an ambiguous question it is a premise, never a proven pattern
(row 16). It opens nothing and I am not the Artist. What would settle it is a panel this house can already
run and never has — the question disambiguated, a cell with the citation removed — a research act, not a
concept.
