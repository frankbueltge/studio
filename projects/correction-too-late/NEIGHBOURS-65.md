## (i) WHO THE OBJECT ACCUSES

**The finished object accuses the issuer — the state that pushed an imperative into half a million pockets
and then had no object to send after it — but only if the visitor's hand is put in the *sender's* chair
rather than the believer's, and as staged in `DRAMATURG-64.md` tonight it is in the believer's.**

**From the body.** A pen does not stage belief. *BALLISTIC MISSILE THREAT INBOUND TO HAWAII. SEEK
IMMEDIATE SHELTER. THIS IS NOT A DRILL.* (`FRONT-BACK-65.md` §1, held verbatim) is an **imperative**, not
a proposition. Writing it out is not agreeing with it; it is **issuing** it. For twenty seconds the
visitor does what the warning officer did — selects a sentence, transmits it, cannot recall it — then
turns the sheet and finds the apparatus produced nothing that reaches the copy. Not *you complied, look at
yourself*, but *you did what the apparatus does; now find the object that takes it back.*
**IMAGINED — my reading, and (i) rests on it.**

**From the pocket.** A card carrying an unattributed sentence in one hand has one identifiable party on
it: the writer; handwriting reads as signature. **Four binding staging decisions, checkable on the

2. **The issuing body and the date are copied in the same hand as the sentence.** The decision the answer

## (ii) THE DAYLIGHT FROM THE PAPER STACKS

**DEVIATION D1, DECLARED NOT DISCOVERED.** C3(ii) required the Guggenheim pages *opened rather than
search-returned*. **This house could not open them on 2026-08-04**: HTTP 200, but the body is
JavaScript-gated and returned only a cookie banner; moma.org returned HTTP 403. The two Guggenheim
sentences at `KRITIKER-GATE-64.md` §4.4 therefore **remain SEARCH-RETURNED, and are neither used below nor
repeated**. Substituted, opened first-hand 2026-08-04, HTTP 200: the artist's own foundation's catalogue
of the stacks, <https://www.felixgonzalez-torresfoundation.org/works/c/paper-stacks>, which describes them
in the **medium line itself** — CR# 78 *(Loverboy)*, 1990: *"Blue paper, endless supply / 7 1/2 inches at
ideal height x 29 x 23 inches (original paper size)"*; CR# 100 *(Death by Gun)*, CR# 119 *(Ross in L.A.)*,
CR# 125 *(We Don't Remember)* and CR# 93 *(NRA)*: *"Print on paper, endless copies / … at ideal height …"*
(CR# 93 on red paper). **The next gate rules on this substitution.**

Three facts inside those medium lines carry the argument: **endless copies / endless supply**; an **ideal
height** in place of an edition size; the sheet that leaves is **the artist's printed sheet**.

1. **Authorship runs the other way.** In the stack the pocket takes the artist's printed sheet. Here the
   printed sheet **stays** and the pocket takes the visitor's own hand: the stack distributes its author's
   object; this table withholds its printed object and distributes only what the visitor made.
2. **A quantity that is a state, against a quantity that is one.** *Endless supply* and *ideal height*
   specify not stock but a condition the work is **returned to** — restoration is written into the
   dimensions, and the taking is absorbed without loss. The card is neither endless nor restored: one
   hand, one instance, no counterpart. And the correction meant to catch it has no distribution at all —
   one of it, on the table, never travelling.

**Tested against `DRAMATURG-64.md` §2.3 — *"a hundred sheets lying correction-side down" IS a stack* — and
on that reading the neighbour swallows the work.** It does, on one condition, and the condition decides:
**a pile replenished to a height is a stack with our text on it, whatever we say about the card.** So the
staging binds — **the sheet is handed over, one person to one person** (§1.8's own realisation form);
**what lies on the table is what people have left, not a supply awaiting removal; nothing is replenished
during a showing.** A stack diminishes toward its ideal height and is restored upward; this pile
**grows**, and what accumulates in it is the correction, unread. Set out a self-service pile to be taken
from and the house has built a stack. **Verdict: the daylight holds, conditionally, on that binding.**

## (iii) THE DAYLIGHT FROM THE HANDWRITTEN ATTESTATION (*mention manuscrite*)

Primary text opened first-hand 2026-08-04: **Code de la consommation, article L331-1**,
<https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000032225694> — version in force **1 July 2016 to
1 January 2022**, **abrogated by Ordonnance n°2021-1192 du 15 septembre 2021, art. 32 (V)**:

> *"Toute personne physique qui s'engage par acte sous seing privé en qualité de caution envers un
> créancier professionnel fait précéder sa signature de la mention manuscrite suivante et uniquement de
> celle-ci : « En me portant caution de X…, dans la limite de la somme de… couvrant le paiement du
> principal, des intérêts et, le cas échéant, des pénalités ou intérêts de retard et pour la durée de…,
> je m'engage à rembourser au prêteur les sommes dues sur mes revenus et mes biens si X… n'y satisfait
> pas lui-même. »"*

**Closer than Fluxus**, because it is a neighbour of the **gesture** and not of the instruction: a citizen
compelled to copy a prescribed sentence, *and only that one*, in their own hand, before signing, to be
bound by it.

**The direction of the copy is the daylight.** The prescribed sentence is **first-person and
performative** — *je m'engage à rembourser* — so copying it **creates** an obligation running from the
writer. Ours is a **second-person imperative** issued by a state to the copier, and copying it creates
nothing: no duty, no counterparty, nothing enforceable. One hand-copy makes the writer an obligor; the
other records a command that ran to the writer. **That is the razor for §(i), and why candidate B fails
leg (b) structurally rather than by taste.**

**And the prescribed sentence is the exact negative of the object's finding.** `DRAMATURG-64.md` §1.4: a
correction lacks the three things an instruction has — addressee, verb, deadline. This hand-copy carries
all three as blanks to be filled: a creditor (*envers un créancier professionnel*), a promise (*je
m'engage à rembourser*), a ceiling and a term (*dans la limite de la somme de… et pour la durée de…*).
The law knew exactly how to build a handwritten text that binds; the correction on our back is what the
same machinery produces when it binds no one.

**What it reveals that the author did not intend — three threats.** **(1)** *Writing it in your own hand
makes it real to you* is not an artistic finding but a **compliance technology**, prescribed by statute
and used for decades by creditors against consumers; the object may never present it as a discovery, and
the dossier must carry this neighbour rather than let it be found. **(2)** The legislator trusted neither
reading nor signature alone — inferable from the text's construction: exact words prescribed, all others
forbidden, hand required, placed *before* the signature [**IMAGINED — my reading, not a claim about
legislative intent**]. So the belief marked IMAGINED at §3.6 has an institutional precedent outweighing it
in scale, and cannot be claimed by us. **(3) The state abolished it.** A critic will read the abrogation
as a verdict on the device and call the piece an art-world rediscovery of an actuarial trick the
legislature had already dropped [**the critic's reading; I hold the version dates and the abrogating
instrument as printed above, not the ordonnance's grounds**]. It also sources the ridicule at
`KRITIKER-GATE-64.md` §8 — *a bank's attestation clause*.

**Verdict: the neighbour holds, and it costs** — first person against imperative, binding against
recording, a counterparty against none — but only while the front is a state's order carrying its issuer.
**Both neighbours converge on §(i), and that is this page's one result: the whole defence of this object
against its two nearest neighbours is the direction of the sentence the visitor copies. If that sentence
becomes a proposition, all three sections fail at once.**
