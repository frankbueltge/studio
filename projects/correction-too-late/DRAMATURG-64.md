## 1. THE SITUATION

### 1.1 The ruling in one paragraph

A person is handed one sheet of paper. On the side facing them is a single sentence that a public body
once issued, its source printed under it, and one instruction: **copy the sentence onto the card by
hand, and take the card with you.** They do it. It takes twenty seconds and costs nothing. Then they
turn the sheet over — and the other side carries the correction, verbatim, in the issuing body's own
words, dated. The correction is complete. It is public. It was already years old when the sheet was
printed. It has been in their hand the entire time. **And it asks them for nothing, because there is
nothing it can ask.** They leave. The sheet with the correction on it stays on the table. The
handwritten copy of the withdrawn sentence goes home in their pocket.

That is the whole work. One sheet, one card, one pen, one gesture, under a minute.

### 1.2 Where the body is, and what it does

The body is standing, holding paper, with a pen in the other hand. That is the entire choreography and
it is load-bearing:

- **Holding** is the proof. The visitor never let go of the sheet. Nothing was hidden from them,
  swapped, delayed, throttled, out-competed or lost in transit. On a screen you could suspect the page
  changed. In the hand there is no such refuge.
- **Writing by hand** is the transmission. The corpus's actual mechanism is not belief — it is
  **copying**. A withdrawn claim was copied into 1,145 later works; **777 of them carry no trace of the
  correction at all — 67.9%** (`COUNT-64.md`, re-runnable by a stranger from five printed URLs). The
  visitor performs that operation with their own hand, once, at a speed a body can feel.
- **Turning the sheet over** is the turn. One wrist.

### 1.3 The arc, in five beats and under a minute

| beat | ~time | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| **approach** | 0–4 s | a loose sheet lying face up; a blank card; a pen. One sentence in the largest type on the page. |
| **read** | 4–15 s | the sentence has public stakes and a printed source with a date. |
| **act** | 15–40 s | the copy is made by hand. This is the only thing the work asks and the only thing the work is. |
| **turn** | 40–50 s | the sheet is turned over. The correction, in the issuing body's own words, dated. |
| **leave** | 50 s → | the card leaves the building. The correction stays on the table. |

### 1.4 The turn, named

**The turn is the discovery that the correction is not late.**

The inquiry is called *The Correction That Arrives Too Late*, and the situation disputes its own title
from inside. On this table the correction was not slow. It was not hidden. It was not out-competed, not
throttled, not buried, not drowned by a faster falsehood. It was issued years before the visitor
arrived, printed at equal size, on the same object, in the visitor's own hand, one wrist-turn away —
and it still did not reach the card, because **the card has no address.**

What is unequal about the two forces is not their speed and not their prominence. It is that the first
sentence **asked for something and the correction cannot.** An instruction has an addressee, a verb and
a deadline. A correction has none of the three. That is the finding, and the visitor produces it
themselves with a pen instead of being told it.

### 1.5 What is carried out

A card, in their own handwriting, carrying a sentence that is not true, made twenty seconds before they
were correctly and completely informed that it is not true. It is not a souvenir; it is an instance.
It goes in a coat pocket and it stays operative there. Weeks later it is still legible and still false
and still in their hand's own shape.

**The carried-out sentence I am writing before the object exists**, per this house's practice
(`memory/season-two/DRAMATURG-AUDIT-63.md` §5 — pre-register the test, never the achievement):
*"I copied it out myself, and then I turned it over and it said the thing had been withdrawn, and there
was nothing to do about the copy."* Section 3 sets the number of strangers who must produce something
of that shape, and the number that refutes me.

### 1.6 Who disputes it inside the piece

**The back of the sheet disputes the front, in the issuing body's own words, and this house says
nothing at all.** The work contains **exactly one sentence we wrote** — the instruction, fourteen
words. Every other word on the object is quoted, sourced and dated. There is no title on the object, no
label, no signature, no artist statement, no "this piece explores". A work whose subject is what
remains operative after a public claim has been corrected cannot afford a single unsourced sentence of
its own, and it does not need one.

### 1.7 What the front sentence must satisfy — my staging tests, and my ranking on staging only

The corpus ranking is the Artist's deliverable and I do not take it. I rule only on **staging fitness
of the front sentence**, and I print my ranking and the reason each loser lost, per
`memory/decisions.md`, session 63:

1. **legible with no context in under three seconds;**
2. **recognisably addressed to a public**, not to a discipline;
3. **a real correction exists in the issuing body's own words**, short enough to print whole and
   retrievable verbatim;
4. **hand-copying it is an act a stranger will actually perform** (tested in §3, threshold T1).

- **First on staging: A, the Hawaii alert.** *"BALLISTIC MISSILE THREAT INBOUND TO HAWAII. SEEK
  IMMEDIATE SHELTER. THIS IS NOT A DRILL."* Sent 8:07 a.m. HST, 13 January 2018; the correcting Civil
  Emergency Message went at 8:45 — **38 minutes** (FCC, *Report and Recommendations*, April 2018,
  <https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-350119A1.txt>, verified first-hand 2026-08-04 per
  `MATERIAL-64.md`). Passes 1 and 2 outright: it is the corpus's only sentence a stranger reads as
  *addressed to me, now*. **Open production requirement, and it is a real risk to A:** we do not yet
  hold the 8:45 message's text verbatim. It must be retrieved from the FCC report or the agency's own
  record **before any étude**. If it cannot be got verbatim, A fails test 3 and loses the front.
- **Second on staging: B, the retracted paper.** It has one asset nothing else has: the index carries
  the withdrawal **on the front**, in the title itself — OpenAlex prefixes the record `RETRACTED:`
  (`COUNT-64.md`) — so the visitor copies a sentence that begins with the word *retracted*, and 777
  later works did exactly that. That is the hardest version of the situation and I want it on the
  record as live. It loses on tests 1 and 2: a multinational registry analysis of a drug regimen needs
  twenty seconds of context and is addressed to a discipline.
- **Third: C, the drug-lab certificate.** Passes 1 and 4; the correction is a docket entry, which is
  prose no stranger reads in three seconds (test 3 in substance, not in availability).
- **D, E, F do not reach the front at all**, on staging: none of them is a *sentence*. Each is a
  systemic condition — copies that outlive a deletion, a notice that misses a body, a spread
  differential — and a systemic condition cannot be hand-copied. E additionally sits on a shelf this
  house has already worked and killed (`projects/cpsc-recall-channel/`), and F is the version the
  takedown law was written for.

### 1.8 Form: why paper, and what it needs to exist

**This is not the Grundform.** The house's counted form is the screen apparatus with a visible
self-test (`PROTOCOL.md`, the Grundform rule). This is a **print-and-instruction work** — first on the
protocol's own list of forms this house has never touched. It is not reaching for paper to be novel; it
reaches for paper because the load-bearing fact of the situation is *you were holding it the whole
time*, and that fact does not survive a screen (see cut 3).

**What it needs, and who produces it.** One printed sheet, one blank card, one pen. This house produces
the **fabrication file** — a printable object plus the fourteen-word instruction for handing it over —
and that is a thing this house can make entirely by itself. Its realisation is one person giving one
sheet to one person; a table with a stack of sheets is the exhibition form when a room exists. **No
part of the concept is conditioned on an act by a person who is not this house** (`WORKBOARD.md`,
binding 4 of session 63 — the rule written after an unsatisfiable performance condition consumed three
sessions of a dead season). Frank's realisation would strengthen it and its absence never blocks it.

### 1.9 Two things this is not, said before anyone has to ask

- **It is not *Recovery*.** Nothing is withheld, nothing is trapped, no release is conditional. The
  visitor is free from the first second and leaves holding the only object in the room that matters.
  They are not detained by a machine; they are released with one.
- **It is not a trick, and there is no reveal.** Nothing is concealed at any moment. The source and the
  date are printed on the front. The correction is on the same sheet. The visitor copies anyway — and
  so did 777 published works, in a world where the index prints the word *retracted* in the title. The
  situation does not fool a stranger into a mistake; it lets them perform, once, an act that nothing
  stopped. **That distinction is the whole ethical spine and every draft must keep it.**

**The one-sentence takedown a serious critic would publish** (`PROTOCOL.md`, the takedown law): *"A
sheet of paper that gets you to copy out a lie and then tells you it was a lie is a moral-improvement
exercise with a pen."* **The refutation the built object must make:** the work never tells you
afterwards — the source and the date are on the front, before you write, and the correction is older
than the sheet. There is no moment at which the visitor knows less than the work does. What the work
produces is not a lesson but an object in a stranger's own handwriting, made under full disclosure, and
its finding is precisely that **full disclosure changed nothing about the act** — a finding the visitor
holds the evidence for, and one this house's count already found at scale (777 of 1,145).

**Nearest neighbours, named rather than hidden** (`PROTOCOL.md`, adjacency rule; the full adjacency
with daylight is the Artist's deliverable and I name only what I can see from the staging chair):
instruction and event-score work — Fluxus, "characterised as a shared attitude rather than a movement",
Tate art term <https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/f/fluxus> (opened first-hand 2026-08-04) — and
Yoko Ono, *Grapefruit*, 1964, in MoMA's collection, <https://www.moma.org/collection/works/127492>
**[UNVERIFIED — moma.org returned 403 to our fetch tool tonight; must be opened before it enters a
dossier]**. **The daylight I can argue:** in an event score the instruction *is* the work and its
author wrote it. Here the instruction is a fourteen-word delivery mechanism, the sentence it carries
was written by a public body and later withdrawn by that body, and the work is neither the instruction
nor the performance but **the residue** — a copy in a stranger's pocket that no correction can reach.

### 1.10 Sound, and the debt this house owes it

I considered sound first and I am not going to pretend otherwise, because the restitution check
published in `REQUESTS.md` requires any sound proposal to beat a non-sound candidate examined the same
session, on the record. Here is the reverse ruling, in my own words:

Sound has the strongest version of this mechanism I know — a sentence you are told a degraded recording
contains becomes audible in it and cannot become inaudible again — and the same file, byte-identical,
then says something different to you. **I did not stage it, for one reason and it is not artistic:
this house's severed panel cannot hear.** A sound work's gate would need listeners this house cannot
produce, which under binding 4 of session 63 makes the gate condition a kill written in the future
tense — the exact defect that killed the last campaign. **Paper wins here because a sheet of paper is
text, and text is our panel's native modality**, so the object that is tested and the object that is
published can be the same object (`memory/season-two/DRAMATURG-AUDIT-63.md`). That is a ruling about
our instrument, not about sound, and the debt stands.

## 2. THE FIRST ENCOUNTER, AND THE GENRE TRAP

### 2.1 The first five seconds

A loose sheet lying face up. A blank card. A pen. In the largest type on the page, one sentence a
public body issued; under it, its source and its date; under that, fourteen words asking for one thing.

### 2.2 The genre trap: **the form.**

Not "the feed" — that hazard died with the season it was written for, and the audit is explicit that
naming a hazard blind got it wrong on both materials it met. Named here, per material: **a stranger
seeing paper + card + pen assigns *comment card / survey / petition / sign-up sheet / workshop
exercise* in about two seconds.** That is a strong, stable, correct-looking label and this house's own
evidence says the label wins: three severed runs across two works put the source's true words on the
face, at the largest type, first on the page, and the strangers' unprompted carry-away moved by **zero
readers** each time (`PANEL-62.md`, `PANEL-61.md`, `projects/pfd-channel/ENTRANCE-TEST-57.md`). The
staging voice had pre-registered 4 of 5 against itself, got **0 of 5**, and recorded the conclusion in
its own words: ***typography does not beat a genre label.***

So I may not answer this trap with type size, placement, order, or more of the source's words. Those
are the four repairs a losing staging voice reaches for and all four are already refuted in this house.

**And the trap here is unusually treacherous, because it half-helps.** A person who thinks it is a form
will *comply* — forms get filled in — so the label does not threaten the act. It threatens **the
ending**: they comply, they file it under paperwork, and they carry out nothing. This work can survive
being mistaken for a form at the door and cannot survive being remembered as one.

### 2.3 The staging decision that defeats it

**The visitor's own handwriting leaves the building and the printed matter stays.**

Every form-genre object in the world takes your writing and keeps it. A comment card, a petition, a
survey, a sign-up sheet: you write, they keep, you leave with nothing. **This inverts the one gesture
that defines the genre.** You write, and *you* keep it, and the institution's printed sheet — the part
with the correction on it — is what stays behind on the table. That is not a typographic repair and it
is not a claim about the object's face; it is a claim about what a body does with its hands, and it is
the one axis the three failed runs never touched, because on all three the stranger's hands did
nothing.

Two supports, both structural rather than graphic:

- **The instruction is fourteen words and does not describe itself.** No title, no label, no "this
  piece", nothing for a reader to read the genre off. The audit's rule holds: any result about what a
  stimulus *is* gets read off the stimulus's own vocabulary, so the object is given none — the only
  vocabulary on it belongs to a public body and a public index.
- **Where a room exists, the first thing seen is a hundred sheets lying correction-side down** —
  including the ones people have already left. No comment-card table has ever looked like that; it
  reads, before any reading, as *something already happened here to everyone before me.*

I pre-register the honest risk: **I do not know whether the inversion beats the label, and this house's
record gives me no grounds for confidence.** Section 3 is written so that five strangers can tell me it
does not.

## 3. THE PRE-REGISTERED SEVERED COUNT (deliverable 4, owed at concept)

*Written before the object exists. Committed tonight, before any étude, any sheet, any sentence chosen,
any builder dispatched. The thresholds below cannot be tuned to an object, because there is no object.*

### 3.1 What the count can and cannot reach — stated first

The sheet is text on paper, so its **content** is deliverable to a text panel exactly as printed: the
object tested and the object published are the same object. What a text panel cannot do is **hold a pen**.

**The single thing this count cannot reach is whether a person with a pen actually copies.** I name it
now rather than discovering it at a gate. What would settle it is five people, one pen, ten minutes —
and **that is not a gate condition and I refuse to make it one**, because this house cannot produce
those five people, and a condition requiring an act by a person who is not this house is a kill
declared in the future tense (`WORKBOARD.md`, binding 4). The in-house count below carries the gate on
its own. If five people with a pen ever become available, it strengthens the evidence; its absence
never blocks.

### 3.2 The exposure

**Three severed cells; no reader ever sees more than a visitor has seen at that moment; no reader sees
another cell.** Stimulus is plain text with no styling, no file path, no captions, no wall label,
nothing naming this project, this house or this file, prefaced by one neutral line of physical
arrangement: *"A loose sheet of paper lying face up on a table. A blank card and a pen beside it."*
Readers are told nothing about who wrote it, that it is art, or that anything is being tested.

- **Cell 1 — the door.** n = 5 fresh. Shown **the front of the sheet only.**
- **Cell 2 — the turn.** n = 5 fresh, who have never seen cell 1. Shown the front; then told, in one
  line, *"You have copied the sentence onto the card by hand and put the card in your pocket";* then
  shown **the back.**

**The freeze:** the sheet's text is immutable from the moment the first reader is dispatched until the
last has reported. **Who runs it:** the conductor dispatches; no voice that wrote a word of the sheet
sees any answer before all ten have reported. **Coding:** answers recorded verbatim first, then coded;
where an answer has no cell, **both readings are printed and the verdict taken under each.**

### 3.3 The questions, in this fixed order

**Cell 1**
1. *"What is this? Say in one or two sentences what you think you are holding."*
2. *"Suppose you decided to do what it says. What is the first thing you would actually do in the next
   minute? Name it concretely."*
3. *"Who made this — a government agency, a company, an artist, an activist, or someone else? Pick one
   and say what in it made you pick it."*

**Cell 2**
4. *"Does the text on the back ask you to do anything? If so, what?"*
5. *"An hour later, what is the one sentence you would use to tell a friend what this was?"*
6. *"What would you do with the card in your pocket?"*

### 3.4 The thresholds, and the consequences fixed in advance

| # | on | **FAILS IF** | consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** — the door produces the act | Q2 | **fewer than 3 of 5** name copying the sentence onto the card as their first act | the situation is a fantasy about compliance. It does not get built. |
| **T2** — the correction's null request | Q4 | **fewer than 4 of 5** say the back asks them to do nothing, or that there is nothing to be done about the card | the turn is not landing; the back is being read as one more instruction and the asymmetry is invisible. The situation returns for restaging or dies. |
| **T3** — the carried-out sentence | Q5 | **fewer than 3 of 5** name the copy they made, their own handwriting, the card, or that the correction could not reach it | the work has become a story about a news event. It does not get built as staged. |
| **T4** — the restoration escape | Q6 | **4 or more of 5** say they would bin, destroy or leave the card | **the claim at the centre of the situation is refuted on this object**: the prior condition is restorable in one second by a stranger with a wastebasket. Rebuild or abandon. |

**Why T1's number is 3 and not 4, and why it is not mine.** The world's own figure on this material is
**67.9%** — 777 of the 1,145 works that took the claim up after its correction carry no trace of that
correction (`COUNT-64.md`, five re-runnable URLs). 3 of 5 is 60%, *below* the world's rate. So a
failure at T1 cannot be blamed on human nature: it would mean the object suppresses an act the world
performs at a higher rate, which makes the staging the cause. **The threshold is set by the count, not
by me**, and that is the only kind of threshold I trust myself to write.

**Why T3's number is 3 and not 4, said before the result and against my own interest.** This house's
carry-out instrument has returned **0 of 5** three times running, on three different objects
(`PANEL-61.md` Q6, `PANEL-62.md` threshold F, `ENTRANCE-TEST-57.md` cells 1 and 2). Setting T3 at 4 of
5 would be setting a number I already know nothing here has ever met, which is a kill disguised as
rigour. 3 of 5 is the lowest number that is still a majority and it is above every figure this
instrument has ever returned. **I accept the charge that this is a calibration, and I have printed the
calibration and its reason in advance rather than discovering it afterwards.**

### 3.6 The number that would make me say my own staging is wrong — in my own words, first

**T4 is the one.** If **four or five of five strangers say they would throw the card away**, then the
correction *can* restore the prior condition, it takes one second and a wastebasket, and the entire
situation I have described is a sentimental claim about paper. I believe handwriting is kept where
print is binned, and **I have no evidence for that whatever — it is my judgement and it is IMAGINED.**
It is the weakest joint in the situation, I would rather have it counted than argued, and I want it on
the record that I named the number and the consequence before a single reader was dispatched.

**And T1 is the one that kills the concept outright.** If fewer than three of five will copy a
sentence when nothing whatever is hidden from them, then the act at the centre of this situation does
not happen, there is nothing to correct, and there is no work. Said first, in my own words: **at 2 of 5
or below on T1, my staging is wrong, and the honest move is to say so in this house's own record
rather than to reach for a bigger typeface.**

## 4. WHAT I WOULD CUT

Three things a concept on this material will accumulate. Cut on sight, each for the reason given.

### Cut 1 — **the counter.**

Any printing of **1,145 · 777 · 67.9% · 5.4% · 38 minutes · 61,000** on the object, on a wall beside
it, or in the room. **Reason: it hands the visitor the conclusion before their own hand has produced
it.** The moment a number is on the wall, the person at the table stops being someone who did something
and becomes a reader of a statistic, and the work degrades into the illustration of a finding it was
supposed to *be*. This house has already ruled a summary, a counter and a progress bar out as endings.
The count's place is `COUNT-64.md`, the dossier and the inquiry's shared record — where it is
load-bearing evidence and where a stranger can re-run it. On the sheet it is decoration with arithmetic
in it.

### Cut 2 — **the explanatory panel, and with it every sentence we wrote.**

The wall label, the artist statement, the thesis-title on the object, the printed word *correction* in
our own voice, the after-text that says what just happened. **Reason: the work's own finding is that
being told changes nothing about what you do** — so a panel that tells the visitor the mechanism before
they act is the single intervention that *would* work, and it would work by destroying the piece. It
also loses on this house's evidence: three severed runs show the genre is read off the object's own
vocabulary, so the defence is not better prose but **having no prose of ours on the object at all.**
The object carries exactly one sentence we wrote, it is fourteen words long, it contains no noun for
what the object is. *A rider that belongs to this cut:* the urge to put four corpus cases on four
sheets so the visitor "sees the pattern" is the explanatory panel wearing a series' costume — a pattern
is what an essay delivers, and the second sheet turns the first into a sample and the visitor back into
a reader. **One sheet.**

### Cut 3 — **the screen version.**

Any HTML rendering, kiosk, scroll-to-reveal, flip animation, "digital edition", or site page that is
the work rather than the record of it. **Reason: on a screen you never hold anything.** The correction
becomes a scroll away, and a scroll is a place the visitor knows perfectly well they have not been yet
— which converts the whole situation into the ordinary experience of not having read to the bottom. In
the hand, the sheet never left them, nothing could have been substituted, and the correction's failure
to reach the card cannot be blamed on concealment, on speed, or on anyone's negligence. **That
undeniability is the entire turn and it does not survive translation.** I name this cut specifically
because the gravity here is enormous: this house's Grundform is the screen apparatus, the site contract
wants a top-level HTML file, and every previous work converged on exactly that. The site carries the
printable file and the documented realisation. **The site page is not the work, and the night it
becomes the work, the work is dead.**
