## 3. THE PLACEMENT QUESTION, ANSWERED

**At the head, immediately before the first name, and again at the foot. Both instances identical.
Nothing else on the page is repeated.**

The hazard the ruling names is real and it is precise: *a line at the entrance in a different register
becomes a wall label, and a work that must explain itself has conceded.* I answer it on the four
things that make a wall label a wall label, and the placement is designed against each.

1. **A label is in the studio's voice. This is not.** Both head sentences are the state's, retrievable
   at their own URLs, and both are about coroners and recipients — never about this page. Nothing at
   the entrance says *source*, *note*, *about*, or *how to read this*.
2. **A label is typographically subordinate or superior — smaller, greyer, italic, boxed, ruled off.
   This is not.** Identical face, size, colour, leading and measure as the 63 duty sentences, with no
   rule, box, indent or italic anywhere near it. If it cannot be told from the body text by any
   typographic means, it is body text.
3. **A label appears once, at the entrance. This appears twice.** That is the decisive move and it is
   why I reject head-only. Nobody repeats an explanation at the foot; a sentence that stands at both
   ends of a sequence is a frame, and a reader who meets it again after fourteen thousand pixels meets
   it as a conclusion they have earned, not as an instruction they were given.
4. **A label explains the encoding. Neither of these does.** They do not tell the reader what the rule
   means, what the marks are, or how long is long. They state two facts about the world that happen to
   be the whole content: *the answer did not come*, and *nothing can be done about it*.

**Why not foot-only.** Because the ruling's own entrance protocol judges the first viewport at 390 px
with everything else covered, and the work's own death condition lives there. A sentence at the foot of
a 14,407-pixel page is not on the face for the purposes of the only test anyone will run, and clauses 3
and 4 of the carried-out sentence would go undelivered in the seconds where the reading is decided.

**Why not head-only.** A single sentence standing alone above forty-nine entries has exactly one
available reading, and it is *this is the explanation*. Repetition is what removes that reading, at
zero cost.

**The falsifier, pre-committed before any stimulus, and it is mine to lose.** The severed entrance
test's genre card gains three options: *an explanation · an introduction · a note*. **If two or more of
the five readers at 390 px name the head lines as any of those three, the head placement is struck,
both sentences move to the foot alone, and the entrance test is re-run before anything opens.** I am
not asking the staging voice to take this on argument; the build renders both placements this session
and the stills decide.

## 7. WHAT I DO NOT CONCEDE

Five, each with the evidence, and none of them a refusal to do the work.

**(i) The no-power line, as the ruling prints it, is a truncation that changes what the source says —
and it is this house's own named failure mode.** The source sentence, as `MATERIAL-2026-08-01.md` §1
carries it and the Verifier re-fetched it live, is: *"There is no power authorising a coroner to take
any steps **if they receive an inadequate or vague reply**… the coroner has no authority in law to take
any further steps."* The Verifier records that the ellipsis joins **§47 and §48** of the guidance and
that the elided material is *"…but as the inquest has concluded…"*. **§47's sentence is about a reply
that came and was inadequate. This work is about a reply that never came.** Printing *"There is no
power authorising a coroner to take any steps"* with a full stop after *steps* takes a conditional
sentence about bad answers and prints it as an unconditional sentence about no answers. That is one
instance generalised — the exact error that put a 21-month clock on this board, and the exact error
that put a maximum of 13 in the material file, both caught in this dossier within one night.

**I comply with binding condition 2 and contest only the words.** My recommendation, on the record
this project holds: the face carries **§48's sentence**, which is the one about a non-responder — the
Verifier confirms §48 also contains *"the non-responder is now in breach of Reg 29(3) and Schedule 5
part 7(2)"* — and it is printed complete, with no ellipsis and no full stop we supplied. **Owed before
unit 1, with a runner: re-open
`https://www.judiciary.uk/guidance-and-resources/chapter-16-reports-to-prevent-future-deaths-pfds/`,
read §§47–48 whole, and take the shortest complete sentence in them that is about a body which did not
answer.** If no such sentence exists, clause 4 of the carried-out sentence is cut and nothing stands in
its place. I will not print an ellipsis on an artwork whose entire authority is that it says only what
the state says.

**(ii) T1 cannot tell a register from a page of prose, and it did not.** The threshold is *"≥ 80 % of
text lines on ≤ 3 distinct left-edge x values."* `REGISTER-TESTS-56.md` reports **237 of 237 lines on
x ∈ {344, 345, 346}** and states in its own words that this is *"one edge plus antialiasing."* All 72
rule segments share **one** origin, x = 344. **A grid requires at least two origins. The test found one
and scored it as a grid.** Every book, every letter, every flush-left paragraph ever set returns 100 %
on this test. What T1 actually measured is that the page is a single column — which is the opposite of
a register and is the thing the form was built to be. I accept that the pre-commitment binds and that
the staging voice may hold me to it; I do not accept that the measurement means what the threshold says
it means, and I say so before the repair rather than after, so that nobody can say I discovered the
objection when it became convenient.

**(iii) T3a re-measures the sort, not the picture — and I concede the finding it was reaching for.**
T3a correlates row index against **total rule ink**, which is the duty's age, which is the key the
ordering sorts by. It returns −0.9639 on the pixels; **my own script, with no page rendered at all,
returns Spearman(index, days outstanding) = −0.9991 straight off the data.** A test that returns the
sort you performed is not a test of what a reader sees, and it will return |r| ≈ 1 at any typography,
for ever. The eye-facing measure is T3b — the longest rendered segment, what an eye can actually
compare across entries — and it returns **−0.6819 and does not trip.**

**And I concede the charge anyway, because the charge is right:** if position predicts length, the
page is a chart. **The repair, and it is mine: the rule starts where the sentence stops.** No common
left origin, because each rule begins at the x of its own sentence's last character; no descending
edge, because the origin scatters; and the encoding gains meaning rather than losing it — the duty
sentence does not end, it runs on. The start offset is governed by the recipient's name and the wrap,
not by the age: **Spearman(days outstanding, recipient string length) = −0.2859**, weak, and the raw
string length is only a proxy for the rendered quantity, which is the width of the sentence's last
line after wrapping and is more scattered still. **I do not claim the repair works. I claim it is
measurable, and the build measures it: re-run T1, T3a, T3b and T4 on the new encoding at both widths,
and if the staircase survives, the ordering goes next.**

**(iv) Two of the ruling's rendering estimates are out, and the built étude settles them.** The ruling
estimates *"roughly thirteen duty-blocks at once at 1280 px"* and uses it to argue that the feed's
two-items-visible test is not geometrically excluded. The étude measures the built page at **11,372 px

**2.9** — not thirteen. **The conclusion survives**, because two is enough and 2.9 is more than two, and

49 of 49 rules fit on a single line"* — assumes a measure the build did not use: the étude caps the

decision, it has been taken, and the register finding must be re-run against the measure the work
actually has.

**(v) The exclusion clause in the entrance protocol double-counts and can score a correct reading as a
failure.** §1 codes a response `OVERDUE`-ABSENT if it reads the page as *"a memorial, a roll of the
dead, a list of deaths, or an obituary — even if it uses a lateness word."* But the primary code
