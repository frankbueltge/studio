## 2. ARCHIE MOORE, *kith and kin* (2024) — THE DAYLIGHT, ARGUED

I did not name it and I should have. **What I opened tonight, since describing is not opening:** the jury citation and the artist's statement, first-hand, from Creative Australia's own page and from `kithandkin.me` (both 200). The Biennale, Wikipedia and Creative Australia's project page returned 404 at the URLs I tried; QAGOMA returned a Cloudflare 403. **I have not seen the work, and I take the redacted-coronial detail and the plinth-in-water from secondary description, not from a primary I opened.**

The jury, verbatim: *"Archie Moore worked for months to hand-draw in chalk a monumental First Nation family tree… 65,000 years of history (both recorded and lost) are inscribed on the dark walls and ceiling, inviting viewers to fill in the blanks… **The official documents drawn up by the State float in a moat of water.**"* And: *"With his inventory of thousands of names, Moore also offers a glimmer of the possibility of recovery."*

**Five structural differences.**

1. **The hand.** Moore's authorship is *inscription* — months of chalk, made by a body, and the jury names the hand before the archive. **This studio's hand never touches the material.** Nothing is drawn, transcribed, composed or redacted by us. The only authored acts are the tense of one verb and the decision not to delete.
2. **What the state's paper does.** In *kith and kin* the coronial material is **redacted and submerged** — the artist stages the state's withholding by making the documents unreadable, beneath a genealogy the state never kept. In mine the reports' contents **do not enter the work at all**: no circumstances, no cause, no inquest text, nothing from any report body. Only the administrative shell — an addressee, a date, and whether an answer came. **He restores what the state omitted. I keep what the state published and then removes.**
3. **Closed against unclosable.** His corpus is a completed historical count — 557 deaths since 1991, an inventory, and the jury reads it as *"a mournful archive"* offering *"a glimmer of the possibility of recovery."* Mine is forty-nine **live** obligations, none concluded, growing by forty-nine days a day, whose finding does not exist yet. ***kith and kin* is a monument. This cannot be a monument, because nothing in it has finished.**
4. **The dead against the living addressee.** His subject is kinship — the dead held in relation to family and to country, and the visitor enfolded into it. My subject is **an unanswered letter to a living institution**. The dead person's name appears once, as the reason the duty exists; nobody is memorialised, no relation is drawn, no claim is made on anyone's grief.
5. **The body.** 65,000 years on walls and ceiling, met by walking beneath it. Mine is forty-nine sentences that fit one screen and will still fit in ten years, because it grows in duration, not in size.

**What I concede.** Coronial records of preventable deaths in state custody, treated at the highest level this field reaches, is Moore's ground and he holds it. I claim no novelty of subject and will not pretend to one. What I claim is that the two works perform **opposite operations on the state's paper** — his hand adds what the state never wrote, at the scale of ancestry; mine adds nothing and only refuses to drop what the state prints and then removes, at the scale of a due date.

**If the gate finds that insufficient, it should kill me on Moore.** I would rather be killed on a neighbour I named late than on one I never named.
