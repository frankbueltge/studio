## 1. THE WORK, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

When a coroner in England and Wales finds that a death reveals a risk that more people will die, they must write to whoever has the power to act. The letter ends with a sentence in the second person and a date: *"You are under a duty to respond to this report within 56 days of the date of this report, namely by 13 July 2026."* The recipient is under a statutory duty to answer. **If they do not, nothing happens.** The Chief Coroner's own guidance states there is *"no power authorising a coroner to take any steps"*, and that once the report is sent the coroner *"no longer has a mandate to take any further steps."* Twice a year the Chief Coroner publishes a list of those who never answered — and removes each entry the moment an answer arrives. **The work is that list, kept.** A visitor meets a single page: forty-nine names of dead people, and under each, one sentence in the present tense naming a public body and the date it was to answer by — a date that has passed. Under each sentence is a solid rule as long as the days that have run since, and after it, one small mark for every day this register has since looked and found the duty still outstanding. The page does not grow by adding items; it grows by getting older, in place. Nothing here is ever deleted, reordered or rewritten. When a body finally answers and the state erases its row, the sentence above it stops, is dated, and goes into the past tense — the only completed thing in the work. **Title: *YOU ARE UNDER A DUTY*.** It is the state's own sentence, and it is addressed to whoever is reading.

**The argument.** This work's accusation is against a web page, for forgetting. ## 6. THE SEASON'S STANDING QUESTION — WHAT THE ACCUMULATION DOES, AND HOW IT IS COUNTED

**The claim, pre-registered before the first unit.** At extent 1 the work can state **who is outstanding now** — which the source also states. At extent *E* it can state **who was ever outstanding and for exactly how long** — which the source cannot state at any extent, because it deletes. The accumulation does not add items. It converts a snapshot into a history, and history is the thing the source is designed to destroy.

**Tested by counting — the severed test, two extents, captions covered, dossier out of the room.** Two readers who were not in any session. Reader A is shown the work at extent 1; Reader B at extent *E*. Both answer one written question: *"Name every public body that has at any point been recorded as not having answered a coroner, and say for how long."* Reader A can name only those currently listed. Reader B can name those **plus every closed sentence**. The differential is the count of closed sentences. It is a number, and it is not ours to manufacture.

**Two doors, because one of them is the world's and might never open.**

- **Door 1 — guaranteed, arithmetic, ours to reach: thirty consecutive observations.** The work is not shown until its first public state already contains a month in which **not one of forty-nine duties moved**. That is stillness argued *as* the finding, now, at the concept gate, exactly as season condition 4 requires — not hoped for as a plot twist. The expected result of this work is that nothing happens, and that is not a defect of the material, it *is* the material: the Chief Coroner's guidance says in terms that there is no power to compel. A register of unenforceable duties, watched daily, that does not move, is the demonstration.
- **Door 2 — the world's, and it opens unmarked: the first closed sentence.** The first time a public body answers a report it had ignored, the state erases the row, this work keeps it, and the work acquires the only thing it can ever complete. No announcement, no highlight, no notice.

## 9. LEGAL HYGIENE

Everything here is published under a real person's name, and these are real dead people and real named institutions in relation to a statutory breach. The bereaved are not material.

**Names of the deceased.** Printed exactly as the Chief Coroner prints them, once, and **nothing else about the person** — no age, no cause of death, no circumstances, no photograph, no narrative, no category, no quotation from the report's body. (The report pages themselves carry all of that; I read the Naagi and Reading reports in full and none of it enters the work.) The name appears because the duty exists only because that person died; a version that named only institutions would be a work about administrative punctuality, which is exactly the intra-methodology trap that killed the last concept's subject.

**Names of recipients.** Printed exactly as the Chief Coroner prints them, never with an adjective, always linked to the row they came from.

**The distinction, held rigidly.** The work asserts only what the state asserts: that these reports *"have been compiled after receiving confirmation from coroner's offices that these responses have not been received."* It never says *failed*, *ignored*, or *badge of dishonour* — that phrase belongs to secondary commentary and judiciary guidance itself rejects the framing; if it is ever quoted it is attributed to its source and never to the Chief Coroner. **And the work never infers non-response from an empty response slot on a report page.** I tested that inference tonight and it does not hold: of 34 report pages whose 56 days expired between 67 and 197 days ago, **32 carry at least one published response** in the page's "Related content" block, and 2 do not — and neither of those two appears on the non-response list. Absence of a published response is therefore not evidence of non-response, the work never uses it, and this ratio is stated at the door: **most recipients answer. These forty-nine are the residue.**

**Tone, taken from the source.** Chapter 16 instructs the coroner, when telling a bereaved family that nobody answered, to *"use neutral terms and not express irritation or frustration at the failure of a PFD recipient to comply with their legal obligation."* The work adopts the state's own required affect as its rule of voice. It expresses nothing. The reader supplies what the state forbids the coroner to show. That is the piece's restraint and its meaning, not its caution.

**Removal, and the one place no-revision yields.** The state operates its own representations route (*"documents will remain online while they are considered. Any amendments or removals will be made as soon as reasonably practicable"*). If a bereaved family, or a named body with a correction, asks — the entry goes. **And unlike the state's register, the removal is recorded in its place, as a removal, without the name.** The no-revision rule binds the record; it does not outrank a person. If judiciary.uk corrects or withdraws a row, the work appends the correction beside the original and never edits it away — protocol rule 6 and the season's law agreeing for once.
