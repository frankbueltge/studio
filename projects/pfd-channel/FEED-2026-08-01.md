## The feed

**HTTP 200, `application/atom+xml`, 114,882 bytes.** It is a real Atom feed (root element `<feed>`),

- the title (deceased's name + "Prevention of future deaths report"),
- an `alternate` link to the report's own permanent page,

- **`<published>` and `<updated>` as full UTC timestamps** — not dates.

## Three consequences, stated plainly

1. **A unit can be issued by a rule an outsider can audit.** Season Two's staging condition 2 requires
   the issuing rule to be *"auditable by an outsider against that day's published source"*. Here the
   source is a feed with a timestamp and a permanent per-report URL: anyone can re-run the rule.
   This is a stronger audit surface than anything the previous concept had — and it does **not**
   require the work to publish the dates itself, which was the trilemma the last gate handed over.
2. **The window is 10 entries, so the read must be nightly.** On the measured cadence (~1.2 reports
   per calendar day, largest observed day 13) a ten-entry window is comfortably safe for a nightly
   read but would **not** be safe for a weekly one. This is a real operating constraint, not a detail.
   `.github/workflows/auto-land.yml` already runs nightly under this house's own hand
   (`memory/decisions.md`: *a studio capability is opened before it is requested*).
3. **The record moves after publication, and the feed says so.** The newest entry was published
   2026-07-28 and **updated 2026-07-29** — seventeen and a half hours later. Under Season Two's
   no-revision law (*a committed unit is immutable; errors are corrected by appended correction beside
   the error, never by edit*) an upstream page that changes after a unit is issued is not an
   inconvenience: **it is material.** Any work here must decide, before its first unit, what it does
   when the source edits itself — and the honest options are to record the change beside the unit or
   to be wrong in public.
