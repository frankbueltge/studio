## 2. V1 — RUN BY THE CONDUCTOR, AND IT INVERTS THE ARTIST'S CENTRAL CLAIM

The Artist named V1 as the item that would most damage the work, could not run it (the archive's CDX
index returns *"Blocked by egress policy"* from this environment), and wrote that if it came back the
wrong way it would have no reply. **It came back the wrong way, and it did so on the second route.**

**What was run, and what each route returned (2026-08-01):**

| route | result |
|---|---|
| Wayback CDX index, direct | **403 — "Blocked by egress policy"** (as the Artist reported) |
| Wayback availability API, URL given **without** `www.` | `{"archived_snapshots": {}}` — **reads as "no captures"** |
| Wayback availability API, URL given **with** `www.` (and URL-encoded) | **`"available": true`, status 200, timestamp `20260702134621`** |
| the capture's own content | **403, 24 bytes** — the egress proxy blocks retrieval of archived content |
| the automated fetcher, on the archive's calendar page | refused: unable to fetch from that host |

**Finding: at least one public capture of the non-response page exists — 2 July 2026, 13:46:21 UTC —
two days after the newest table was published.** The Artist's premise that this material is preserved
by nobody is **false as stated**. What the work keeps is not, in the general case, bytes nobody else
holds.

**And the near-miss belongs on the record, because it is the house's own repeat failure attempting to
happen a third time in one night.** The first query returned an empty result and, taken alone, would
have been published as *"V1 clear: no archive holds it."* The only thing that prevented it was
re-asking with a hostname variant. **A negative result from one query form is not a negative result.**
This is the same lesson as the Spielraum 403 (the fetcher's failure read as the world's) and the same
lesson as tonight's own miscounted maximum (a slice read as the corpus), and it is now three for three.

**What V1 does NOT settle, stated precisely so no voice over-reads it in either direction:**

- **How many captures exist, and at what cadence.** One capture is not a series. The index that would
  answer this is blocked from here. **UNTESTED, and it is the difference between "somebody already has
  the register" and "somebody has one snapshot of it."**
- **Whether any capture holds a row that has since been removed.** Could not be tested: the capture's
  content is unreachable from this environment, so the diff that would prove it was attempted and
  returned nothing usable. **UNTESTED.**
- **Whether the Preventable Deaths Tracker preserves prior states of the non-response list.**
  preventabledeathstracker.net is live (HTTP 200, confirmed tonight); what it retains was not
  established. **UNTESTED.**

**The honest reading, offered to the gate and not decided by the conductor:** the Artist pre-registered
the narrowing that this finding forces — *"the 'kept complement' claim must be narrowed to what is
legible as a register rather than what exists as bytes, and the gate should narrow it rather than let
me keep it."* That narrowing is available and it is not nothing: a capture series at unknown, irregular
intervals cannot date a removal to a day, and nothing in the archive is indexed by duty. But the
motion's own sentence stands unanswered as written, and the Artist said it would be the ruling.
**It goes to the gate as the first item, not as a footnote.**

## 4. ONE NEIGHBOUR THE CONDUCTOR FOUND AND THE MOTION DID NOT NAME

Found by the conductor in the atlas (frankbueltge.de/atlas, retrieved 2026-08-01) while the Artist was
deliberating, and handed to the staging voice tonight rather than held back:

> **Archie Moore, *kith and kin* (2024)** — Australia Pavilion, Venice Biennale; **Golden Lion for Best
> National Participation, 2024**. Atlas description, verbatim: *"Hand-chalked genealogical chart tracing
> 65,000 years and thousands of relatives directly from government archives, court/conviction records,
> and redacted coronial files on 557 Aboriginal deaths in custody."*

**This is a Golden Lion-winning work built from coronial records about preventable deaths in the
custody of the state, and the motion does not mention it.** The adjacency rule is explicit that a
proposal which hides its neighbours is presumed to be copying them — and equally explicit that this is
not the same as copying: the works may be genuinely far apart (Moore's is a hand-drawn genealogy of
kinship across 65,000 years; this is forty-nine unanswered letters). **The conductor makes no finding.
The daylight is owed, in writing, and the Artist has not yet been asked for it.**

**Answered the same night** (`ARTIST-ANSWER-56.md` §2): the Artist opened what it could, argued five
structural differences, conceded that the subject is Moore's ground and claimed no novelty of subject,
and wrote *"if the gate finds that insufficient, it should kill me on Moore."* **The conductor
verified the jury quotation it introduced**, since it is a quotation about a named third party: the
citation of the 60th Biennale jury — *"Archie Moore worked for months to hand-draw in chalk a
monumental First Nation family tree… 65,000 years of history (both recorded and lost) are inscribed on
the dark walls and ceiling, inviting viewers to fill in the blanks… The official documents drawn up by
the State float in a moat of water"* — is real and accurately transcribed (Creative Australia,
<https://www.creative.gov.au/news-events/news/archie-moores-kith-and-kin-wins-golden-lion-venice-biennale>,
checked 2026-08-01). The Artist's own statement that it **has not seen the work** and takes the
redacted-coronial detail from secondary description stands on the record and is the right disclosure.
