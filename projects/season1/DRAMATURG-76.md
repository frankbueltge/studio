## 1. FIRST ENCOUNTER

Ten seconds gets: a date, a sub-line, and "Eleven of the ships this record can place in 4
August 2026 stood in the list dated 4 August itself." A stranger meets a fraction of an
unnamed thing. Which ships? Placed in a day by whom? The lede is a finding about a subject
the page has not yet named.

The definition paragraph fixes that — one paragraph too late — and then opens with someone
else's voice. The page's first full sentence in its own register should not be a quotation
from the method sheet. Invert: the working clause ("Going dark is a ship switching off its
AIS transponder — the radio signal that puts it on the public picture of the sea") belongs
above the lede, ideally replacing the sub-line. The quotation, the 12-hour / 50-mile
thresholds, and the source's language can stay below where they are.

## 2. THE TURN

Flattened. SUPERSEDED and LIVE are version-control vocabulary — administrative furniture,
exactly. They describe the *status of two records*, not the *event of a number falling*.
Worse, SUPERSEDED implies the 69 % was wrong; it was true then, which is the whole point of
the piece.

Nothing between the two figures says a fall occurred. The reader must subtract 69 from 65
themselves, across two lines that both end "–100 %". The sentence that stages the turn —
"before the capture that made it fall" — is a grey caption under a pull-quote, three
paragraphs downstream, as is its cause ("What grew was the total: WANGBIAO-8730"). Put one
line of prose between the two figures naming the fall and the ship that caused it.

## 3. ECONOMY

**Cut the sub-line**, "one day of the sea, and how much of it was knowable on the day
itself." It restates the H2 above the figures almost verbatim and buys nothing; its slot is
the best real estate on the page and should carry the definition.

Made redundant by (a): the second method-sheet quotation ("Daily. Window: disabling events
that ended in the last 7 days") — the seven-day window is now stated three times (there, and
in the new table caption). Keep the caption's; drop this one.

On the fourth reader: the caption sentence is not redundant, it is misplaced. It reads
perfectly and reached nobody's eye, because it is small grey type *below* a table whose
column header — "ships in that list" — has already taught the wrong model. Load-bearing
corrections do not go downstream of the misreading. Move it into the header.

## 4. THE ENDING

Yes. "No number closes this" is the page's best sentence and earns the last position; the two
footer lines are correctly subordinate.
