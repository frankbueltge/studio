## Q1 — the diagnosis was right, and the fix was one sentence

All three carried the day away by its calendar date, unaided — *"August 4th 2026"* (A), *"August 4,
2026"* (B), *"4 August 2026"* (C) — and all three carried the mechanism with it, C's being the
plainest: *"any single day's count is incomplete on the day and can only grow afterward, never
shrink."*

**No reader said the list was published on the day.** That refutation was written because a date is
cheap to buy with a false claim; it was not bought. C read the ledger the other way, unprompted and
correctly: *"the 'list of 4 Aug' itself was actually fetched on 5 Aug per the OBSERVED table."*

**What this settles.** §D named two alternative explanations in advance — that unaided recall drops
particulars wherever they sit, and that the day was competing with a crowd of other dates. Neither
is needed, and both stand unspent. **A three-word anaphor was costing this work its subject**, and
nothing else was.
