# DRAMATURG 83 — 2026-08-10 — the announced run, judged on the running object

Convened on a finished, running object, as the constitution requires. It was given the built page
and told to drive it itself; **it did — at four widths, taking its own timings against
`performance.now()`, comparing screenshot buffers, and reading the accessibility properties off the
live DOM.** It was told what the previous staging voice had ruled and what this house had built in
answer, so it would not spend its judgement re-finding what was already found. It was shown **no
reader's answer**, and the panel was scored before it wrote but never sent to it. **It judges and it
cuts; it does not co-write.** Verbatim, unedited.

**WHAT THIS HOUSE DID WITH IT, written above the memo so nothing below reads as endorsed:** its
central finding — that the page's only live region is filled 3.2 ms before `DOMContentLoaded` and so
is never spoken — was **re-measured first-hand before it was believed** (filled 75 ms, DCL 109 ms)
and **repaired the same night**, together with the one change it prescribed. Its prescribed **cut**
of the constant line is **REFUSED**, and the reason is that the verifying pass, working in parallel
and shown none of this memo, found the four words this memo wants to keep — *"The upper end never
moves"* — to be **false of this work's own arithmetic**. The two voices met on one sentence from
opposite sides. The verifying pass wins: a cut that keeps the false clause and deletes the true
qualification is not available at any price.

---

## 1. FIRST ENCOUNTER

> Load. At **103.8 ms** the state line has text; DOMContentLoaded fires at **107.0 ms**, load at
> **107.5 ms**. Then nothing. The first DOM mutation of any kind arrives at **12,801.6 ms**. I
> screenshotted the head at t=1.5 s and again at t=10.5 s and compared the buffers:
> **byte-identical**. `document.getAnimations()` returns empty. The hold is a still frame, not a
> slow one. The page declares `"first_dwell_ms": 12857`; I clocked the replay pause at
> **12,855 ms** and the whole run at **22,470 ms**. The instrument is honest about itself to two
> milliseconds.
>
> Is the first beat legible as held? **Yes, now — but only in one place, and only as a promise.**
> The ladder shows `ON THE DAY` filled black at rung one of seven; the line below says the run
> exists. That is the repair, and it works: I no longer sit in front of a static page wondering
> whether the seven buttons are seven buttons. Compare the reduced-motion arm, which gets a
> different and better-formed sentence — "Your machine asks for no motion, so nothing runs: this is
> the day's own answer" — and never moves. Both arms now know what they are.
>
> Is the line doing work or apologising? **Both, in that order.** Its opening sentence does real
> work — it names the length ("about twenty-two seconds", measured 22.47) and the fact of a pause
> before it. Its second and third variants apologise: click a button at t=4 s and I got *"You
> stopped the run at +3 DAYS. Press "run it again" to see it whole."* — which is a caption written
> about a defect the click has already caused. That is the honest thing to publish, and it is still
> an apology.
>
> The line's real failure in the first thirty seconds is that **it says a pause is coming and then
> never mentions it again.** It does not change once between 0 and 22,417 ms. For 12.86 seconds a
> reader is asked to trust a sentence set at 10.56 px, dim ink, at 4.76:1 — the second-smallest type
> on the head — against a page in which literally no pixel moves. And the sentence sizes the wait
> wrongly: *"a pause as long as the sentence above takes to read."* The sentence directly above the
> state line is the hedge, 23 words, **5.8 s** at the page's own cited 238 wpm. The pause is
> calibrated to the gloss instead — 51 words, **12.86 s**, four blocks up and two sentences long.
> The page tells the reader to expect roughly six seconds and holds them for thirteen. The
> instruction is off by **2.2×**, and it is off in the direction that makes the stillness read as
> breakage.

## 2. THE TURN

> **Yes. It has one now, and it is not in the figure.**
>
> The turn is the reserved space. At t=0 the head reads *"NAMED ONLY BY LATER LISTS — nothing yet.
> The space below is the part of this day that nobody could have had on it,"* and below it sits an
> emptiness roughly 90 px deep that the layout refuses to close. At 20,824 ms that heading has
> become *"nineteen ships dark on that same day that nobody could have had on it,"* and the space is
> full — nineteen boxes where there were none, the last five in black. Nothing reflows; the figure
> does not move down. The emptiness was labelled before it was filled, so filling it is an argument
> and not an animation.
>
> That is a real turn and the head should be proud of it. Note where it is **not**: the figure. 100
> → 79 → 69 → 65 → 55 → 44 → 37 is a countdown, and countdowns do not turn. The two-ended treatment
> helps — `100 %` in 46.4 px/700 black against `–100 %` in 46.4 px/400 dim — but the reader's eye is
> caught by the space filling, not by the numeral falling. The head's best moment is the one it
> argues for least.

## 3. THE COST

> Words. The head's prose is now **288 words** across eight blocks. The three changes that add text
> add **34** (state line), **47** (the constant line), and **+8** to the gloss (43 → 51) — about
> **89 words**, roughly a third of the head, spent on commentary rather than the day.
>
> The gloss's eight extra words are the expensive ones: because the pause is derived from the
> gloss's own word count, making the premise more honest **lengthened the dead air from 10,840 ms to
> 12,857 ms — +2,017 ms, a 19 % longer silence bought with eight words.** That is a real trade and
> nobody appears to have priced it.
>
> Said three times, inside a **611 px** band from gloss-top to caption-bottom at 1280:
>
> - gloss: *"which name a ship only once it has come back"*
> - constant: *"A list that can name a ship only when it comes back can never rule out one that
>   never comes back"*
> - caption: *"A ship reaches the list only after it comes back, so a day that is over keeps being
>   answered"*
>
> And the conclusion twice: constant's *"the lower end is the only end this record can lower, and
> the next list can lower it again"* against caption's *"That is why the figure above falls, and why
> it can only go on falling."*
>
> **What I would cut, by its text:** everything in the constant line after the first four words.
> Keep *"The upper end never moves."* Delete *"A list that can name a ship only when it comes back
> can never rule out one that never comes back — so the lower end is the only end this record can
> lower, and the next list can lower it again."* — 43 words that restate the caption sitting 300 px
> below. The banked change asked you to mark the constant; the type already marks it, and one
> four-word sentence names it. The rest is the head arguing with a reader who has not disagreed yet.

## 4. THE WEAK JOINT

> **The page's only live region is filled 3.2 ms before DOMContentLoaded, so the one sentence
> written this session to announce that a performance exists is the one sentence no screen reader
> will ever speak.**
>
> Measured: `#sd-arrive-state` is `role="status" aria-live="polite"`, it is the sole live region in
> the document, and it carries text at **103.8 ms** — inside the initial render, before **107.0 ms**
> DCL. Live regions do not announce their initial contents. Then it does not change again until
> **22,417 ms**. A screen-reader user therefore receives: silence at load, silence across all seven
> states, and finally *"The run has finished."* — an announcement that a thing they were never told
> about is over.
>
> This is exactly the argument of the page turned against the page. STILL DARK exists to publish how
> little of a day was knowable *while the day was happening*, and it stages its own performance so
> that the only account of it arrives after it has ended.
>
> Two supporting measurements in the same joint. On a 390×844 phone the figure sits at y=248 and is
> visible; the ladder at y=938 and the state line at y=991 are both **below an 844 px fold** — so the
> mobile reader watches the number change with the announcement and the controls off-screen. And the
> five names the last beat adds carry `class="sd-arrive-new"`, weight 700, `#111` border, with **no
> `title`, no `aria-label`, and no words anywhere on the head explaining the mark** — while the
> stylesheet calls them "the only thing on this page that is meant to be seen changing" and change
> #2 was adopted on the principle that a mark the eye reads must be said in words. The law is stated
> and broken in the same head.

## 5. VERDICT

> **RETURNS FOR RESTAGING.** The head built the exact mechanism the last verdict demanded — a live
> region that says a performance exists — and wired it to fire 3.2 ms before the browser will carry
> it, so the half of the defect that was named most plainly is still open, now with a sentence
> sitting on top of it that looks like a repair.
>
> **The one change:** leave `#sd-arrive-state` empty in the initial render, and write the opening
> sentence into it from a timer **≥250 ms after the `load` event** so the region fires as a change;
> then write one further sentence into it at the instant the 12,857 ms dwell ends — the run
> beginning is the only event in the piece that currently has no words at all. Costs no new prose
> and no new element.

## What was built from this memo, the same night, and what was measured after

**The one change: TAKEN, both limbs, and re-measured.** `#sd-arrive-state` is empty in the initial
render; the opening sentence is written 250 ms after `load`; a new sentence lands at the instant the
dwell ends. `announce.mjs` — itself repaired tonight, because it had been counting DOM writes and
calling them announcements — now reports, on the shipped page:

```
LIVE REGIONS ......... 1
SPOKEN ............... 3        443 ms  the run exists, and how long it is
                            14,223 ms  the run has started
                            23,823 ms  the run has finished
```

**Three spoken announcements across a twenty-four-second run in which the figure takes seven
states**, and a stop pressed at 3 s is spoken in 175 ms. **The cut is REFUSED** (reason above).
**The wait's own description is repaired** — it now names the paragraph the beat is derived from
instead of the sentence above the line. **The mobile fold, the unworded `sd-arrive-new` mark, and
the 19 % longer silence bought with eight words are banked and owed**, not taken tonight: two of
them change the face this session's four readers were scored on, and the third is the price of a
sentence the verifying pass required.

**The restaging verdict is NOT self-discharged.** This memo judged the object as it stood before its
own prescription was built. It stands open until a staging voice, convened on the repaired object
and shown no answers, rules on it — the gate of the next session.
