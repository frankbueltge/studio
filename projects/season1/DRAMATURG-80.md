# DRAMATURG 80 — 2026-08-09 — the running head, judged on the finished object

Convened on a finished object, as the constitution requires: the five states of the new
head in order, the whole page at both widths, and the page in reading order without paint.
It was given the object and the problem it was built to solve, and no memo of this
project's. **It judges and it cuts; it does not co-write.** Verbatim, unedited.

---

## 1. FIRST ENCOUNTER

> For the first six seconds a stranger is not watching the head — they are reading the four
> static lines stacked on top of it. Title, subtitle, small-caps label, and a three-line
> grey definition of "dark" all sit *above* the numeral. The run is 5 × 1.6s = about 6.4
> seconds. That is roughly the time it takes to read down to it. **The element that runs is
> placed below the point at which the run is over.** Most first readers will arrive at step
> 3 or step 4 and see a static list of twenty names. The one re-entry is `run it again`, a
> pale ghost button parked at the far right of the stop row, quieter than everything around
> it.
>
> When the reader *is* looking, it works. **The moment it works is step 0 → step 1**: three
> names outline into existence, the count goes 11 → 14, the caption flips from "the day
> itself" to "a day after the day had ended" — and the enormous date above it does not
> move. That single frame does what the failed sentence could not: it shows a fixed question
> and an unfixed answer. No fade, no easing, no decoration. Credit where due — this is
> disciplined staging, not a slideshow.
>
> Does it embody the delay or illustrate it? **It embodies the wrong half of it, in the
> wrong sign.** The work is about ships that *vanish*; the head animates names *arriving*.
> The reader's eye learns the verb "appear." Worse, an accretion loop that fills and settles
> is formally identical to a progress bar — the universal sign for *the system is fetching,
> please wait*. A progress bar that finishes says: now you have it all. That is the precise
> inverse of the page's closing line.
>
> **The moment it dies is step 4.** The run does not conclude; it runs out. Twenty sits
> large and black and finished, the fifth stop highlighted exactly like the four before it,
> and nothing in the head admits that twenty can still grow.
>
> One more collision worth naming: the stop buttons are labelled `4 AUG … 8 AUG` under a
> title that reads `4 AUGUST 2026`. The same string means the day-being-held-open at the top
> and the edition-doing-the-holding forty pixels lower. To a stranger the row reads as a
> date picker — *pick a day, see its ships* — which is the severed readers' original
> misreading, rebuilt in hardware at the exact joint where the mechanism lives.

## 2. THE TURN

> **There is no turn. There is an increment.** Steps 1–4 are the same gesture four times
> with diminishing yield (+3, +2, +1, +3); by step 2 a reader has learned the rule and the
> remaining beats are arithmetic. The run has an ending only in the sense that it has a last
> frame.
>
> The real turn is on the page, three screens down, and the head never touches it:
> `69 %–100 %` struck through, falling to `55 %–100 %`. That is a reversal — the count goes
> *up* and the knowledge goes *down*. The head shows only the rising half. **Its arithmetic
> and the page's argument point in opposite directions and are never allowed to meet.** A
> fifth beat that let the share fall as the names arrived would give the run an ending
> instead of a stop.

## 3. THE COST

> Yes — the page is now double-headed, arguably quadruple-headed. The same proposition is
> stated four times in the first two screens: the run itself (11 → 20 across five editions);
> the caption directly beneath the buttons; the display-size lede; and the timeline's own
> headings.
>
> **Cut #3 — the bold lede, in full, except its final clause.** It is set at the
> second-largest type size on the page and it is a prose transcription of the element
> immediately above it; if the run works, this paragraph announces the punchline of a joke
> still being told. Keep only *"— four of them after this page had printed its figure,"*
> welded onto the paragraph below, because that is the one fact the run cannot show: that
> the page's own published number was wrong.
>
> Second cut, smaller: the head's caption down to its last clause — *"and no later night can
> make it smaller."* The rest narrates the buttons a reader has just pressed.
>
> Note that the timeline groupings are the strongest telling of the four — they carry the
> dark-and-return spans, the actual withholding — and are now partly spent by the time a
> reader reaches them.

## 4. THE WEAK JOINT

> A page whose subject is ships that switch themselves off has given itself a head in which
> ships switch themselves on — an accretion loop indistinguishable from a progress bar,
> which finishes at twenty and thereby tells the stranger, in the one language they read
> fastest, that the day is now fully known.

## 5. VERDICT

> **Returns for restaging** — narrowly, and it is closer than the sentence ever got. The one
> change that would earn a second look: **make the no-motion reader's state step 0, not step
> 4, and let the run's fifth beat be the share falling rather than the count landing.** Then
> the delay is something the reader has to do — eleven names, five buttons, fill the day
> yourself — and the run ends on the only number that moves the right way.

## What this house does with it tonight, and what it does not

**Nothing is rebuilt on this memo tonight, and that is the standing rule, not fatigue.**
Cuts ordered on judgement alone have twice been banked here rather than taken, and both
times the readers who eventually arrived changed the shape of the cut (owed items (l) and
(m)). This memo goes to the next session as a pre-registered question with a refuting
number, not to the file tonight.

**But it does not stand alone, and that is why it is load-bearing.** Two instruments
convened separately, told nothing of each other, agree on the same defect:

- This memo: *"an accretion loop indistinguishable from a progress bar… tells the stranger
  that the day is now fully known."*
- `PANEL-80.md`, Q1, both severed readers: the page is about *"ships… out at sea"*, *"ships
  that were at sea"* — **presence, arriving, confirmed**. H1's own Q3 word is
  *"confirmations"*.

**The staging voice predicted the reading the panel produced, from the form, without seeing
the answers.** Where a diagnosis and a measurement arrive at the same defect from opposite
directions, this house has ruled that it acts — and the action is a pre-registered restaging
with a number that can refute it, which is what §5's prescription becomes in owed item (k).
