## 2. The claim — **Episode 7/7, the open slot**

**STILL DARK**, operated across days. A visitor is given one day of the sea: today. It is almost
empty — because upstream, by its published method, a disappearance counts only once the ship
returns: *"disabling events that ended in the last 7 days (complete vanish-and-return stories)"*
(`https://frankbueltge.de/werke/ghost-fleet/`, 200). They are asked to leave and come back. On the
return, **the same day — the one they already looked at — has ships in it.** They did not miss them;
nobody could have seen them. A person takes a date, watches it be almost nothing, and returns to find
their own past still arriving.

## 4. The takedown, and the refutation

**The takedown:** *"A studio took someone else's daily dataset, added a delay, and asked you to come
back tomorrow to watch a loading bar finish."*

The delay is upstream and structural, not ours to remove. The built work produces what no dataset
holds: a **day-addressed** record — one calendar day kept open for weeks — and a figure that exists
nowhere, how much of a day's darkness was knowable on that day. A loading bar finishes; this does
not, because the ships that never return never arrive.

**Leg (a)**, a finding of its own, carried by **(c)**: only nightly machinery holds a day open for
weeks. **(b) is not claimed** — upstream alleges no illegality, nor do we.

## 5. The form, and the Grundform rule

Not the screen apparatus with a self-test. The form is **the return visit as material**, untried here. The
subject *is* latency; a form resolving in one sitting can only describe it, and this house has
died twice on absences that needed a caption. The absence is served, not described: the first visit
is deliberately unsatisfying, the only event happens between visits, without us. Nothing inside one
screen can prove a day was incomplete when you saw it.

## 6. The first étude — buildable tonight

One self-contained HTML file; no build, no runtime network. One day-strip: **4 August 2026**, the ten
named vessels of tonight's edition with their durations, ends fuzzed across the declared 7-day
window. Visit 1 shows the day as it could have been known on itself, then asks you to close the
page. Visit 2 — a genuine reopen, persisted locally, fallback control — returns to **the same day**,
now carrying what became knowable later. (Bar geometry: IMAGINED, marked on the face.)

**What tells me the form does not carry:** if readers on visit 2 call it a live feed updating rather
than a day they had already looked at that was incomplete when they saw it — if they cannot name that
what changed is the past.

## 7. My own weakest joint

The ships that never come back never arrive here either: the darkness this work is about is the one
thing it cannot show.
