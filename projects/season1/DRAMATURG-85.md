## 3. STAGING OR ANNOTATION — plainly, annotation

**Prose beside a run is annotation. It is a wall label with the work still running behind it.**

The measurement, three loads, `MutationObserver` on `#sd-arrive-cut-head`, `-figs`, `-said`,
`-refused`, `-tier` and `-since-note` across the whole 23.8 s: **0 mutations. Every time.** The
screenshot diff agrees: of the 37,448 pixels that change at 1400×900 across the run, **0 fall inside
the new block's rectangle (y 434–691).** The number falls from 100 % to 35 % and the block that says
what the number is a share *of* does not move, does not brighten, does not tick, is not touched by
the thing it qualifies. The two are on the same page and in no relation the eye can see.

The house has staged the correct sentence in the wrong grammar. Its own comment in the CSS says the
figures line stands "at the weight of the figure they qualify" — and at 16.8 px against the figure's
46.4 px it is the third-largest type in the head, above the vessel chips. That is prominence. It is
not staging. Prominence makes a reader look; staging makes the work *do* the thing.

**What the object would have to do instead.** The finding is that a falling quotient has the wrong
denominator — 11 of 31 where the list itself says 230. A run that discloses this stages it only if
the denominator is inside the run. Three forms are available to this machinery and none costs a new
paragraph: a **second numeral in the same frame**, fixed, running the same quotient against
`examined` while the first falls; the **reserved space drawn against its true field**, so the
thirty-one marks stand in a rule of 230 and the empty part of that rule is the argument the prose is
currently making in words; or a **run that does not stop at seven states** but continues past them
into the part it cannot fill. Any of the three puts the disclosure where the reader's eye already
is at 14.2 s. The present arrangement puts it where the reader's eye was at second two, and asks
the reader to hold it for twelve seconds until the numeral moves.
