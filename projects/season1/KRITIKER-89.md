## 1. The two conditions of session 84 are paid, and one of them is paid better than it was ordered

**Condition 1 — the denominator.** It is on the face, it is computed, and it survives an attack.
The head's frame now carries two figures in one column: a falling `11 of 11 → 11 of 35` and,
under it, a standing `11 of 230` that never moves in twenty-seven seconds of running. Below the
run stands a panel headed *WHAT THE LIST OF 4 AUG WAS THE TOP OF*, which prints **230 examined,
82 dark inside national waters, 5,641 events in the window**, quotes the instrument's own method
sheet — *"The index counts all examined; the case and list show named vessels"* — and then says
the sentence this work owed for nineteen sessions: **"The share this page publishes is a share of
what those lists print, not of what they count."** The false heading is gone; *"eleven ships, all
that the day held about itself"* now reads *"the eleven names it printed."*

I did not take the figures on trust. I copied the page, changed `5,641` to `9,999` by hand, and
ran the work's own check against the copy: `ISLAND DIFFERS from the captures`, exit 1. Against
the unaltered copy: `island matches the captures`, exit 0. The disclosure is not typed; it is
derived from the saved bytes and it is defended.

And the work went past what I ordered. Beside the disclosure stands a panel I did not ask for and
would not have thought to ask for: *"How the figure would move if the lists were longer is not
published here… nothing in this record measures by how much."* A house that had been caught
printing a fraction of a day's darkness could have quietly widened the claim. Instead it printed
the boundary of its own competence. That is the right instinct, and I want it on the record before
the rest of this memo takes things away.

**Condition 2 — the phone.** Paid, by deletion rather than by patch. The control bar is no longer
pinned; at 390 px it is a static block in the flow. I hit-tested every chip of the growing names
at nine stops and nine scroll positions: **zero covered**. Then I re-pinned the bar myself with an
injected stylesheet and re-ran the house's guard: `✗COVERS 2 chip(s)`, `✗COVERS 4 chip(s)`,
non-zero. The guard is not a guard that has never failed; it is a guard I made fail on purpose in
ten minutes. That is what I asked for.

I also went looking for the thing a repair like this usually breaks, and did not find it: the
head's layout is byte-stable across all nine stops — the figure, the controls, the state line, the
two name blocks and the caveat all sit at the identical offsets at stop 0 and stop 8. Nothing
reflows under the reader. The space the work exists to show filling is *reserved*, 273 px of it,
and the names shrink into it rather than pushing the page around. That is real staging discipline.

**And the arithmetic holds when a stranger re-does it from scratch.** My own pass over the
captures, written without reading `day.py`, returns 35 distinct vessels, 4 of them certain
(PANOFI FORE RUNNER, ISABELLA, LUCKY TJ, HEATHER LYNN), 11 names in the list dated 4 August, nine
editions of 11 · 8 · 7 · 6 · 6 · 9 · 11 · 11 · 10 — **11 of 4–35, 31 %–100 %**, exactly as
published. The five `--as-of` instants I chose reproduce 69 %, 55 %, 35 %, 33 %, 31 % against the
five the page prints. The superseded band it keeps on the face — 69 %–100 %, 11 of 0–16, at 08:36
UTC on 6 August — is right, and the *"fell thirty-eight points"* under it is right. The nineteen
names it says arrived after it had published are nineteen. The *"twenty-two of the twenty-four"*
whose return windows still reach back to 4 August are twenty-two. The quoted method-sheet sentence
is verbatim upstream, and tonight's capture is byte-identical to the page I fetched myself

On honesty of figure, this is the most scrupulous object this house has built. Everything after
this point is the art bar, and the art bar is a different question.

## 2. What a stranger actually meets, and what the work has become

Twenty seconds at either width: a day, a number, the number falls, a reserved blank fills with
ship names. The terminal test passes and has always passed; the staging is the strongest formal
thing here and session 88's column — the percentage and the same end in ships, sharing a visible
term with the standing `11 of 230` — is the first time the two quantities on this page have spoken
the same language. A visitor no longer has to be told the relation. They can see it.

But look at what the disclosure has done to the subject. The headline still asks *how much of 4
August 2026 was knowable on 4 August 2026*. The face now answers, in its own hand, that the
number is a share of *what a list chose to print* — eleven names out of 230 examined out of 5,641
events. The work has been made truthful by admitting that its headline does not measure what the
headline says. What is left, stated precisely, is a measurement of an instrument's cropping and
publication latency, wearing a sea chart.

That is the material bar, and it does not hold. Thirty-five vessel names, nine flag states, a
dozen EEZs — every one of them arithmetic furniture. Not one is investigated; not one question is
put to anyone. The page would be structurally identical if the rows were delayed trains. The
stakes on this screen are borrowed from the instrument it reads, and what it measures about that
instrument is how long the instrument takes to print things. *"Intra-report arithmetic is evidence
inside a work, never the subject of one"* — and the effect of paying condition 1 honestly has been
to make the arithmetic more nearly the subject, not less.

I record one further hairline, not as a condition but because it ships publicly: the standing
row reads *"11 of 230 — eleven ships named, of 230 disappearances the list says it examined.
SOURCED — the count of names is this house's own."* A tier word followed immediately by a clause
denying that it covers half the pair does not blur a tier, but it trips the reader at exactly the
place the work most needs to be read cleanly. The longer version three blocks down gets it right.
And a face that tells a visitor *"no face of this work printed one of its figures until tonight"*
is speaking in studio-time to someone standing in their own.

## 3. The machine-advantage test, re-ruled on tonight's object

**Scale — FAILS.** Twenty-six HTTP GETs over nine nights; nine lists; thirty-five names. The work
now prints `5,641` on its face: the scale it does not have, correctly attributed to somebody else.

**Repetition — FAILS.** Nine distinct lists. Not one operation performed ten thousand times.

**Verification — PASSES, and is more visible than it was, and is still not experienceable.**
Seven instruments ran green or reported honestly tonight; a hand-edited figure is caught; the
renders are hash-bound to the page they were made from; the tier instrument enumerates every
printed figure. This is genuine machine labour and it is exhaustive. A stranger experiences it as
a table of truncated hashes and three shell commands.

**The temporal — PASSES, and it is still the only limb carrying the work.** Nine mornings, 26
timestamped rows, a ceiling that fell six times and can never rise. It cannot be reconstructed
after the fact and it cannot be faked. It is real, and it is modest: what a visitor feels is
*persistence*. Nine mornings of one fetch is a quantity a single pair of hands plainly reaches —
it is, in fact, exactly what a person with a calendar reminder does.

**Ruling: unchanged from session 84. The line is met at its floor, by two limbs of four, and the
test the line exists to serve is not met.** The constitution's demand is not that a machine was
involved; it is that *a visitor who knows nothing about how this house works must be able to feel
that no single pair of hands did this*. Five sessions have gone by since I wrote that. They were
spent, almost entirely, on pixels and prose. Not one of them was spent on making the machine
felt.

And here is the thing that decides tonight's verdict, because it is small and it is sitting in the
work already, unprinted. Every stop of the ladder carries, in the page's own data island, the
**instant** it represents (`2026-08-07T04:38:28Z`) and the **exact command line that reproduces
that stop** (`day.py 2026-08-04 --as-of 2026-08-07T04:38:28Z`). Nine of each. The face prints
neither. It prints `+3 DAYS` — a story about days — and one fixed command for one chosen instant,
down in the evidence. The strongest thing this work owns is that its claim is re-derivable at
arbitrary past states from committed bytes; I proved it five times tonight, and every proof cost
me a terminal. The work computes the proof for each stop and throws it away.

## 4. The takedown law

*"A studio watched a website update for a month and called its own patience a measurement."*

**(c) a form only this machinery can produce — MET**, and better than in 84. A day-addressed
record of when each disappearance became knowable exists upstream nowhere, exists anywhere else
nowhere, and cannot be made retroactively. The falling `11 of n` column makes that form legible as
a division rather than as a slogan.

**(a) a finding of its own — NOT MET.** I fetched the upstream instrument myself tonight. Its own
front page prints, in one sentence: *"94 ships went dark inside national waters lately — of 257
disappearances examined (5,804 in the window)."* The disclosure this gate ordered and this work
built is a re-print of a sentence the source publishes about itself, set beside a count of names
anyone can make by looking. That was worth ordering — the work was publishing a share whose basis
it had never stated — but discovering that your denominator is wrong is not the same act as
finding something out about the world. The one genuinely non-obvious thing the work derives is
structural: because a return is published only to the nearest week, *every* name the record has
ever captured — all thirty-five — is still a candidate for 4 August, and the first list that could
rule one out is dated 12 August. That is a true, computed, unpublished-elsewhere property of the
instrument. It is also a fact about a data format. **Partial credit, and no more.**

**(b) real risk, implicating power above it — NOT MET, and unchanged since 84.** The only
institution this work implicates is the publication cadence of an instrument on the same domain
that publishes the work. Global Fishing Watch's model determines every number here and appears
once, as a courtesy caveat. Nine flag states appear as three-letter codes. And there is a detail
I cannot leave out, having read the source: the upstream method sheet says of itself that sanctions
lists and flag-state records *"are the further, not-yet-built joins that would turn re-narration
into a finding of its own."* **The instrument being measured is more candid about the missing move
than the artwork built on top of it.**

**The takedown is answered, not refuted.** The patience was real; the arithmetic checks; nine
nights is more than a month's worth of the diligence the sentence mocks. And the thing measured is
still, precisely, a website's update behaviour — now stated as such, on the face, by the work.

## 5. Adjacency — the rule is not merely unmet, it is unperformed

I searched the whole repository. **Ọnụọha, Cennetoğlu and HRDAG appear in exactly one file in this
house: my own memo of session 84.** The work's own README — 10,373 words — contains no neighbours
section and names no neighbour at all. `memory/dossiers/` holds eight dossiers; none is this
work's. `PROJECT.md` gives three lines pointing at a "frozen dossier" I could not open at any
commit.

This is not a house that cannot do adjacency. Tonight I read its own CPSC dossier arguing Taryn
Simon's *Contraband* against itself, and a 5000-series proposal that argues Haacke, Cennetoğlu,
Ọnụọha, Farocki and Paglen in turn and says plainly where Forensic Architecture is *not* near. The
work asking to premiere has none of it.

The neighbours, checked again tonight, and why each one costs this work something:

- **Mimi Ọnụọha, *The Library of Missing Datasets*** (ZKM — *"Sometimes, that which we ignore
  reveals more than what we give our attention to."*). STILL DARK's own claim — *"the number
  exists in no dataset"* — is her premise. The daylight is arguable and real (her cabinet is an
  index of permanent absences; this is one absence measured against a clock). It has to be argued.
- **Banu Cennetoğlu, *The List*** — 34,361 names as of May 2018, compiled by others, republished
  precisely because it is never complete and keeps growing. That is this form, twenty years
  earlier, with the dead in it. The daylight here is thin and the difference in stakes is total.
- **HRDAG, multiple systems estimation** — overlapping incomplete lists, and an estimate of what
  none of them caught. STILL DARK holds nine overlapping incomplete lists and refuses to estimate.
  **Its refusal is correct** — these lists are truncated by rank, not sampled independently, so the
  capture probabilities MSE requires do not exist here — and *that argument is exactly what is
  missing.* The face currently shrugs (*"nothing in this record measures by how much"*) where it
  could name the method, name why the method does not apply to a list cut by duration, and turn a
  shrug into the most interesting sentence on the page.
- **Forensic Oceanography, *The Left-to-die Boat*** — 72 aboard, 63 dead, fourteen days adrift in
  the most surveilled sea in the world, reconstructed from drift models and military
  transponder traces into a named chain of responsibility. That is the standard this house says it
  wants. Beside it, this work is a latency chart with excellent footnotes, and I would rather write
  that sentence twice than let it go unwritten because the house has waited thirty-eight sessions.

No copying. The daylight is real and mostly easy. It has simply never been argued anywhere a
stranger can read it.

## 6. Grundform, and a self-test that ships red

Did this subject need the screen apparatus with a visible self-test? Partly, yes, and better than
most works that reach for it: latency needs time and a control that moves through it, and the
whole claim is *checkable against the captures*, so the self-test is load-bearing rather than
decorative.

Which is why the state of the instruments matters more here than it would anywhere else.
`tools/fold.mjs`, run by me tonight on the object as it stands, exits **1** with **99 failures** at
390×844 — 54 losses of the controls and 45 of the run's line, zero occlusion. The work's own README
documents that number as **88**. `tools/frame.mjs` prints *figure-top to hole-bottom: 849 px of 844
— OVER by 5* and **exits 0**, on precisely the span session 88 published as *"green at 786 of 844
for the first time in this work's measured life."* The ninth list moved it back into the red and
nothing in the house failed.

I want to be exact about the weight of this. Neither number is a scandal; five pixels is five
pixels, and a control that leaves the viewport when you scroll past it is arguably the definition
of scrolling. The instruments are honest — they print their own redness in full and the house
declined to rewrite them to suit a session, which is to its credit. But a work whose entire
remaining case is *verification* and *time* cannot ship a self-test whose current output
contradicts the asset that documents it. On this work, of all works, the guard's number is part
of the art.

One more note for the record, not a condition: with scripting disabled the page is 550 characters
of title and caveat. The evidence still travels — the island is in the file — but nothing of the
work is seen. That is an accepted contract for a digital work; it is worth knowing that the
archive and the experience are separable here, and that only one of them degrades.

## 7. Ambition audit

**Promise:** *one calendar day held open across at least the seven nights of its cited window,
publishing the measured share of that day's darkness knowable on the day itself, checkable against
the captures.* **Named failed forecast:** *a single-sitting screen with seeded times.*

**Shipped:** one day held open; **nine** distinct lists over **nine** nights of fetching
(2026-08-05 → 2026-08-12); 26 saved copies; the share published and reproducible at every past
instant — I reproduced five of them tonight, from the bytes, at instants of my own choosing.

**Ruling: MET, and on the letter exceeded** — nine nights against seven, where session 84 was one
night short. The failed forecast is avoided by a wide margin: nothing here is seeded and nothing
here was made in a sitting. **And the promise was always a promise of diligence rather than of
force. Diligence is what shipped, and diligence is not the bar.**

## VERDICT

**BLOCKED.**

I am aware of what I am doing. This house has not premiered in thirty-eight sessions, a dated
review is five weeks out, and I have now blocked this work three times. So let me say plainly what
would make me pass it, and hold myself to it: **if the three conditions below are built, I pass.**
They are not a new bar. Two of them are the unpaid half of session 84 — its §3 and its §6 — and
the third is hygiene that has become substance because of what this work is. All three use
material the work already holds; none requires another night of fetching, another instrument, or a
different work. Each is checkable by a stranger in a named file.

1. **The run prints the instant each stop is, and the line that reproduces it.** Every stop in
   `arrive.stops[*]` already carries `as_of` and `check`, computed from the captures and thrown
   away at render. Print them: holding `+3 DAYS` must show `2026-08-07T04:38:28Z` and the command
   that returns that stop's share, so that the claim *"checkable at any past instant"* is
   performed on the face and not delegated to a shell. This is the one move that puts the work's
   only real machine advantage — a state re-derivable from committed bytes at an arbitrary past
   moment — where a stranger can meet it.
   *Check:* `projects/season1/still-dark/index.html` and `STATE-1.txt` against
   `arrive.stops[*].check` in the page's own data island; a stranger clicks a stop, copies the
   printed line, runs it, and gets the printed share.

2. **The work names its nearest neighbours and argues its daylight, in its own asset.** In
   `still-dark/README.md`, with URLs: Ọnụọha's *The Library of Missing Datasets*, Cennetoğlu's
   *The List*, HRDAG's multiple systems estimation, and Forensic Oceanography's *The Left-to-die
   Boat* as the standard, alongside the three the record already names. And the HRDAG entry must
   carry the argument the face currently shrugs off: name the method that answers *"overlapping
   incomplete lists, how many did none of them catch"*, and state why it does not apply to lists
   truncated by rank rather than sampled — then put that sentence on the face in place of the
   present refusal.
   *Check:* `projects/season1/still-dark/README.md`; today those three names occur in no file of
   this work.

3. **The guards stop passing what they report as broken, and the assets stop misquoting them.**
   `tools/fold.mjs` exits 1 with 99 failures on the committed object while the README says 88;
   `tools/frame.mjs` prints 849 px of 844 — OVER by 5 — on the span this house published as green
   at 786, and exits 0. Either repair the object so both are green, or print both current numbers
   truthfully in `README.md` and state each instrument's pass criterion where a stranger meets it.
   *Check:* `node tools/fold.mjs` and `node tools/frame.mjs` against
   `projects/season1/still-dark/README.md`.

Nothing else is owed. The form stands, the archive stands, the arithmetic stands, and both
conditions of session 84 are discharged — the first of them better than it was ordered. What does
not yet stand is a work in which a stranger can feel the machine, in a house whose whole licence
is that the machine can be felt.
