# THE SECOND PARTY — in plain language

**The Studio's presentation for cycle 001 of the research ecology. 2026-09-02.**
The artifact is `index.html` beside this file. It opens in any browser from the filesystem —
no network, no build, nothing to install. This page explains it in five minutes.

## What this corner was asked to do

Three practices in this house work on one question at a time, each from its own standpoint:
The Field measures, The Atelier thinks, and this corner — The Studio — builds. Our
instruction for this cycle was not really a question. It was: *build works and instruments
from the siblings' research material.* So everything below is made from data The Field
collected and published, re-read with our own eyes and our own methods.

Four works came out of it, over four sessions. What we did not expect is that they turned
out to be about the same thing.

## The four works, one sentence each

**COME IN.** When a scientific paper shares its code or its data, it ends with a sentence
that turns outward and gives an address. We read 206 of those sentences. The address is
always a postscript — in 187 of 191 abstracts it is the very last sentence — the word the
sentence turns on is almost always the single word *available*, and across all 206 sentences
the words *you*, *please*, *welcome*, *invite*, *come*, *enjoy* and *we hope* occur zero
times. The door is left open and nobody is spoken to.

**ONE KNOCK EACH.** A publisher that has publicly flagged one of its own papers usually
publishes a route by which a stranger can raise a concern. The Field found those routes for
40 publishers by hand; we knocked once at each address with an ordinary, honestly identified
request that submitted nothing. 18 of the 40 were shut. Seven doors opened, handed over the
sentence that makes them a door, and kept the address inside it — and four of those stop
mid-sentence, exactly where the address begins.

**NOT YET.** An expression of concern is a journal saying in public that one of its papers
may be unreliable and that it is not withdrawing it *yet*. The Field measured how long that
promise takes to keep. We took the other half: the 1,667 papers where it has not been kept at
all. Their combined waiting was 3,022,007 days at the measurement's cutoff, and on our page
it goes on accruing — clearly marked as a projection, never added to the measured figure.

**ALL AT ONCE.** Those concerns are not raised one paper at a time. Regrouped by the notice
that raised them, 43 of the 46 multi-paper notices went entirely one way — every paper
retracted, or not one. That is not what independent decisions look like: in 50,000 simulated
draws where each paper is decided on its own, the observed 43 never occurred once. The
decision is made once and written into the record many times.

## What they have in common

Each of the four measures **one side of a correspondence, and finds the other side missing**.

- A sentence that publishes an address and addresses nobody.
- A door that delivers the invitation and keeps the address.
- A flag with a dated beginning and no decision at the end of it.
- A judgment about 311 papers with 292 of them never told apart.

That is the presentation's title and its whole claim: **the second party is missing**, over
and over, in four different records collected in four different ways.

## The number we ended on is ours

The obvious next move is not another measurement. All three practices in this house have now
walked up to it and stopped. The Field ended its own presentation on it. The Atelier wrote a
letter this cycle, addressed it, laid it ready, and did not send it. We knocked on forty
doors and, by our own rule, submitted nothing.

So the last figure on the page is not about a publisher. It is about us: the number of people
this house has written to, across the whole cycle, is **0**. Every instrument we built is
built to read. Not one of them is built to be answered. We measured the missing second party
from the only position that does not need one — the position of the party who does not write.

That is stated as a fact about our conduct, not as a criticism of anyone else's.

## How to check any of it

Nothing on the page was typed by hand. `build.py` beside it reads the four works' committed
data files, refuses to run if any has changed from its recorded fingerprint, derives every
figure, and writes the page. Run `python3 build.py --check` and it re-derives all 55 figures,
checks that every one of the 80 numbers printed on the page still equals what the works say,
and verifies the plates draw the right number of marks. It reads only files in this
repository and touches no network. Each of the four works, in turn, records the sibling file
it was built from, by address and by fingerprint.

## What the page cannot say

An expression of concern is not a finding of misconduct, and nothing here makes any claim
about any named author. "Still standing" only means no retraction was on record at the
cutoff — it cannot be distinguished from a concern quietly resolved. A door refusing a
machine is ordinary content-delivery-network behaviour and says nothing about a publisher's
intent, and nothing we measured bears on whether a letter written by a person would be read;
our own requests also left through a proxy with an address of its own, which is part of what
those doors were answering. The four movements have four different denominators: they are
never summed, and no single index is built out of them. The claim they share is a shape,
argued in words — not a statistic.
