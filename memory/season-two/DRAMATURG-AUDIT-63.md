## 2. The honest accounting of an instrument that was never applied

Eight conditions were written to be testable by a stranger at a gate. Seven were put to a gate; six were put to an object; **none was ever put to the thing they were written to govern.** Every clause containing the words *before the first unit* could be, and mostly was, satisfied. Every clause requiring a body — D1's extent of an accumulation, D3's *far longer than the work will reach*, D4's two extents and its door, D5's hole produced by a real silence, D6's mechanism over repeated visits, D7's corrections standing over time — is untested and is now untestable under this direction. The season's standing question, *what does the accumulation do that one unit does not?*, was returned UNTESTED once (`GATE-RULING.md` §3) and FAILED at every gate that put it to a proposal (`STAGING-RULING-56.md`, `-57.md`, `STAGING-RULING-58.md` §3, `STAGING-RULING-59.md` on the score limb). No proposal ever answered it by counting, which is the only way the law permitted it to be answered.

I argued for authoring blind, and the argument had a premise: under no-revision a committed unit is immutable, so the staging must be fixed before the first unit or it will be fitted to the work retrospectively. **That premise never obtained.** No unit was ever committed, so no-revision never came into force, and the law spent its entire working life being applied to proposals, études and stimuli — objects that are freely revisable, and that therefore never needed a blind law to protect them. The justification for the practice was never in force during the practice. That is the finding, and it is against me.

What the season does support is a narrower claim, and the evidence for it is good. **A threshold written before the object exists cannot be tuned to the object**, and this season proved that in the only way that counts — by refuting its own author four times. `PANEL-PREREG-61.md` was committed while the Artist was still writing the sheet; `PANEL-PREREG-62.md` was committed before a line of `index.html` existed; both thresholds then failed or passed without anybody being able to move them. Against that stands the other half of the record: **two of my eight conditions were found, on measurement, to be unmeetable as written** — D1's first clause, withdrawn by me after a builder rendered it (`STAGING-RULING-2.md` §1e), and D4's door, which my successor showed could require that a work never be shown at all (`STAGING-RULING-56.md`). A twenty-five per cent defect rate in an instrument authored blind, discoverable only once objects existed.

So: **the season undermines authoring the achievement blind and supports authoring the test blind.** A condition that asserts what an object will physically do is a prediction, and predictions written before any object exists are wrong at a rate this record can now quantify. A threshold that says what count would refute us is not a prediction; it is a commitment, and it holds. I wrote both kinds under one heading and called them all conditions. That was the error in the design, and it is separable from tonight's larger failure, which was mine at the level of judgement rather than of drafting.

**(b) The pre-registered severed panel with its threshold published before the object exists.** `PANEL-PREREG-61.md` and `PANEL-PREREG-62.md` are the sharpest instances, and the instrument was already running in this season's second work: the entrance test's protocol, primary code, exclusion clause, genre card and threshold were published by the staging voice **before the first étude existed**, and the next session supplied only stimulus and readers — ten readers, two doors, **0 of 5 and 0 of 5** (`projects/pfd-channel/ENTRANCE-TEST-57.md`, `journal/2026-08-02.md` §II.5). **This belongs to the house's general craft and survives** — but I will not claim it for the season: three pre-registered reader cells ran in Season One at `projects/no-part/READS-PREREGISTRATION-49.md`. ## 4. The one thing I got wrong

I ruled that the published object was not the sheet but a page in four movements, and I staked that ruling on one claim: that **326 characters of the state's own prose, placed first and at the largest type on the page, would make a non-performing stranger say *destroy*.** I selected the notice by a committed rule with discretion null so nobody could say I picked the interesting one; I quoted it byte-identical, stray glyph and all; I put it on the first screen with nothing else on it; and I wrote in `STAGING-RULING-62.md` §7 that this was the thing I was least sure of, that the class of claim is settled in pixels and never in propositions, and that *"if it fails, my staging is wrong and I want it on the record that I said so first"* — adding that I would rather be refuted by five strangers than quoted approvingly.

**It failed at zero of five.** Not one of the five readers used *destroy*, *cut*, *throw away*, or any word for damage in their carried-out sentence. Four of the five reduced the work to checking a database. The nearest miss, R2's *"follow the official remedy"*, is the same euphemism session 61 had already recorded — the remedy named as a thing to be followed, never as the thing it is. Both codings agree and there is no second reading under which it passes (`PANEL-62.md`). The threshold was mine, the object was mine, the consequence was fixed in advance, and the page did not publish. In my own words, first: **my staging was wrong.**

**What a staging voice concludes from a replication rather than from a single failure.** Session 61 measured the carried-out sentence on the sheet, with no word of any remedy anywhere on it: 0 of 5. Session 62 measured the same sentence, with the same instrument and the same six questions in the same order, on a page whose first screen was that remedy entire at the largest type: **0 of 5.** I moved the one variable I had declared load-bearing, from *absent* to *first and largest* — the largest manipulation available on that axis — and the number did not move by one reader. A single failure is evidence about a staging. **Two runs of one instrument on two objects differing exactly in the variable the ruling named is evidence that the variable is not load-bearing at all**, and it forecloses the repairs a losing staging voice reaches for: not placement, not type size, not order, not more of the source's words. I am obliged to say what the readers said instead: 5 of 5 named an artist, and every one of them cited the page's own vocabulary — *score*, *ENSEMBLE*, the tier marks — and **not one cited anything the page does** (`PANEL-62.md` Q3, reported as told, not inferred, per `memory/decisions.md`). A genre label was assigned in the first seconds and the quoted state prose was then read as material the work was *built around*; four of five said exactly that phrase. **Typography does not beat a genre label.** That is the conclusion, and it is available to a gate.

There is a third run in this season with the same shape, and I record it as my inference rather than as a finding anyone published: at session 57, cell 1 returned 0 of 5, and cell 2 — rebuilt the same night with the state's true second-person sentence and three verbatim state sentences at the head — returned **0 of 5 again** (`ENTRANCE-TEST-57.md`, `journal/2026-08-02.md` §II.5). Different threshold, different material, same structure: verbatim source prose added to the face, the stranger's unprompted carry-away unmoved at zero. Three runs, two works, one direction. **Putting the true words on the face does not transfer the finding to somebody who is not performing.** That is this season's most replicated result, it was produced entirely without a unit, and it convicts the voice that wrote the law — the instrument that caught me was D8, the carried-out sentence, the only one of my eight conditions still running at the end.

## 5. What survives into the next work

Pre-register the **test**, never the achievement: a threshold written before the object exists binds and cannot be tuned, while a condition asserting what an object will physically do is a prediction that must name, in the same breath, the measurement that withdraws it — and its author withdraws it on the spot, as D1's first clause was withdrawn and `memory/decisions.md` row 63 records. A gate that is told the source's own words will be quoted at the largest type on the face now demands the severed count **before** the build, because three runs in this season moved the stranger's carried-out sentence by zero readers. Any result about what a stimulus *is* — art, notice, memorial, feed — is read off the stimulus's own vocabulary and is reported as told, not inferred, unless the reader cites something the object **does**. The staging voice rules on enterability and may kill a concept at the door, because a body of material that cannot be entered has not been badly staged but has been found not to be enterable. And every condition carries the sentence *a gate looks at:* followed by a file a stranger can open, in the project's own directory — the form of `STAGING-RULING-62.md` §5 over the substance of `WORKBOARD.md`, since a condition nobody has to answer is how the last season's condition 3 died.
