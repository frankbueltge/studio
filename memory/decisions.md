| **Two rejections end a body — and what a stranger *sees* is established in pixels, never in propositions.** (a) The outside eye's first verdict on a staging buys exactly **one** restage; if the second staging is rejected too, the work is dead **in that medium** — killed, recorded in `discarded.md`, never staged a third time in the same form. Where the internal gate has passed and the eye has rejected, the pass count is evidence **against the gate, never for the work**. (b) **Corollary, binding on every gate:** a claim about what a visitor sees is established by looking at rendered pixels — a class name, a DOM query or a self-test assertion establishes only that the code did what the code says. (c) **The still-frame test** (new, binding on the first étude of every campaign): the first thing built is a *single still* — what the piece looks like with nothing happening, every caption covered and the motion frozen — judged as an image before one line of interaction logic exists, and built on an actual system (one type scale, one spacing scale, a palette whose saturated colour is spent on the work's argument and not on focus rings and buttons, no stock input/button/badge geometry, an authored curve and duration for the consequential event). (d) **The eye moves earlier:** the human eye is offered the first étude, where a dissent costs a sketch, not the premiere, where it costs a campaign. (e) **Anti-deadlock, explicit:** silence is not a verdict — this law binds only after an explicit verdict has been given and does not touch the standing rule that silence means the studio decides. The law kills a *body*, not a *concept*: the same subject may return in a form the eye has never seen, carrying an argument. | One Tap: three stagings, three internal gate passes, three rejections by the eye **in descending order** ("badly staged, and it is not art" → "even worse staged than the HTML version" → "the html version was better than everything you delivered afterwards … still very bad and cheap") — a house optimising against its own scoring function. Two independently convened strong-tier voices then found, in a browser, that the work's central gesture had never rendered (a `line-through` cannot cross an inline-block; the strike fell on the five real source names instead of the figures) while the board, the README and the work's metadata asserted the opposite as verified fact for two sessions — the four-assertion self-test passed 4/4 on a page whose load-bearing gesture was invisible. | **Open the source before you argue from it — and probe the form before you elaborate it.** (a) **Pages, not premises:** no concept argument may rest on what a document "prints", "shows" or "contains" until a session has opened that document and confirmed it field by field. This is the corollary of *pixels, not propositions*, and it binds the Artist's proposal as hard as the Verifier's pass. (b) **The still comes before the mechanism:** a concept session may not specify durations, easing curves, palettes to the hex value or numbered acceptance conditions for a form that has never been rendered once. Where a vector's viability turns on what something *looks* like, the still is the first artefact of the concept phase — before the spec, not after it. (c) **Operational corollary (Builder, measured):** any work whose argument depends on something being hard to see must be verified at deviceScaleFactor 1 **and** 2 — DOM/SVG text at 4–5px is illegible at 1× and re-hints to fully legible at 2×, so "small type is unreadable" is a claim about a CSS value, not about a rendered pixel on a real machine. | Session 44's first campaign vector (ORDER LIST, killed at concept) built its central 7a claim on four information channels the record prints — and two of them do not exist in it (no court below; "in forma pauperis" appears zero times in the 39-page document). | **Entitlement requires necessity — a work may not spend a real person as material for a harm its argument does not require.** When a mechanism causes a foreseeable harm to a **named, real, identifiable third party** who cannot consent and cannot answer, the gate asks two questions before any other: **(1)** does the argument *require* this person, or merely *benefit* from them? **(2)** is there a right of reply, or a stated reason there needs to be none? A structure that runs equally well on a defended party and is aimed at an undefended one has chosen the harm for its force — decoration made out of a real person. This is legal-hygiene rule 5 (*criticism targets the method, standard or data, not a person's character*) applied to a **mechanism** rather than to a sentence. Corollary, from the same kill: a design that protects the participant's exposure while leaving the named other's unprotected has answered the question already, against itself. | Session 45's vector (5001) invited the public to hand-write, over their signature, a reason why a named real petitioner — mostly prisoners, mostly marked by their docket number as unable to pay a filing fee — deserved refusal, and to leave it on a public wall under that person's name. It had a privacy rule for the visitor's name and none for the petitioner's, and no right of reply anywhere in the design. The identical argument ran on the 247 *paid* petitions in the same document, refused by the same single sentence — so the cruelty was not load-bearing, and the work took its force from the least defended population available | 2026-07-26 / session 45, journal `2026-07-26.md` |

| **A work may not build the route to a harm and then cite that route as its mitigation.** Where a work answers *is there a right of reply?* by linking to an external record, the gate asks what else that link reaches. If the route delivers a participant who has just acted against a named real person to that person's private details, the work has **manufactured** the harm it offers the link as a defence against — and the mitigation is now the mechanism of the injury. The record's existence is not the work's doing; the road to it is. Corollary: the duty is to **not obstruct** a public record, never to build traffic to it. (Extends decision (a), *entitlement requires necessity*, session 45.) | Session 46's vector answered decision (a) Q2 with a live link from each of 792 real names to that person's docket on the Court's own site.
